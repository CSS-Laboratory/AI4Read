## Findings

### CRITICAL

**C1 · Rung 4 · Bengio's Brown-corpus perplexities**
- Post: "In Bengio's experiments on the Brown corpus, the neural model reached perplexity 252 while the best counting model reached 321 <cite>pp. 1149–1150</cite>."
- Source (Bengio 2003, Table 1, p. 1149): the best n-gram is the class-based back-off model with 500 classes, at **test perplexity 312**. 321 is the best *word-level* Kneser-Ney model. The caption says: "The difference is 24% in the case of the best n-gram (a class-based model with 500 word classes)." 312/252 = 1.238, which is the 24% quoted in Step 3. 321/252 = 1.274 does not match it. Also, 252 is MLP10, whose outputs are **mixed with an interpolated trigram** ("mix: yes"). The whole table is on p. 1149.
- Fix: "In Bengio's experiments on the Brown corpus, the neural model (averaged with a trigram model) reached a test perplexity of 252, while the best counting model, a class-based n-gram, reached 312 <cite>p. 1149</cite>."

**M3 · Rung 9 · reason for the breakdown misattributed**
- Post: "A power law cannot fall forever if language has non-zero entropy; that is why Kaplan expects the trend to break near \(L^*\sim1.7\) nats per token <cite>pp. 17–18</cite>."
- Source: Kaplan derives L* from the point where \(L(D(C_{min}))\) and \(L(C_{min})\) intersect, which is "a contradiction" (p. 17). The entropy reading is an optional conjecture (p. 18: "In this scenario, we would expect the loss trend to level off at or before L*"). The general point that performance "must flatten out eventually before reaching zero loss" is on p. 3.
- Fix: "A power law cannot fall forever: the loss must flatten out somewhere above zero <cite>p. 3</cite>. Kaplan locates a likely breakdown at or before \(L^*\sim1.7\) nats per token, where two of his trends contradict each other, and conjectures that \(L^*\) might roughly estimate the entropy of language <cite>pp. 17–18</cite>."
