From B2 to C1: how native speakers talk about evidence

B2 English says what is true. C1 English says how sure you are, how much it matters, and where the argument turns. These expressions are common in seminars and reviews, and each example uses an idea from this post.

A paragraph to loop

Expressions stick when you hear them inside real sentences many times. This short talk uses eleven of the expressions above to sum up the whole post. Loop it until you can say it with the audio, then without it.

What Shannon showed was that predicting text and compressing it are the same problem. On the face of it, then, a model that predicts well must know a lot. But what does it actually know? Granted, the loss tells us how many bits the model saves. But it cannot tell us what those bits are about. Xie’s account suggests that a prompt selects a hidden concept, but only if the model fits its training data exactly, and that’s a big if. Attention maps are tempting, but we shouldn’t read too much into them. The theory-of-mind results should give us pause: a drop from almost perfect to zero after one small change is a far cry from robust understanding. Then again, the argument cuts both ways, because people fail some of those changes too. In the end, the debate boils down to what we mean by meaning. Piantadosi and Hill stop short of saying that models understand language as we do, and for now, the jury is still out.

Four sentence patterns that sound C1
Hedging ladder
shows → indicates → suggests → is consistent with → hints at. “Xie’s results are consistent with concept selection; they do not show that GPT-3 computes a posterior.”
Concession, then your point
“Granted, the loss falls smoothly. But that tells us little about when a skill appears.” / “While it is true that … , …”
Cleft sentence for focus
“What Shannon showed was that prediction and compression are the same problem.” / “It is the output layer, not the hidden layer, that dominates Bengio’s cost.”
Inversion for emphasis (writing, formal talks)
“Not only does the model fail, but it fails with 97% confidence.” / “Only when the output is made cheap does the hidden layer become the bottleneck.”
Small fixes that make a big difference
research (uncountable)
“do research” or “conduct a study”; not “a research” or “researches”.
discuss / explain
“discuss the result” (no “about”); “explain it to me” (not “explain me”).
in my view
Use “in my view” or “as I see it”, not “according to my opinion”.
a / the
“the entropy of English” (one specific quantity), “a model of text” (one of many), “Ø language models” (in general).
