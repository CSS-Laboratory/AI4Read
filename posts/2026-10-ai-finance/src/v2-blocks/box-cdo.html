Look inside: four loans, two tranches

Instance. A pool has 4 loans worth 10 each; each defaults with chance 10% and loses its 10. The junior tranche (20) takes the first losses. The senior tranche (20) loses only when a third loan defaults. Question. How safe is the senior tranche?

Mechanism. If loans default independently, three or four defaults together are rare. If all four depend on one hidden cause, such as falling house prices, they default together. Worked numbers (each loan defaults with chance 10%):

casechance the senior tranche loses
independent: exactly 3 defaults\(4\times0.1^3\times0.9=0.0036\)
independent: all 4 default\(0.1^4=0.0001\); total 0.37%
one common cause decides all four10%: 27 times more

Formula (default chance \(p\), common-risk share \(w\), \(q\) the independent chance):

\[\begin{aligned}P_{\text{senior loses}}&=w\,p+(1-w)\,q\\q&=4p^3(1-p)+p^4\end{aligned}\]

Check. At \(p=0.1\): \(w=0\) gives 0.370%, \(w=0.5\) gives 5.185%, \(w=1\) gives 10.000%.

Press “safe loans, p = 0.02”: 2.000% with one common factor, 0.0032% if independent, 634.5 times more.
