Look inside: a contract built to resist tampering

What would help? In their simple model the authors propose a “tree of majorities”: take the majority of 3 inputs, then the majority of 3 such majorities, and so on to depth \(k\), over \(3^k\) assets. Their Theorem 9: fixing any \(t\) inputs to 0 raises the chance that the tree outputs 0 to at most \((1+t/2^k)/2\) Arora et al. 2010, p. 61. A seller must plant junk in a number of inputs comparable to \(2^k\) to move the price much. Their Theorem 10 adds that such manipulation is detectable afterwards when the number of inputs is large enough (much larger than \(\log^3\) of the number of products) and the buyer can see all the products, a transparency that “currently does not exist” pp. 61–62. Toy (ours, code-checked): to force the output, 9 inputs need 4 junk; 27 need 8; 81 need 16.
