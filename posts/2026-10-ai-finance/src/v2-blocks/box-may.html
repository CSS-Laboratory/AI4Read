Look inside: May’s ecology result (an analogy only)

Instance. A food web of many species, each stable on its own, linked at random: some species help others, some harm them. Ecologists once thought more links meant more stability. Model ecosystems showed “a sharp transition from overall stability to instability” as the links grew in number and strength Haldane and May 2011, p. 351. Formula. For many species, “this transition occurs once mα² > 1, where m is the average number of links per species, and (±) α their average strength” p. 351.

Worked numbers. \(m=4\), \(\alpha=0.4\): \(4\times0.16=0.64\), stable. Double the links, \(m=8\): 1.28, unstable. Weaken them to \(\alpha=0.3\): \(8\times0.09=0.72\), stable again.

What it means. It is an ecology result, and Haldane and May do not apply the formula to banks; in their bank model more links can soften loan losses p. 353. What carries over is the question it prompted: which structures let a complex system survive? Their answer for banks includes diversity and modularity: “Modular configurations prevent contagion infecting the whole network in the event of nodal failure” p. 355.
