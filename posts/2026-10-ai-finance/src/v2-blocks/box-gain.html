Look inside: the trend-follower rule, and why it tips at \(m=2/3\)

The rule, with \(A=1.5\) (chase gain) and \(G=0.5\) (pull-back gain):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{gap}_{t+1}&=\text{gap}_t+A\,m\,(\text{gap}_t-\text{gap}_{t-1})\\&\quad-G\,(1-m)\,\text{gap}_t\end{aligned}\]

First step at \(m=0.2\), from a gap of 0 then −1: \(-1+0.3\times(-1)-0.4\times(-1)=-0.9\). Check. At \(m=0.2\) the toy reports a largest swing of 0.04 up and −1.00 down, and a gap of 0.000 after 40 steps.

A rough reason. Each step the chasers repeat \(Am\) times the last move, so a swing keeps its size when \(Am=1\), at \(m=1/A=2/3\).

The exact argument. Write the rule as \(g_{t+1}=b\,g_t-c\,g_{t-1}\) with \(b=1+Am-G(1-m)\) and \(c=Am\). A pattern \(g_t=x^t\) fits when \(x^2-bx+c=0\): the characteristic equation, whose roots are the factors by which the pattern is multiplied each step. The two roots multiply to \(c\). When they are complex numbers (a wave, which needs \(b^2\lt 4c\)), each has size \(\sqrt c\), so the wave shrinks if \(Am\lt 1\) and grows if \(Am\gt 1\). Numbers (code-checked with numpy.roots). \(m=0.2\): \(b=0.9\), \(c=0.3\), size 0.548. \(m=0.67\): \(c=1.005\), size 1.002, just above 1, so the swing grows very slowly. \(m=0.9\): \(b=2.3\), \(c=1.35\), size 1.162. For very small \(m\) (below about 0.07) the roots are real and both below 1. Near \(m=1\) the roots become real again, but by then \(Am\gt 1\) and the larger root already exceeds 1.
