Layers of certainty, and critics

Four layers, from most certain to most contested

  1. Running is automated in parts of finance. Measured: about 70% of US equity trading is algorithmic; the 2010 index future traded on a fully electronic market.
  2. Checking needs variety, and some products resist checking. Mathematics (Ashby), and a theorem resting on a conjecture in a simple model (Arora and colleagues).
  3. Shared models make failures coincide. Shown in toys; flagged as a risk (“could”) by the FSB and as a hypothesis by Gensler and Bailey; judged “contained” for now by the IMF. Not measured for AI.
  4. Whether machines can declare and answer. Contested: collective acceptance, liability, personhood.

Critics, one line each

Poole answers Harari’s claim that chatbots create new ideas: “no one has yet seen a chatbot create new ideas” Poole 2024. Narayanan and Kapoor: in the world they describe (advanced but not “superintelligent” AI, a notion they view as incoherent), impacts are slow and control stays with people; they call it their “median outcome”, not a certainty Narayanan and Kapoor 2025. FSB: existing frameworks address many vulnerabilities, but “more work may be needed” FSB 2024, p. 2. Skingsley, Harari’s interviewer at the BIS: we already trust what we only vaguely understand BIS 2024 [7:18]. Harari’s own remedy: institutions and “humans in the loop”, offered with his doubt: “Unfortunately, humanity has never been united” Guardian 2024.
