Layers, conditionals, a tension and critics

Four layers, from most certain to most contested

  1. Running is automated in parts of finance. Measured: about 70% of US equity trading is algorithmic; the 2010 index future traded on a fully electronic market.
  2. Checking needs variety, and some products resist checking. Mathematics (Ashby), and a theorem resting on a conjecture in a simple model (Arora and colleagues).
  3. Shared models make failures coincide. Shown in toys; flagged as a risk (“could”) by the FSB and as a hypothesis by Gensler and Bailey; judged “contained” for now by the IMF. Not measured for AI.
  4. Whether machines can declare and answer. Contested: collective acceptance, liability, personhood.

A tension between the lenses (ours)

Ashby’s third lever says: impose a constraint, such as a standard menu of contracts, and checking becomes possible. Haldane and May say: when everyone holds the same thing, the system grows fragile. A standard menu makes checking easier and firms more alike. Which effect wins depends on what is standardised: the contract form, or the bets made with it.

Five conditionals

  • If many firms automate deciding with one model, their errors coincide and diversification shrinks (\(N_{\text{eff}}\); FSB; Gensler and Bailey).
  • If supervisors can neither raise their variety nor constrain the menu, outcomes stay varied (Ashby).
  • If the seller knows where the junk is and buyers have bounded computing power, pricing can become infeasible (Arora and colleagues; conjectured assumption, simple model).
  • If the principal can check the agent, or the agent has reason to be honest, the worst case is the status quo; if neither holds, delegation fails (Lupia and McCubbins).
  • If a community accepts a type of token as money, it counts as money in that community (Searle); whether AIs could be that community is open.

Critics, one line each

Poole answers Harari’s claim that chatbots create new ideas: “no one has yet seen a chatbot create new ideas”. Narayanan and Kapoor: in the world they describe (advanced but not “superintelligent” AI, a notion they view as incoherent), impacts are slow and control stays with people; they call it their “median outcome”, not a certainty. FSB: existing frameworks address many vulnerabilities, but “more work may be needed”. Skingsley, Harari’s interviewer at the BIS: we already trust what we only vaguely understand. Harari’s own remedy: institutions and “humans in the loop”, offered with his doubt: “Unfortunately, humanity has never been united” Guardian 2024.
