Look inside: Ashby’s law with probabilities

Counting states treats every disturbance as equally likely. With probabilities, entropy replaces variety: \(H(D)=-\sum_d P(d)\log_2 P(d)\), the average number of yes-or-no questions needed to name the disturbance. For equally likely states it equals the variety. The law becomes

\[H(E)\;\ge\;H(D)+H(R\mid D)-H(R)\]

\(H(R\mid D)\) is the uncertainty left in the regulator’s choice once the disturbance is known; it is 0 if R is a function of D. Two steps. (1) The table rule, no outcome twice in a column Ashby 1956, p. 207, means that given R, different disturbances give different outcomes, so \(H(D\mid R)\le H(E\mid R)\). (2) Then \[\begin{aligned}&H(D)+H(R\mid D)\\&=H(R)+H(D\mid R)\\&\le H(R)+H(E\mid R)\\&\le H(R)+H(E)\end{aligned}\] where the last step uses the fact that, on average, conditioning reduces entropy (\(H(E\mid R)\le H(E)\)) Cover and Thomas 1991. Rearrange. Numbers (toy, ours). Four kinds of contract arrive with chances \(\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac18,\tfrac18\): \(H(D)=\tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac14\cdot2+\tfrac18\cdot3+\tfrac18\cdot3=1.75\) bits. A one-bit regulator that is a function of D gives \(H(E)\ge1.75-1=0.75\) bits.

Trap · a misprint in the reprint

Ashby writes the conditional term as \(H_D(R)\), i.e. \(H(R\mid D)\) Ashby 1956, p. 208. One line of the reprint prints \(H_D(E)\), though the line above and the sentence after need \(H_D(R)\). We use the corrected form.
