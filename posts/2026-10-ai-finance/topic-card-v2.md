# Post 02, v2: Could a society run itself on AI?

## 1. The question
- **For a high-school reader.** If machines ran the markets, the courts and the money, and no person ever stepped in, would it still work? And would it still be a *society*?
- **Research version.** Which jobs of a social system can AI do with no human intervention? Under which conditions does removing people from each job break three things:
  - **stability**: correlated failures and cascades;
  - **control**: a regulator needs as much variety as what it regulates;
  - **meaning**: institutions exist because people accept them, and accountability needs someone who answers?
- **Harari is the hint, not the topic.** In one paragraph:
  - he asserts that "anything made of words will be taken over by AI";
  - he imagines AI-made financial devices nobody understands;
  - in *Nexus* he describes "computer-to-computer chains" and "inter-computer realities" that run without humans.

  His full lines go in a fold-out.

## 2. The framework: five jobs (ours)
A social system does five jobs. "Fully automated" means AI does all five with no human in the loop.

| Job | Instance: a stock market | Automated today? (to be sourced) |
|---|---|---|
| **Run**: carry out the work | matching buy and sell orders | largely, through electronic trading (Kirilenko et al. 2017; IMF 2024) |
| **Decide**: choose what to do | which trades to make | partly: algorithmic and AI trading (IMF 2024; FSB 2024) |
| **Check**: verify and regulate | supervisors, circuit breakers | a little: proposals for AI supervisors (Danielsson & Uthemann) |
| **Declare**: say what counts (money, law, a person) | what counts as a valid trade, as money, as a legal person | no: people's collective acceptance (Searle 1995) |
| **Answer**: bear losses and be held responsible | who pays, who is blamed | no: people and firms (Lupia & McCubbins) |

The post asks, job by job: can it be automated, what happens if it is (impact), and what it would mean (philosophy).

## 3. Lenses (4)
1. **Control and cybernetics: possibility of the *check* job.**
   - Ashby's law of requisite variety. Humans cannot match AI-speed, AI-variety disturbances, so checking gets automated too, which raises "who checks the checker?".
   - Ashby's three levers.
   - Arora et al.: some products cannot be checked by any realistic checker.
   - Toys: the Ashby table and the junk-placement toy.
2. **Complex systems and decision theory: impact of automating *run* and *decide*.**
   - Shared models make failures arrive together.
   - Tools: the effective number of independent opinions; the six-bank cascade; the herding feedback toy.
   - Haldane & May: complexity and homogeneity versus stability, by analogy with ecology. May's result is that a random web becomes unstable once m·a² > 1, where m is the average number of links per species and a their average strength. This is a new lab widget.
   - Gensler & Bailey on model monoculture, now read in the paper itself.
3. **Philosophy of social reality: the meaning of automating *declare*.**
   - Searle: status functions ("X counts as Y in C") exist only through collective acceptance by minds.
   - Harari: computers create "inter-computer realities", such as Pokémon, Google rank and possibly new financial devices.
   - Dennett: the intentional stance.
   - The crux: can machines be members of the "we" that makes money, law and persons real?
4. **Political science: the meaning of automating *answer*.**
   - Delegation and accountability (Lupia & McCubbins: knowledge or incentive).
   - Harari on democracy and unfathomable algorithms (*Nexus* ch. 9).
   - Legal personhood for AI (Harari, WEF 2026).
   - What it means for democracy when no human can explain a decision.

## 4. Hook and guesses
- **Hook (real instance).** 6 May 2010: for about 36 minutes, computer programs ran a market into a crash and back (Kirilenko et al. 2017). People had built a pause into the market's own rules, and that pause helped stop the fall. Then Harari's hint: what if nobody had built it, and nobody could? A society where no human steps in.
- **Guess first:**
  1. Which of the five jobs is most automated today?
  2. Ten banks, one model: how often are all ten wrong together?
  3. Can a token become money with no human accepting it?
  4. Is Harari's ten-year scenario a forecast?

## 5. Short-answer thesis (provisional, ours; the post must earn it)
- **Run and decide** can be automated, and in markets largely are. The cost is correlated failure.
- **Check** can be partly automated, but each automated checker needs a checker, and checkers that share models share blind spots.
- **Declare and answer** cannot be handed over without changing what a society *is*. They need a "we" that accepts, and someone who bears the loss.
- So a "fully automated society" would either keep humans quietly in those two jobs, or become a different kind of thing: machines that treat each other as persons. Whether that is still a society is the philosophical question Harari's personhood debate is really about.
