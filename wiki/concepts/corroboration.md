# corroboration

## Canon spine entry

- **corroboration** (analytic-philosophy): Popper's term for a theory's record of surviving severe tests, which he distinguishes from probability (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch04-popper]] (p. 68): Popper's measure of survival of falsification attempts; here read as purely backward-looking, like a transcript
- [[popper-1959-logic/appii-untitled]] (p. 319): here only mentioned: Popper wants to show it is not a probability
- [[popper-1959-logic/appiv-untitled-2]] (p. 335): Popper says the theory of corroboration needs second arguments of zero absolute probability (p. 335); the unit does not define corroboration.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appix-untitled]] (p. 402): degree to which a theory has passed severe tests; here measured by $C$, not a probability
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvi-untitled]] (p. 303): here: a theory of indeterminacy could be corroborated only statistically
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvii-untitled-2]] (p. 374): degree to which a theory has survived severe tests; Popper says it is not a probability
- [[popper-1959-logic/appviii-untitled]] (p. 398): record of severe tests; it can rise while probability stays zero; distinct from probability
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch01-survey]] (p. 10): the status of a theory that has withstood severe tests; only temporary support
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch04-falsifiability]] (p. 67): here: a hypothesis passing tests against accepted basic statements
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch05-problem]] (pp. 82, 86): outcome of a test that a theory survives; it too depends on accepted basic statements
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p1]] (p. 133): the corroboration of hypotheses is deferred to sections 79–85
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p3]] (p. note *4, p. 194): high negative corroboration of a large sample may be read as falsification
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch09-observations]] (p. 244): even if the experiment of section 77 did not refute them, the uncertainty relations could only be tested and corroborated as frequency statements
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch10-corroboration]] (pp. 248–249, 264, 275): a timeless logical report of how a theory has stood up to severe tests relative to accepted basic statements; Popper's neutral replacement for "confirmation"

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch02-science]] (pp. 26, 30): a theory's survival of attempts to falsify it. It is not proof, and it depends more on severity than number of tests [new]
