## Definitions in the sources

### economics-finance

- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch04-value]] (pdf 44–45): loss aversion: losses hurt about twice as much as equal gains; explains the endowment effect [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch07-bargains]] (p. 25): loss aversion is used to argue that routine payments are not felt as losses [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch09-buckets]] (p. 42): here only mental accounting: money is sorted into budgets or accounts with different spendability [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch11-willpower]] (pp. 64–65): wealth is not fungible across accounts [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch14-seems]] (p. 92): fairness norms follow from the endowment effect: usual terms of trade become the reference point, and deterioration is a loss [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch17-debate]] (p. 124): mental accounting: labeling money (e.g., "income") makes spending feel different; Thaler also mentions loss aversion in connection with dividend cuts [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch20-narrow]] (pp. 144, 149): loss aversion (a loss hurts more than an equal gain pleases) drives timid choices and Brown's refusal [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch31-save]] (pp. 264, 267): here, loss aversion is the dislike of paycheck cuts, measured in nominal dollars; the "retirement" mental account is also less tempting to dip into [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/conclusion-next]] (pp. 302, 305): money is not treated as the same everywhere; losses loom larger than gains [as `mental accounting, endowment effect, loss aversion`]
