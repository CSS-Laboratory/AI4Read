# conditions-for-persuasion

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch04-people]] (pp. 68–69): persuasion requires that the listener perceive the speaker as knowledgeable and trustworthy; the basis for trust can come from external forces or perceived common interests
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch07-laboratory-p1]] (pp. 102, 108–109): in the theory, persuasion is determined by external forces and perceived speaker attributes; perceived speaker knowledge is a necessary condition. Here these are set by design in the lab
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch07-laboratory-p2]] (pp. 120, 130): the authors expect more persuasion as a speaker is believed knowledgeable and to share interests (pp. 115, 124), or as external forces (a larger lying penalty, verification, costly effort) are added for a speaker believed knowledgeable (pp. 133–139); perceived, not actual, knowledge and interests are expected to be necessary
