# analysis

## Canon spine entry

- **analysis** (analytic-philosophy): explaining a puzzling concept or sentence by breaking it into clearer parts or restating it in clearer form (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch08-response]] (p. 88): philosophical analysis of a tradition's assumptions can weaken its grip
- [[lycan-2019-language/ch07-psychological]] (p. 92): here, an analysis of "By uttering x, S meant that P" in terms of intentions
- [[magnus-2025-forallx/ch24-sentences]] (p. 205): here, stepwise paraphrase of an English sentence toward something directly symbolizable in FOL
- [[magnus-2025-forallx/ch28-definite]] (p. 244): Russell's analysis restates descriptions without using *the*
- [[popper-1959-logic/front-preface-2]] (p. xix–xx): language analysts' method of clarifying puzzling expressions by breaking them into clearer parts; Popper grants it a role but denies it defines philosophy
- [[russell-1905-denoting/main]]: rewriting propositions containing denoting phrases into forms with none (para. 5, 12)
- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch03-proposition]] (p. 3.201, 3.25, pp. 32–33): one and only one complete analysis of a proposition, ending in simple signs
- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch04-proposition]] (§4.221, p. 49): resolving propositions down to elementary propositions of names
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch01-augustine]] (p. 33e): replacing an ordinary sentence by a "further analysed" one; Wittgenstein asks whether the first conceals the second
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch02-family]] (§§90–91, p. 47e): substituting forms of expression to clear misunderstandings; the idea of a "final analysis" is described as something it may come to look as if there were
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch05-thinking]] (§383, p. 125e): Wittgenstein says we analyse concepts (the application of words), not phenomena
