# framing-effect

## Canon spine entry

- **framing effect** (decision-game-theory): logically equivalent descriptions produce different choices (HS)

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[kahneman-1979-prospect/main]] (pp. 271–273): equivalent problems get different choices (two-stage game; bonus problems); the authors also conjecture contingent insurance is more attractive than probabilistic insurance [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/appb-choices]] (pdf 435): equivalent descriptions give different preferences [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch00-introduction]] (pdf 17): decisions shaped by inconsequential features of how a problem is posed [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch07-machine]] (pdf 87): different ways of presenting the same information (e.g. 90% survival versus 10% mortality) often evoke different emotions [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch25-bernoulli]] (p. only mentioned, pdf 263): large changes of preference that are sometimes caused by inconsequential wording changes [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch28-bad]] (pdf 296): not named here; the status quo as reference shapes how a change is judged
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch30-rare]] (pdf 321–322): the same risk described as a percentage or as "x in n" gives different weights and choices [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch31-risk]] (pdf 327): the same choice problem can yield different, even inconsistent, preferences depending on how it is split or bundled
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch32-keeping]] (pdf 336): framing the sale as pleasure versus pain shapes the choice
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch33-reversals]] (pdf 351–352): here, narrow single versus broad joint frames change judgments [as `framing effect`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch34-frames]] (pdf 354): unjustified influence of formulation on beliefs and preferences
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/conclusions]] (pdf 405): presentation as loss or gain changes impact

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 31): expressed preferences shift with wording or framing, e.g. "welfare" vs "assistance to the poor" [as `framing effect`]
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch03-tumbling]] (pp. 75–76): outcomes may depend on question wording (Houston affirmative action)
- [[chong-2007-framing/main]] (pp. 104–105, 115): change in opinion caused by changing the emphasis or presentation of an issue; defined here via changed weights $w_i$
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch02-information]] (pp. 7–8): Lippmann's stereotypes, culturally given or elite-supplied, that determine what the public thinks an issue is about [as `framing effect`]
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch05-making]] (pp. 81–83): question frames raise some considerations, and can also inform the unaware

### economics-finance

- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch17-debate]] (p. 119): logically equivalent options chosen differently depending on wording [as `framing effect`; new]
