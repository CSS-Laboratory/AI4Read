# universality

## Canon spine entry

- **universality** (complex-systems): different microscopic systems share the same macro behavior near critical points (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### complex-systems

- [[bak-1987-soc/main]] (p. 384): whether the exponents depend on physical details is left open
- [[castellano-2009-socialdynamics/main-p1]] (p. 3): in most situations large-scale behavior depends only on symmetries, dimension, conservation laws, not microscopic details
- [[castellano-2009-socialdynamics/main-p2]] (pp. 23, 37): shared exponents or scaling functions across systems (e.g. $vQ/N$ scaling, $h\sim w^{-2/3}$, SPP universality class)
- [[castellano-2009-socialdynamics/main-p3]] (pp. 47, 49): invoked as an advantage of simple models; finite-size exponents independent of $\langle k\rangle,\gamma$
- [[mitchell-2009-guidedtour/ch02-dynamics]] (pdf 59): here, the same for all unimodal maps (Feigenbaum's constant)
- [[strogatz-2024-nonlinear/ch01-overview]] (p. 4): Feigenbaum: roughly, completely different systems can go chaotic in the same way
- [[strogatz-2024-nonlinear/ch10-one-p2]] (pp. 377, 380–381): in this unit: the order of cycles, δ and α are the same for all unimodal maps (with quadratic maximum for the constants); the author draws an analogy with phase-transition exponents
- [[strogatz-2024-nonlinear/ch11-fractals]] (pp. 422–423): at the onset of chaos, multifractals make the universal quantity a function $f(\alpha)$
- [[strogatz-2024-nonlinear/ch12-strange]] (p. 445): the 1D unimodal-map predictions (U-sequence) hold for the BZ data
