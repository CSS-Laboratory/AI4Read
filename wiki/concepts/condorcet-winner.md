# condorcet-winner

## Canon spine entry

- **Condorcet winner** (political-science): an option that beats every other option in pairwise majority votes (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 26): a position preferred by a majority to every alternative. In general it does not exist in several dimensions [as `Condorcet winner`]
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p1]] (p. 419): an option that beats all other options in pairwise contests
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch03-getting]] (p. 43): an alternative that beats all others pairwise. The unit calls it the round-robin winner and does not use the term
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch04-group]] (p. 57, fn. 4): alternative that beats every other in pairwise majority contests
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p1]] (p. 109): a point preferred by a majority to every alternative; McKelvey says there is none outside rare cases [as `Condorcet winner`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch07-voting]] (p. 194): an alternative that wins a majority against every other in pairwise contests. Here also a "procedure" that has no winner if none exists

### decision-game-theory

- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch12-voting]] (p. 336): a candidate who beats every other candidate in head-to-head votes [new]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch03-nash-p1]] (p. 74): a position that a majority of voters prefer to every other position [new]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch08-coalitional]] (p. 257): policy preferred by a majority to every alternative; with strict preferences the core of majority voting is the Condorcet winner if one exists [new]
