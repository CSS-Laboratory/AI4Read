# public-sphere

## Canon spine entry

- **Public sphere** (communication-theory): the space where private persons reason together about common affairs, open in principle to all (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch03-reconceiving]] (pp. 58, 62): Carey says Lippmann contributed to its depoliticization; "the public" is for Dewey the instrument of democratic order
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch06-space]] (p. 127): the space of discussion and argument that Innis said democracy presupposes; eclipsed by privatized reading and audiences
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch07-history]] (p. 154): used in the last paragraph, with Dewey's "conjoint life of the polity" (p. 154); the authors do not define it further
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch00-introduction]] (pdf 10): a sphere between civil society and state where critical public discussion of general interest was institutionally guaranteed
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch01-introduction]] (pp. 3, 7): here a historical category. In its bourgeois form it is a forum where private people, as a public, were readying themselves to compel public authority to legitimate itself before public opinion (pp. 25–26). Habermas also describes a Greek model and feudal representative publicness, which differ from it
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch02-social]] (p. 27): here, the bourgeois public sphere: private people come together as a public and use their reason against public authority in a debate over the rules of commodity exchange and social labor
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch03-political]] (pp. 57, 74): a sphere of private people arguing about public affairs; here it acquires political functions and becomes an organ of the constitutional state
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch04-bourgeois-p1]] (p. 89, p. 104): here the sphere of private people debating public affairs critically, which interprets itself through "public opinion"
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch04-bourgeois-p2]] (pp. 117, 125, 127): a sphere of private people debating state and society. Here it is shown as ideology and then reshaped by a wider public
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch05-social]] (pp. 141–142): the realm of private people reasoning on general matters. In this chapter it is a part of the private realm that depended on state/society separation
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch06-transformation-p1]] (p. 181): here, the realm of private persons as a public, debating critically. Its institutions are changed by commercialization and interest politics
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch06-transformation-p2]] (p. 211): space of debating private people mediating state and society. Here its liberal form is described as decayed in the mass democracy
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch07-concept]] (p. 247): space where private people debate; its collapse is assumed as background here
