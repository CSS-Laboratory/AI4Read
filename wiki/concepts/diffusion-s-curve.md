# diffusion-s-curve

## Canon spine entry

- **Diffusion S-curve** (communication-theory): cumulative adoption over time follows a logistic-like curve (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch08-blind]] (§1, para. 5): loosely related: Bury says a speculative idea finds it hard to reach the general mind until it has a concrete embodiment or striking material evidence (§1, para. 5); Lippmann gives no curve
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch01-elements-p1]] (pdf 49–50): cumulative adoption curve that "takes off" at about 10–20% [as `Diffusion S-curve`]
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch01-elements-p2]] (pdf 62): cumulative adoption over time is usually S-shaped; steepness varies
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch02-history-p1]] (pdf 82, 94): cumulative adoption over time is S-shaped; seen by Tarde and Bowers
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch02-history-p2]] (pdf 105, 112): cumulative adoption over time; in Carlson's and the drug study it took off after opinion leaders adopted
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch02-history-p3]] (pdf 127–128): Sept 11 awareness rose slowly, took off around 7:00 and reached 99% by noon; the text says "diffusion curve", not "S-curve"
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch03-contributions]] (pdf 165): S-shaped cumulative adoption curve, used in the chapter's figures
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch04-generation]] (pdf 189): the left tail (first adopters) is where past diffusion studies began
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch05-innovation-p3]] (pdf 274): cumulative adoption curve; its bell-shaped non-cumulative form underlies the Bass model
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch07-innovativeness]] (pdf 347, 350): cumulative adoption over time is S-shaped, with a bell-shaped frequency curve. It is innovation- and system-specific and describes only successful innovations
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p1]] (pdf 377): adopters per period take off after leaders adopt
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p2]] (pdf 405, 407): cumulative adoption curve; here explained by chain-reaction contagion in networks
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p3]] (pdf 443): here, varying individual thresholds are offered as one explanation
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch09-change-p1]] (pdf 462): adoption climbs largely independently of agents' effort later on
