# equilibrium

## Canon spine entry

- **equilibrium** (economics-finance): a state where no agent wants to change its action given what others do (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### economics-finance

- [[akerlof-1970-lemons/main]] (p. 490): price at which supply equals demand, with demand depending on the average quality that the price itself produces, $S(p) = D(p,\mu(p))$
- [[arthur-1994-elfarol/main]] (p. 410): the paper speaks of an "equilibrium pattern" of predictors (p. 409–410) and of consistent, mutually coadapted sets of hypotheses; a unique such set corresponds to a rational expectations equilibrium
- [[grossman-1980-impossibility/main]] (p. 398): overall equilibrium = equal expected utility of informed and uninformed
- [[hayek-1945-knowledge/main]] (§§ VI–VII): Hayek treats equilibrium analysis as a useful preliminary that assumes knowledge matches the facts and so omits the social process
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch02-postulates]]: the situation the classical theory assumes is a limiting point of the possible positions of equilibrium (ch. 1)
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch03-principle]]: employment level with no inducement to expand or contract; may be below full employment (paras. 8, 17)
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch13-general]] (§ II, para. 5): here the rate at which desired cash equals available cash
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch14-classical]]: in this theory the rate "comes to rest" where investment equals saving at that rate (para. 2). Keynes accepts that the rate lies at the intersection if income is assumed given (para. 6).
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch16-sundry]] (§III ¶2): here, in the capital-saturated society Keynes describes, a position where aggregate saving is zero and employment is low
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch18-general]] (§§ II–III): the position determined by the factors, which is itself shifted by repercussions; Keynes notes a "resting-place" for wages is possible only at full employment under the counterfactual in condition (iii)
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch21-theory]] (§ I): Keynes contrasts stationary with shifting equilibrium
- [[minsky-1992-instability/main]] (p. 1): the classic view of the economy as always seeking and sustaining equilibrium, which Minsky disputes
- [[stiglitz-2002-paradigm/main-p1]] (pp. 484–485): in Stiglitz's use, an equilibrium need not clear markets, and may fail to exist
- [[stiglitz-2002-paradigm/main-p3]] (p. 521): Stiglitz doubts equilibrium models describe the dynamics of change well
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch01-supposedly]] (pdf 18): free prices adjust until supply equals demand
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch06-gauntlet]] (p. 13): one of economics' two central concepts, with optimization
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch14-seems]] (p. 93): theory says falling labor demand should lower wages until the market clears
- [[thaler-2015-misbehaving/ch16-mugs]] (pp. 111–112, 114): here, the market-clearing price at which supply equals demand
