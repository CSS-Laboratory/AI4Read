# selective-exposure

## Canon spine entry

- **Selective exposure** (communication-theory): the tendency to seek media that agree with existing views (UG)

## Canon spine entry

- **selective exposure / media choice** (political-science): people choose media that match their tastes or politics, so the high-choice environment widens gaps between groups (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch01-between]] (pp. 21–22): Related: exposure is often voluntary; targets are often least likely to be in the audience (pp. 21–22). The term itself is not used here. [weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch06-interpersonal]] (p. 96): people tend to avoid communications conflicting with their predispositions; the authors find interpersonal flow similar
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch07-group]] (pp. 131, 133): not discussed under this name here; the chapter says media research must study people in their group contexts and that the interpersonal environment is basic to understanding their "exposure and reactions"
- [[lazer-2018-fakenews/main]] (p. 4): preferring information that confirms prior attitudes
- [[lewandowsky-2012-misinformation/main]] (p. 111): choosing news sources that support existing views; fostered by fractionated media
- [[mccombs-1972-agendasetting/main]] (p. 182): the authors' term is "selective perception": voters attend mainly to material favouring their own party
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch05-innovation-p1]] (pdf 231): attending to messages consistent with existing attitudes
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch05-innovation-p2]] (pdf 252): seeking only information expected to support a decision already made
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch05-innovation-p3]] (pdf 270): a barrier that interpersonal exchange can help overcome
