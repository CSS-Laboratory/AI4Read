# spatial-model-of-voting

## Canon spine entry

- **spatial model of voting** (political-science): voters and parties located in a policy space, with voters choosing the nearest party (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch01-democratic]] (pp. 13–14): Downs's account of ideological reasoning helping control of elites; challenged by Converse
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 24): voters and parties placed in policy space. Voters choose the closest party, and parties choose platforms to win [as `spatial model of voting`]
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch04-rational]] (pp. 93–94, 109): the model in which voters choose by policy distance; retrospective theory contrasts with it
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch08-very]] (pp. 216–217): imported economic model of voters picking the nearest party. The authors say it performs poorly empirically
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch10-it]] (pp. 270, 272): voters compare their own position with perceived party positions; partisan perception undercuts it
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch11-groups]] (p. 299): Downsian policy-distance model; the authors count it among the failed scholarly versions of the folk theory
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p1]] (p. 430): Box 11.1 applies the MVT to parties: with one dimension, an odd number of sincere single-peaked voters and two parties, both converge on the median voter
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p2]] (p. 450): actors' preferences as points in policy space, nearer is better
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch15-institutional-p3]] (pp. 720–721): veto players as ideal points, with indifference circles, in an issue space
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 142): a 0–100 left–right scale, single-peaked voters, parties constrained not to leap [as `spatial model of voting`]
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch10-principles]] (p. 182): actors and policies sit in a policy space, with each politician at an ideal point; DW-NOMINATE adds the supposition that legislators vote for proposals closer to their ideal point than the status quo
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/app-ch02]] (p. 234): here, utility is minus distance from an ideal point on $[0,1]$
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p1]] (p. 91, p. 101): voters have ideal points in a policy space and prefer points closer to them [as `spatial model of voting`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p2]] (pp. 112–115): voters and candidates are points in a policy space. Voters pick the nearest candidate (Downsian rule)
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p3]] (p. model named p. 142; ideal points and 'closer is better' appear in the experiment, p. 145): actors' ideal points in a policy space; closer outcomes preferred
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch07-voting]] (p. 208): the one-dimensional framework used for the plurality equilibrium analysis
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch15-courts]] (p. 491): actors have ideal points on $[0,100]$ with preferences declining with distance
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch16-cabinet]] (pp. 519, 522): parties' ideal points on two policy dimensions; closer is better [as `spatial model of voting`]
