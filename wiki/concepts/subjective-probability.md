# subjective-probability

## Canon spine entry

- **Subjective (personal) probability** (bayesian): probability as a coherent degree of belief of an agent, measurable through betting (de Finetti) (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### bayesian

- [[efron-1986-bayesian/main]] (p. 3): de Finetti–Savage subjective Bayes, which has the legitimate claim to philosophic coherency but, per Efron, has failed to make much of a dent in scientific statistical practice [alias `subjective-personal-probability`]
- [[gelman-2013-bda3/ch01-probability]] (pp. 11, 13): here, probability as a yardstick of uncertainty. The authors argue that all statistical methods using probability rely on idealisations, and that judgment is needed for both prior and likelihood
- [[gelman-2013-bda3/ch09-decision]] (p. 256): the authors do not generally find this view useful, and note that decision analysis built on it can be circular
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/appa-other]] (p. 655): probability tied to betting preferences and personal opinion; Jaynes assigns the elicitation of personal probabilities to psychology [work fields: bayesian, information-theory]
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch02-quantitative]] (pp. 44–45): here: "subjective" means that probability describes only a state of knowledge, not that it depends on the person; assignments are meant to be objective in that sense [work fields: bayesian, information-theory]
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch12-ignorance]] (p. 372–373): the view Jaynes criticizes: priors as private opinions, coherence only [work fields: bayesian, information-theory]
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch13-decision]] (p. 425): de Finetti's coherence basis, which Jaynes rejects as foundation [work fields: bayesian, information-theory]
- [[mcelreath-2020-rethinking/ch02-small]] (p. 35): the "subjective Bayesian" school choosing priors from the analyst's beliefs; the author treats the prior as part of the model instead
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch01-causes]] (pp. 8, 11): here, a belief or hunch entering the mathematics, which critics rejected
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch03-many]] (p. 51): belief quantified by a wager (Borel, Ramsey, de Finetti)
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch04-bayes]] (pp. 67–68): hunch-based belief that bans made measurable
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch06-arthur]] (p. 95): subjective, nonstatistical information, such as the quality of a company's managers, used in ratings
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch07-tool]] (p. 103): probability as a measure of belief, usable in a bet; Savage's "personalist" view
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch10-46]] (p. 135): Savage's version, which had a big impact in economics
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch11-business]] (pp. 145, 148): belief expressed in bets and judgment; Raiffa's .92 statement [alias `subjective-personal-probability`]
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch12-who]] (p. 156): degrees of belief about unique events
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch13-cold]] (p. 169): Tukey's acceptance that analysts may reach different results from the same data
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch14-three]] (p. 179): probability reflecting judgment; frequentists called expert judgment subjective and not reproducible
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch15-navy]] (pp. 185, 204–205): expert and commander judgments kept as weights, not discarded
- [[mcgrayne-2011-theory/ch17-rosetta]] (pp. 233–234): probability as a measure of belief, so that it can apply to unique events, as Lindley put it
- [[pearl-2016-primer/ch01-preliminaries]] (p. 15): a prior that is a personal belief, not computable from frequencies; controversial

### information-theory

- [[jaynes-1957-information/main]] (p. 622): probability as a measure of knowledge given the information, in contrast to objective frequency [new]
- [[mackay-2003-information/ch02-probability]] (p. 26): probability as degree of belief that depends on assumptions [work fields: information-theory, bayesian]
- [[mackay-2003-information/ch03-more]] (p. 50): probabilities as degrees of belief about hypotheses, not as randomness in the world [work fields: information-theory, bayesian]

### analytic-philosophy

- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch14-bayesianism]] (pp. 205–206): probability as degree of belief, read from fair betting odds [new]
