# intentional-stance

## Canon spine entry

- **intentional stance** (theory-of-mind): predicting a system by treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires appropriate to its situation (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch01-setting]] (p. 11): Dennett's proposed explanation of why folk psychology works; only named here, defined in later chapters [as `intentional stance`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch02-true]] (p. 15, p. 17): predicting a system by treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires it ought to have; Dennett calls it the "intentional strategy"
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch03-three]] (pp. 49, 73): predicting by attributing beliefs, desires and rational acumen; here "intentional systems" and the stance's limiting-case relation to the design stance
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch04-making]] (pp. 83–93, 106–112): predicting a system by treating it as having beliefs and desires that make sense; here pressed on errors and frogs [as `intentional stance`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch05-beyond-p1]] (p. 116): idealizing characterization of a system (e.g. a frog) by belief-talk; beyond the best approximation, no fact of what it "really believes"
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch05-beyond-p2]] (p. 206): Dennett's own defense of it relies on propositions but only as a heuristic overlay
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch06-styles]] (p. 234): Dennett's predictive strategy; its great (if flawed) predictive power is Dennett's tactical reason against eliminativism
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch07-intentional]] (p. 242): deciding to predict and explain a system’s behaviour with intentional idioms like believes and wants, which presupposes its rationality [as `intentional stance`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch08-evolution]] (pp. 300, 311, 320): attributing content to a system by assuming optimality, applied here also to evolution
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch10-mid]] (p. 348): not named on these pages; the chapter places Dennett among the interpretation theorists who accept Quinian indeterminacy and principles of charity [as `intentional stance`; weak: not really used in this unit]
