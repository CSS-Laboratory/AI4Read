# opinion-leader

## Aliases

## Canon spine entry

- **Opinion leader** (communication-theory): a person who disproportionately shapes others' views within their own social circle (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch06-transformation-p2]] (p. 213): in Katz and Lazarsfeld's finding as reported, usually a higher-status person (wealthier, better educated) through whom political opinion flows vertically
- [[habermas-1989-publicsphere/ch07-concept]] (p. 246): person channelling media influence in groups
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/appb-guide]] (p. 346): here a woman identified by her own report of being asked for advice, on two questions per area
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/appc-follow]] (p. 354): here an "influential" named by a respondent as implicated in her decision
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/appd-construction]] (p. 369): here, a woman with two reported advice episodes, or one episode plus a "more likely" self-rating, in an area
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch00-introduction]] (pp. 3, 5): a person influential for others in their immediate environment, identified in Decatur by self-designation and by follow-up on those named
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch01-criteria]] (p. 138): informal, face-to-face guide of others' opinions; casual and sometimes unwitting
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch02-played]] (pp. 32–33): person with disproportionate influence on others' vote intentions, seemingly spread across all levels. Later suggested to be a role in relations, not a trait
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch02-technique]] (pp. 160–161): here, a specific influential identified by the self-detecting question, whose work is personal influence
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch03-essay]] (p. 44): named, with earlier illustrations, as the starting point for speculating about the two characteristics
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch03-place]] (p. 163): the specific, face-to-face influential in a person's recent decision, even if himself influenced from above
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch04-norms]] (p. 64): mentioned only as a source of research on contact with anonymous others
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch05-evaluating]] (p. 186): mentioned only in passing: leaders may accept media influence more often than non-leaders
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch05-role]] (p. 81): here only a speculation that leaders are more responsive to outside suggestions and more influential in group change
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch06-interpersonal]] (pp. 82, 109, 113 fn. 45): here a bundle of roles (origin, sanction, diffusion of ideas), not one trait; the authors use "multidimensional" terms
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch07-group]] (p. 118, fn. 3): informal influencer in a group; the authors call the gatekeeper idea "very closely related"
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch09-describing]] (pp. 219–220): here, a "self-detected" woman who reports being recently asked for advice on a specific matter
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch10-marketing]] (p. 234): in marketing, a woman recently asked for buying advice (twice, or once plus self-rated as more likely to be consulted)
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch11-fashion]] (p. 247): here, a woman asked for fashion advice on both interviews, or once and self-rated "more likely" to be consulted
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch12-public]] (pp. 271, 273): here a woman recently asked advice on a current social or political issue, a rarer type than in other areas
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch13-movie]] (p. fn. 2, p. 298): here: a woman asked advice twice recently, or once and rating herself "more likely" than others to be asked
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch14-two]] (p. 309): person influential for others within a realm (marketing, fashion, public affairs, movies)
- [[katz-1955-personalinfluence/ch15-summary]] (p. 321–322): a person who disproportionately shapes others' views; here located by concentrations of leaders in groups, rather than by describing one "typical" leader
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch03-contact]] (§3.2): only loosely related here: set leaders guard the set's pattern, but Lippmann gives no media-effects argument [weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch01-elements-p1]] (pdf 41): villagers who could have activated networks to spread the innovation [as `Opinion leader`]
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch01-elements-p2]] (pdf 66–67): informally influential member, central in networks, conforming to norms
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch02-history-p1]] (pdf 82): Tarde: takeoff begins when opinion leaders adopt

- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch03-contributions]] (pdf 159): influential people whom agents could have used

- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch07-innovativeness]] (pdf 358, 368): a person others consult for advice and information. Early adopters have the most opinion leadership in most systems

- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p3]] (pdf 447): as defined in summary
