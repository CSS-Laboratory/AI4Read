# paradox-of-voting

## Canon spine entry

- **paradox of voting** (political-science): the chance that one vote is decisive is tiny, so voting seems irrational when it has any cost (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 27): here used for the majority-decision paradox explored by Condorcet and Dodgson [as `paradox of voting`]
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch05-v]] (p. 48): cited as showing majority decision violates Condition 1 for more than two alternatives [as `paradox of voting`]
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch14-social-p1]] (p. 607): voting is costly and one vote is very unlikely to decide, so parties help mobilize turnout
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 146): Downs's version: since a decisive vote is very unlikely, information for voting is not worth buying. He does not discuss turnout itself [as `paradox of voting`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch09-collective-p2]] (p. 298): the chance of making or breaking a tie is tiny, so instrumental voting seems irrational
