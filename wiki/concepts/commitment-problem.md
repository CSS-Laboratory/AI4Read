# commitment-problem

## Canon spine entry

- **commitment problem** (political-science): a party cannot credibly promise not to exploit a future change in power (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch08-democratic-p3]] (pp. 316, 310): Kalyvas: religious parties must credibly promise not to impose theocracy; amnesty promises also raise it [as `commitment problem`]
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch09-democracy]] (p. 327): asset holders fear later seizure; democratic institutions can serve as a credible commitment mechanism
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch10-varieties-p2]] (p. 379): in Menaldo's account, a monarch's promise to share rents is made more credible by royal courts acting as commitment devices [as `commitment problem`]
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch14-social-p2]] (p. 622): used for urban credibility problems with money, weights and measures
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch16-consequences-p2]] (p. 781): in Iversen–Soskice, SMDP parties cannot credibly promise centrist policies after elections
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch02-institutional]] (p. 44): in this chapter: credibly binding oneself to follow rules when later breaking pays and breaches may go unobserved; condition $C_t > B_t - S$
