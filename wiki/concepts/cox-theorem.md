## Definitions in the sources

### information-theory

- [[jaynes-2003-probability/appa-other]] (p. 657): consistency forces the sum and product rules once plausibilities are real numbers
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/appb-mathematical]] (p. 662): here only cited: it applies on finite sets, where no inconsistency has been found
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch02-quantitative]] (pp. 27, 33, 47): here: the functional-equation derivation of product and sum rules from consistency. Jaynes gives Cox's (1961) shorter proof of (2.27) and a derivation of (2.58) that differs from Cox's original (1946) argument; the unit mentions "Cox's theorems" (p. 47) but does not state a theorem under this name
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch05-queer]] (pp. 133, 142–143): cited to argue that non-Bayesian reasoning is deliberately inconsistent, and, separately, that Bayes' theorem is a valid theorem required by elementary desiderata of rationality and consistency
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch13-decision]] (p. 425): logical consistency as the foundation, preferred over betting coherence
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/ch16-orthodox]] (p. 493): consistency work Jaynes says critics did not examine
- [[jaynes-2003-probability/front-preface]] (pdf 21): Cox's consistency theorems; with Pólya's desiderata they fix the quantitative rules if plausibilities are real numbers
- [[mackay-2003-information/ch02-probability]] (p. 26): beliefs satisfying the Cox axioms map onto probabilities (the author states the axioms, not the theorem name)
