## Canon spine entry

- **common-pool resource (CPR)** (political-science): a resource from which exclusion is hard but use is rival (fisheries, pastures, irrigation water) (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch16-consequences-p2]] (p. 784): actors use a shared resource while paying only a share of its cost, so they over-consume it
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch01-reflections]] (pp. 3, 22, 26): a resource used by many; Ostrom's concern is CPRs where at least some potential beneficiaries cannot be excluded (p. 22), and her focus is small-scale renewable CPRs
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch02-institutional]] (p. 30): resource system large enough that excluding beneficiaries is costly but not impossible; units are subtractable [alias `common-pool-resource-cpr`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch03-analyzing-p1]] (p. 58): a resource system such as a mountain meadow, forest or irrigation system whose users (appropriators) here devised and monitored their own rules (p. 58); a formal definition is not given in these pages
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch03-analyzing-p2]] (p. 88): shared resource system governed by its users. Here, irrigation systems and mountain commons [alias `common-pool-resource-cpr`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch04-analyzing]] (p. 108): a groundwater basin without limits on who may withdraw is an open-access CPR [as `common-pool resource (CPR)`; alias `common-pool-resource-cpr`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch05-analyzing]] (p. 143): shared resource (fishery, groundwater, irrigation water, forest) where users' rules matter [alias `common-pool-resource-cpr`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch06-framework]] (p. 182): a resource whose units are scarce and shared. The chapter studies small-scale CPRs with up to about 15,000 users [alias `common-pool-resource-cpr`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch10-public-p2]] (p. 335): a commons: owned by everyone, so the responsibility of no one [as `common-pool resource (CPR)`; alias `common-pool-resource-cpr`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch17-final]] (p. 531): common resources are mismanaged and overutilized
