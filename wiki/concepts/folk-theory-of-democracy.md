# folk-theory-of-democracy

## Canon spine entry

- **folk theory of democracy** (political-science): the idealized view that informed voters choose policies and governments respond (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch01-democratic]] (pp. 1, 3): idealized view that informed voters choose policy and governments respond; legitimacy from consent
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 21): idealized view: informed voters choose policies, and elections make government follow majority preferences. Dahl's "Populistic Democracy" [as `folk theory of democracy`]
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch03-tumbling]] (pp. 53, 60): idealized view that informed citizens rule directly and "more democracy" is the cure for political ills; the guide for Populist and Progressive reform
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch04-rational]] (p. 90): the idealized view undercut in ch. 2; retrospective theory is a less demanding replacement
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch05-blind]] (p. 145): the view that citizens critically assess candidates and choose by their values. Blind retrospection is hard to square with it
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch07-chicken]] (p. 211): the romantic image of "the people" that the authors say these cases cast doubt on
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch08-very]] (pp. 213–214): idealized view of informed citizens choosing policies. Here it is the "populist/liberal" foundation the authors reject
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch09-partisan]] (pp. 237, 266): the view that informed voters choose policies and parties respond. The authors say its reading of the ideology–party link is misleading
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch10-it]] (pp. 272, 277): the view that informed citizens form preferences and pick policies; the authors' target
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch11-groups]] (pp. 297–298): idealized view that informed citizens choose policies and government follows; the authors treat it as empirically refuted but still used as a normative benchmark
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 147): Downs contrasts his conclusions with the "traditional conception of good citizenship" [as `folk theory of democracy`]
