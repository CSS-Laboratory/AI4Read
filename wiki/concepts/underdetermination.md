# underdetermination

## Canon spine entry

- **underdetermination** (analytic-philosophy): evidence alone does not settle which of several rival theories is true (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch12-scientific]] (p. 181): the empiricists' claim that there will always be alternative theories compatible with all actual, maybe all possible, evidence
- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch15-empiricism]] (pp. 220, 222–223): many rival theories (or layouts of objects) fit all the evidence. Here it is also applied to perception
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch01-introduction]] (p. 4): here: method and observation restrict belief but cannot alone determine one body of belief
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch07-crisis]] (p. 76): Kuhn mentions it as a result philosophers have repeatedly demonstrated: more than one theoretical construction can always be placed on given data; he notes that scientists seldom invent alternates outside the pre-paradigm stage and special occasions
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch09-nature]] (p. 94): related only loosely: logic and experiment alone do not settle paradigm choice (p. 94). Kuhn does not use the term here.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvii-untitled-2]] (p. 383–385): rival laws fit the same finite data yet predict differently (penny tosses, discs on the chessboard)
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch05-problem]] (p. 86): nearest canon term, but not stated in this unit; the unit says only that testing basic statements has no natural end
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch07-simplicity]] (pp. 123, 131): experience does not fix a unique theory. Popper agrees on this point
- [[quine-1951-dogmas/main]] (p. 42): total science is more extremely underdetermined by experience than rational-number algebra by its own base
