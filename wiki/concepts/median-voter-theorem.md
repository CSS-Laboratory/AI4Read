# median-voter-theorem

## Canon spine entry

- **median voter theorem** (political-science): with one dimension and single-peaked preferences, the median voter's ideal point beats all others under majority rule (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 24): with two parties, one dimension and single-peaked preferences, both converge on the median ideal point [as `median voter theorem`]
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch11-groups]] (p. 324): appear only in Zaller's summary of the Downsian optimistic view
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch07-vii]] (p. 80): Black's shortcut: social choice is the median of first choices on the basic ordering; Arrow states it as a remark, not a theorem
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p1]] (p. 429): with an odd number of sincere voters whose preferences are single-peaked on one dimension, the median voter's ideal point beats any alternative
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p2]] (pp. 439–440): stability with one dimension and single-peaked preferences; "cold comfort"
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch16-consequences-p1]] (p. 764): referred to for the ideal majoritarian two-party convergence
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch16-consequences-p2]] (p. 772): here, the median-income citizen is assumed to dictate fiscal policy
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p1]] (p. 96): Black: with single-peaked preferences, the median voter's ideal point has an empty winset [as `median voter theorem`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p2]] (pp. 117, 123–124): in one dimension with single-peaked preferences, the median's ideal beats any other point, and wins any pairwise vote
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p3]] (p. 143): one dimension, single-peaked preferences: the median participant's ideal point has a "magnetic attraction" and majority politics is subject to "centripetal forces" (p. 142); movement toward it is "an equilibrium tendency" in pure majority rule
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch07-voting]] (p. 208): the one-dimensional framework used for the plurality equilibrium analysis
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch12-legislatures]] (p. 389): Black's Theorem applied: the chamber median $x_m$ is the majority's preferred point in one dimension
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch15-courts]] (p. 492): Black's theorem; the median ideal represents each majority-rule body
