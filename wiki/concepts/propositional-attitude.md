## Canon spine entry

- **propositional attitude** (theory-of-mind): a mental state that relates an agent to a proposition ("believes that p", "wants that p") (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p1]] (p. 16): a belief is an attitude to a content; adults' content is propositional
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch01-introduction]] (p. 5): in the normative model, agents hold attitudes (seeing, knowing, believing) towards propositional contents
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch03-three]] (p. 71): Fodor's realism and Dennett's "sort of realist" view of beliefs and desires
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch04-making]] (p. 111): in the Realist picture, beliefs are as numerous and distinct as the propositions that complete them [as `propositional attitude`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch05-beyond-p1]] (pp. 117, 120–121): a mental state relating a person to a proposition; Dennett says the technical term has no stable interpretation
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch05-beyond-p2]] (p. 186): here the broad state attributed by fitting a subject into the real world, in contrast to notional attitudes
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch06-styles]] (p. 228): belief-like states; Fodor's realism takes them to be computational relations to internal representations
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch07-intentional]] (p. 243): attitudes like believes that p, wants that q, expressed with intentional idioms [as `propositional attitude`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch10-mid]] (p. 342): belief, desire and similar states, attributed through intentional idioms [as `propositional attitude`]

### analytic-philosophy

- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch00-introduction]] (pp. 16–17): believing, doubting, desiring, etc., covered by the problem of the logical form of belief; Russell analyses belief as a relation between facts [alias `propositional-attitudes`; new]
