# loss-aversion

## Canon spine entry

- **loss aversion** (decision-game-theory): losses hurt more than equal-sized gains please (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[kahneman-1979-prospect/main]] (p. 279): losses loom larger than gains: $v(x)<-v(-x)$, and $v'(x)<v'(-x)$ if the derivative exists [as `loss aversion`; work fields: decision-game-theory, economics-finance]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/appb-choices]] (pdf 433–434): value function steeper for losses than for gains [as `loss aversion`]
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch26-prospect]] (pdf 274, 276): losses loom larger than equal gains; the ratio is usually 1.5–2.5
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch27-endowment]] (pdf 285, 288): a loss hurts more than an equal gain pleases; loss-side slope is steeper, roughly 2:1 here
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch28-bad]] (pdf 294): we are driven more strongly to avoid losses than to achieve gains
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch31-risk]] (pdf 328–329): losses weigh more than gains; Sam values a loss at twice a gain
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/ch32-keeping]] (pdf 340–341): here weighted about twice as much for losses as for gains, and more for important matters or when responsible
- [[kahneman-2011-thinking/conclusions]] (pdf 404): used to explain why Save More Tomorrow avoids resistance to an immediate loss
