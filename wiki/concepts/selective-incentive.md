# selective-incentive

## Canon spine entry

- **selective incentive** (political-science): a private reward or punishment given only to contributors, used to overcome free riding (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/app-untitled]] (p. 177): reward that can be given only to those who contribute to the group effort; large groups need it or coercion [as `selective incentive`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch00-introduction]] (p. 2): Olson's "separate incentive", given to individuals on condition that they bear costs; for large groups, either it or coercion is needed before they act [as `selective incentive`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch01-theory-p1]] (pp. 15–16): idea only: a sanction or attraction "distinct from the public good itself" (pp. 15–16); the term is not used in this unit [as `selective incentive`; weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch01-theory-p2]] (p. 51): reward or punishment applied to individuals according to whether they contribute; the only thing that stimulates a rational member of a latent group [as `selective incentive`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch02-group]] (p. 61, n. 17): an incentive that distinguishes contributors from non-contributors; social and moral incentives count only if they do so
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch03-labor]] (pp. 72–73): here the "positive" kind: noncollective benefits (insurance, seniority, grievance handling) given to those who join and denied to those who do not [as `selective incentive`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch06-by]] (pp. 133–134): a reward or penalty available only to those who join, such as coercion, services or dividends [as `selective incentive`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch02-institutional]] (p. 45): Elster notes that punishing non-members of a union would need "second-order" selective incentives, which face a third-order free-rider problem
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch03-analyzing-p2]] (p. 96): monitors receive private rewards such as status, part of fines, or sake
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch09-collective-p1]] (p. 269, n. 5): footnote 5 notes that denying benefits to non-payers (tolls, dues, tickets) makes contributions easier to elicit if the benefit is highly valued. The term itself is not used here [weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch09-collective-p2]] (p. 284): benefit given only to contributors, which makes the group goal a by-product
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch11-institutions]] (p. 359): leaders' discretion, partly a reward for shouldering organizational burdens (Olson's term)
