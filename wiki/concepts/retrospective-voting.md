# retrospective-voting

## Canon spine entry

- **retrospective voting** (political-science): judging incumbents by past outcomes rather than by future policy promises (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/app-retrospective]] (p. 329): judging incumbents by past outcomes; here formalized as selection or sanctioning
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch01-democratic]] (p. 3): voters appraise past performance of incumbents instead of weighing policy proposals
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch03-tumbling]] (p. 89): in the chapter, the alternative view that elections hold officials accountable after the fact
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch04-rational]] (p. 91): voting on how things went under incumbents, not on their policy platforms [new]
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch05-blind]] (p. 118, p. 142): judging incumbents by past outcomes. The authors call it natural but not obviously rational
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch06-musical]] (p. 146): judging incumbents by their past performance. Here the authors test it with economic data [as `retrospective voting`]
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch07-chicken]] (p. 181): voting on how conditions have gone, not on policy promises; here applied to election-year income
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch08-very]] (p. 214): judging leaders by past results. The authors say it asks too much of people and yields capricious judgments
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch11-groups]] (p. 303): voters judge incumbents by past performance; the authors say it fails because voters misattribute responsibility and weigh the term poorly
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch16-consequences-p1]] (p. 755): voting on incumbents' past performance
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 138): voters judge mainly on the incumbent's performance in the period just ended, adjusted for expected change [as `retrospective voting`]
