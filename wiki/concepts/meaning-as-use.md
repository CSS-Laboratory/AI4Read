# meaning-as-use

## Canon spine entry

- **meaning as use** (analytic-philosophy): for a large class of cases, a word's meaning is its use in a language (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[lycan-2019-language/ch05-traditional]] (p. 77): "use" theories: meaning explained through language use, left unspecified here
- [[lycan-2019-language/ch06-use]] (pp. 80–81, 88): an expression's meaning is its characteristic function in rule-governed social behaviour. This is "a roughly Wittgensteinian" view, not a doctrine Wittgenstein held universally [as `meaning as use`]
- [[lycan-2019-language/ch12-speech]] (pp. 158–159): here realised by Alston's and Barker's illocutionary theories [as `meaning as use`]
- [[lycan-2019-language/ch14-expressive]] (p. 193): the use theorist's rejoinder: meaning and use are not sharply separable [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch01-augustine]] (p. 25e): for a large class of cases of the word "meaning", though not all, it can be explained as the word's use in the language [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch02-family]] (§138, p. 59e; §141, p. 61e): §138 asks what follows if meaning is the use we make of a word, and sets against it the sense that we grasp meaning at a stroke; §141 accepts the application made over time as one criterion [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch03-understanding]] (§§164, 189, 197, pp. 72e, 82e–83e, 86e): W. discusses *derive*, *read* and *determined* by asking how the expressions are used; he reports that we say a word's meaning lies in its use [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch04-private]] (§270, p. 101): perhaps the way "S" is employed is what makes it a sensation-name [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch05-thinking]] (§§316, 340, 370, p. 111e–123e): meaning is found by looking at a word's application, not at an inner process [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch06-intentionality]] (§432, p. 135e): here: a sign seems dead by itself, and the text says that in use it lives [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch07-negation]] (§§557–560, pp. 157e–158e): meaning shown by the rules and technique of applying a sign (brackets, "is", chess king); the meaning of a word is what an explanation of its meaning explains [as `meaning as use`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch09-ppf]] (p. 223e): the instruction to let the use teach the meaning of "state of seeing"
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch10-ppf]] (§265, p. 226e): the objection raised at §265 is that meaning is the use of the word; the reply is that the felt "fullness" of a word is a figure, and a figurative use cannot conflict with the original use
