# prisoners-dilemma

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch01-problemof]] (pp. 8–10): two-player game with payoff order $T>R>P>S$ and $R>(T+S)/2$; defection is dominant but mutual defection is worse than mutual cooperation [new]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch01-ten]] (p. 32): each side's best choice leads both to an outcome worse for both than another outcome [new]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch03-prisoners]] (p. 79): both players have a dominant strategy (Defect), and mutual Defect is worse for both than mutual Cooperate [new]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch09-cooperation]] (pp. 255, 276): here extended to many players: everyone makes the same, wrong choice
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch14-case]] (p. 402): criminals cannot commit jointly; each gains by cheating [new]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch09-reciprocal]] (pp. 247, 261): each prefers attack although joint no-attack is better; also the warning-system equilibrium [new]

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch04-origins-p2]] (pp. 121–123): game where each has a dominant strategy to defect and the equilibrium is worse for both than another outcome [new]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch01-reflections]] (pp. 3–5): game in which each player's dominant strategy (defect) yields a Pareto-inferior equilibrium; used to formalize the tragedy [new]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch08-cooperation]] (pp. 236–238): a payoff structure where not cooperating is dominant yet mutual cooperation beats mutual non-cooperation [new]
