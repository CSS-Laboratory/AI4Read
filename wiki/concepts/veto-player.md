# veto-player

## Canon spine entry

- **veto player** (political-science): an actor whose agreement is needed to change the status quo. More, and more distant, veto players mean more policy stability (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch01-introduction]] (p. 15): federalism, bicameralism and judicial independence can be thought of as checks and balances that create institutional veto players [as `veto player`]
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch09-democracy]] (p. 337): in the canon, an actor whose agreement is needed. Block says capitalists have a "veto" over state policy through investment, which is a looser sense
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch15-institutional-p1]] (p. 674): here: federalism, bicameralism and constitutionalism are all institutional checks that influence how easy it is to change the status quo ("veto player theory")
