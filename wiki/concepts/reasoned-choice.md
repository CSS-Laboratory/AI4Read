## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/app-ch02]] (p. 229): the kind of choice that, the authors conclude, requires the ability to generate accurate predictions, not knowledge of all pertinent details; these pages do not define it [new]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch01-knowledge]] (p. 2, fn. 2): choice made with knowledge. The amount of knowledge needed is left to the reader's normative view [new]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch02-people]] (p. 18): a choice based on an accurate prediction about its consequence [new]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch04-people]] (pp. 76–77): a choice made with knowledge sufficient for reasoned choice (p. 68); knowledge here means the ability to predict accurately (fn. 1, p. 68). The authors say reasoned choice rarely requires detailed information and does not often need encyclopedic recall [new]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch07-laboratory-p1]] (p. 104): here, operationalised as a correct prediction of the coin toss [new]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch07-laboratory-p2]] (p. 113): choosing the alternative with the highest payoff given the true consequences; benchmarked at 97% in complete information [new]
