## Definitions in the sources

### economics-finance

- [[keynes-1936-general/ch09-propensity]] (§ II, para. 2–4): a factor, used but not defined in this unit, that is taken into account with the interest rate in judging how far interest favours investment [new; weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch11-marginal]] (§ I): discount rate equating expected yields over the asset's life with its supply price [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch12-state]] (§ I): link between an asset's supply price and its prospective yield; defined in the previous chapter [as `marginal efficiency of capital`; new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch14-classical]]: Keynes's schedule of investment demand. It partly depends on the scale of current investment (paras. 6, 16). [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch16-sundry]] (§I ¶4): expected yield of new investment relative to its cost. Investment is worth doing only if it reaches the interest rate [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch17-essential]] (§ I): discount rate equating the present value of an asset's expected yields to its supply price. It can be measured in any standard [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch18-general]] (§ II): expected return on new capital relative to its supply-price; investment goes on until it is roughly equal to the interest rate [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch19-changes]]: expected return on investment; wage expectations shift it (§ II, item (4)) [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch22-notes]] (§ II, para. 2): expected return on new capital goods, depending on scarcity, cost and expected future yield [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch23-notes]] (§ III, V, VI): Keynes's term for the return schedule of new capital; used here but defined in earlier chapters; must be kept distinct from the interest rate [new]
