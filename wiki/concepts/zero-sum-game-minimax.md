# zero-sum-game-minimax

## Canon spine entry

- **zero-sum game & minimax** (decision-game-theory): one player's gain is the other's loss; each guarantees the best worst case (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch01-problemof]] (pp. 5, 14–15): the Senate case is "hardly" zero-sum. Chess contrasts as a game of completely antagonistic interests, where assuming the most feared move is safe
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch04-live]] (p. 77): Axelrod's description of the war at the national level [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch06-choose]] (p. 110): one side's gain is the other's loss; chess is the example. The chapter contrasts it with the Prisoner's Dilemma [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch01-ten]] (p. 37): a game where one side's gain is the other's loss, as in futures trades
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch02-games]] (p. 52): here only the notion: one player's gain is the other's loss; the authors stress that many games are not zero-sum [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch03-prisoners]] (p. 78): only the contrast is used: the pricing game is not zero-sum, as both are better off at top-left than at bottom-right [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch05-choice]] (pp. 144, 147): payoffs always sum to a constant; minimax payoff equals maximin payoff
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch07-making]] (p. 219): the early focus on pure conflict [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch09-cooperation]] (p. 255): contrast case: in a zero-sum game it is not possible to make everyone better off; relative-rank games are not zero-sum, since everyone can gain by cutting inputs
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch11-strictly]] (pp. 339–340): here, a two-player game with exactly reversed preferences; payoffs can be chosen to sum to zero [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appc-re-p3]] (n. 97, p. 374): von Neumann says there could be no theory of games "on these bases" without the minimax theorem [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch02-essay]] (p. 65): here "minimax" is an outcome either party can secure alone, with no worse threat available
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch06-game]] (p. 191): here: pure conflict where minimax makes each decision unilateral, needing no meeting of minds [as `zero-sum game & minimax`]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch07-randomization]] (p. 202): pure conflict, where randomization prevents the adversary's anticipation
