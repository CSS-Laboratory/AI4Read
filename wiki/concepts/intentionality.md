# intentionality

## Canon spine entry

- **intentionality** (theory-of-mind): the "aboutness" of mental states: they represent something, possibly something false or non-existent (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch02-true]] (p. 15): what Brentano and others call the feature of mental states such as beliefs and desires; here the states the strategy attributes
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch03-three]] (p. 63): Brentano's Thesis read via intentional systems as a "reductionist" thesis (p. 67); intentionality of the labels in sub-personal theories
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch04-making]] (pp. 92–93, 112): the "internal state exhibiting intentionality" that we are tempted to find behind verbal acts; Dennett ties content to holistic interpretation
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch05-beyond-p1]] (pp. 153, 159): aboutness; here handled by the notional world, with Pickwick-aboutness and Brentano's intentional objects
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch06-styles]] (p. 224): contentfulness of inner states; Dennett holds that their external semantic properties are fixed only by the global functional role of the state
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch07-intentional]] (pp. 240, 271): aboutness: states that point to other things; Dennett separates this from the everyday sense of acting on intentions
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch08-evolution]] (p. 288): "aboutness" of states; here a contested property: real in humans (opponents) or derived and interpretation-relative (Dennett)
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch09-fast]] (pp. 336–337): the directedness (aboutness) of mental states; here Dennett argues all of it is derived, with no intrinsic kind
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch10-mid]] (p. 341): the meaning or content of psychological states; the shared target of the debate
