# anomaly-crisis-revolution

## Canon spine entry

- **anomaly → crisis → revolution** (analytic-philosophy): Kuhn's cycle in which accumulated anomalies force a change of paradigm (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch05-kuhn]] (pp. 82–83): unsolved puzzles accumulate to a critical mass, scientists lose faith, then replacement [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch06-kuhn]] (pp. 88–89): Kuhn's cycle; here a crisis is disorder after confident work, usually needed with a candidate paradigm [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch01-introduction]] (pp. 5–6): here only anomalies that cannot be evaded, then extraordinary investigations, then revolution. The word "crisis" is not used in this unit [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch06-anomaly]] (pp. 52–53, 61): the sequence from awareness of anomaly, through crisis, to large-scale paradigm change. This unit mainly covers the first stage, anomaly. It mentions crises that lead to large-scale paradigm change and says X-rays took part in the crisis that led to twentieth-century physics [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch07-crisis]] (pp. 67–68): Kuhn's stage of crisis: persistent failure of normal-science puzzles, generating insecurity before a new theory [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch08-response]] (pp. 82, 84): an anomaly usually evokes crisis only when it comes to seem more than just another puzzle of normal science; crisis ends in one of three ways [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch09-nature]] (pp. 92, 97): a felt malfunction is prerequisite to revolution; only recognized anomalies give rise to new theories [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch10-revolutions]] (p. 122): normal science leads to anomalies and crises, ended by a relatively sudden, gestalt-like event, not by interpretation [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch11-invisibility]] (p. 138): only touched on here: the scientist's position seems secure "except during crisis and revolution" [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch12-resolution]] (pp. 146–147): anomalies evoke a rival candidate; the rival's triumph is the "falsification/verification" stage [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch13-progress]] (pp. 166, 169): the community's rigid expectations help generate crises; revolutions end in victory for one camp [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/front-preface]] (pdf 9): anomaly is a violation of expectation. Repeated failure to fit it may induce a crisis (pdf 9). Revolution is not defined here. [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/postscript-1969]] (p. 181): crisis is the usual prelude to revolution, not an absolute prerequisite; it may be caused outside the community [as `anomaly → crisis → revolution`]
