## Canon spine entry

- **rational-agent assumption / principle of efficiency** (theory-of-mind): agents take the most efficient means to their goals; the core constraint for inferring goals (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch02-true]] (pp. 17, 20–21): start from ideal rationality (believe implications, no contradictions) and revise downward; attribute what the system *ought* to have [alias `rational-agent-assumption`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch03-three]] (pp. 49–50): beliefs and desires are those a system ought to have; behaviour is what would be rational; a "myth" that works because we are pretty rational [alias `rational-agent-assumption`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch04-making]] (pp. 94–98): here the assumption that agents are rational, interpreted as "cognitive approval", not deductive closure or perfect consistency [as `rational-agent assumption / principle of efficiency`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch07-intentional]] (pp. 242, 249): attributing intentional states presupposes rationality, used to derive testable predictions as in conditional (14) [as `rational-agent assumption / principle of efficiency`]
- [[frank-2012-rsa/main]] (pp. 2, 10): the speaker is a rational actor maximising utility (informativeness minus cost) [as `rational-agent assumption / principle of efficiency`]
- [[premack-1978-chimpanzee/main]] (p. 518): the authors' proposal: the chimpanzee assumes the actor wants the goal and knows how to reach it [alias `rational-agent-assumption`]
