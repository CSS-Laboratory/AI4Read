# two-systems-account

## Canon spine entry

- **two-systems account** (theory-of-mind): an efficient-but-limited system tracks belief-like states; a flexible-but-costly system represents beliefs as such (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p1]] (p. 4): an efficient, limited system and a flexible, costly system; here argued by analogy with number
- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p2]] (pp. 24, 45): an efficient but limited belief-tracking system plus a flexible but costly one
- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p3]] (p. fn 1; p. 68): the "two systems" view; some authors posit more than two [as `two-systems account`]
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch01-introduction]] (p. 9): two kinds of system make complementary flexibility/efficiency trade-offs; Apperly will argue for this in chapter 6
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch06-cognitive]] (p. 108): adults have efficient low-level modular mindreading and flexible, effortful high-level mindreading
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch07-elaborating]] (pp. 143, 174): efficient-but-limited low-level processes plus flexible-but-costly high-level processes, with independent criteria from signature limits
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/coda]] (p. 183): human mindreading combines high-level and low-level processes that make complementary trade-offs between flexibility and efficiency [as `two-systems account`]
- [[heyes-2014-cultural-evolution/main]] (p. 3): Frith's view: separate fast and slow systems, both specialized for mental states [as `two-systems account`]
