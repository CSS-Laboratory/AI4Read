# propensity-to-consume

## Definitions in the sources

### economics-finance

- [[keynes-1936-general/ch03-principle]]: psychological characteristic fixing how expected consumption depends on income; Keynes holds that consumption rises with income, but by less (paras. 8, 11, 17) [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch06-definition]] (§ II, para. 9): the conception, built on decisions to consume, that takes the place of the "propensity to save" [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch08-propensity]] (§ I): functional relation between income and consumption spending, both in wage-units [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch09-propensity]] (§ I, para. 1): the amount consumed out of a given income, shaped by objective and subjective factors [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch16-sundry]] (§I ¶2): the tendency to spend income on consumption. A lower propensity depresses employment, other things equal [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch18-general]] (§ II): how consumption responds to income; the marginal propensity gives the extra consumption per extra income [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch19-changes]] (§ II, "Perhaps it will help"): how consumption responds to income; for an expansion to pay, the gap between the increments of income and consumption must be matched by investment [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch22-notes]] (§ II, para. 9): how much of income is spent; hurt by falling stock values [new]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch23-notes]] (§ VII): two constituents of effective demand, each with a historical group of theories [as `propensity to consume`]
