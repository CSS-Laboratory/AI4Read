# strategic-sophisticated-voting

## Canon spine entry

- **strategic (sophisticated) voting** (political-science): voting for a less-preferred option to influence the final outcome (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch01-i]] (p. 7): misrepresenting tastes by action when profitable; here the "game aspect" excluded
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch07-vii]] (p. fn. 8, p. 80): misrepresenting an ordering to get a better final result in successive votes
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p1]] (p. 425): voting for a less preferred option to get a better final outcome. Contrasted with a sincere vote, a vote for one's most preferred option
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch16-consequences-p3]] (p. 799): AV is said to reduce the incentive for it [as `strategic (sophisticated) voting`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch03-getting]] (p. 43–44): voting against one's preference to get a better outcome. Whether it pays is assessed by feasibility and desirability
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p1]] (p. 99): voters may misreport preferences; the assumption of sincere voting is flagged, with the topic deferred to Chapter 6 [as `strategic (sophisticated) voting`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p2]] (pp. 110, 138): footnotes say majorities here vote preferences, and sophistication comes next chapter
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch06-strategic]] (p. 167): voting against one's nominal preference to get a better final outcome; "sophisticated" for fixed agendas, "strategic" for one-shot choices [as `strategic (sophisticated) voting`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch07-voting]] (p. 199; pp. 218–219): voting against one's sincere preference, such as Borda misrepresentation or voting guilty
