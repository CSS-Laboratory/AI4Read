## Canon spine entry

- **dominant / dominated strategy** (decision-game-theory): a strategy that is best (or never best) whatever others do (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch01-problemof]] (pp. 8–10): defection is best whatever the other does, so two egoists in a one-shot game both defect
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch04-live]] (p. 77): shooting is dominant in a one-move game [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch01-ten]] (p. 32): in Buffett's offer, supporting the bill is best for each party whatever the other does
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch03-prisoners]] (p. 79): strategy that is best whatever the others do; Rule 2 says use it [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch04-beautiful]] (p. 124): a strategy that is uniformly worse than another is dominated; it will never be used
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch05-choice]] (pp. 148–149): Right dominant for a kicker with poor non-natural-side success; then no mixing
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch06-strategic]] (pp. 175–176): dominance compares payoffs holding the other's choice fixed, so it loses its significance for a first mover
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch10-auctions]] (p. 284): best (or never best) whatever others do; true-value bidding in Vickrey [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch12-voting]] (p. 341): under the median rule, truthful reporting is best for a voter in each of the three cases (left of, at, right of the median), so it is dominant
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch14-case]] (pp. 384, 387, 401): best whatever others do: tendering, truthful Vickrey bidding, criminals carrying guns [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch15-workouts]] (p. 415): truthful bidding in a Vickrey auction is dominant
- [[nowak-2006-five-rules/main]] (§ "Evolutionary success"): defectors dominate without a mechanism: $\alpha<\gamma$, $\beta<\delta$ [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch02-nash-p1]] (p. 25): Fink is optimal in the Prisoner's Dilemma whatever the opponent does [alias `dominant-strategy`]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch02-nash-p2]] (pp. 43–44): strict domination: strictly better against every $a_{-i}$; never a best response, so never in a Nash equilibrium
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch03-nash-p1]] (p. 64): price below $c$ is weakly dominated by $c$ yet can be a best response [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch03-nash-p2]] (p. 83): used in its weak form: never worse, and better against some opponent actions
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch04-mixed-p1]] (pp. 117–118): strict domination may be by a mixed strategy; strictly dominated actions never used in equilibrium, weakly dominated ones may be [alias `dominated-strategy`]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch04-mixed-p2]] (p. 131–132): strictly dominated actions can be dismissed by introspection
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch07-extensive]] (pp. 212, 215): weak dominance of voting for one's favourite, used to prune equilibria [as `dominated strategy`; alias `dominated-strategy`]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch08-coalitional]] (p. 263): each X's naming her true ranking is dominant in the matching revelation game [alias `dominant-strategy`]
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch09-bayesian]] (p. 292): second-price bid equal to valuation weakly dominates
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch12-rationalizability]] (pp. 355, 359): strictly dominated: a mixed strategy does strictly better against every $a_{-i}$; weakly dominated: at least as good everywhere, strictly better somewhere
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appc-re-p3]] (n. 114, p. 377): attack can be dominant for R, yet bring both only zero [as `dominant / dominated strategy`]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch05-enforcement-p2]] (p. 183): a row is dominated if another is at least as good everywhere and better somewhere
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch09-reciprocal]] (p. 247): above the limit, each would rather attack than hope for joint no-attack
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch10-surprise]]: not used here. [as `dominant / dominated strategy`; weak: not really used in this unit]
