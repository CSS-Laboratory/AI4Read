# conditions-for-persuasion-lupia-mccubbins

## Canon spine entry

- **conditions for persuasion (Lupia–McCubbins)** (political-science): a listener learns from a speaker only if the speaker is believed knowledgeable and to share the listener's interests, or if external forces make the speaker's message credible (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/app-ch03]] (p. Thm 3.3, p. 255): trusted speaker, perceived knowledge, and a signal showing the prior is insufficient: individually necessary, collectively sufficient [as `conditions for persuasion (Lupia–McCubbins)`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/app-ch05]] (p. 257): here, speaker credibility is set outside the model; two extreme cases substitute for the conditions
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch01-knowledge]] (p. 9): persuasion requires that the listener perceive the speaker as knowledgeable and trustworthy. External forces can substitute for character
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch03-people]] (p. Theorem 3.3; p. 55): the principal must perceive the speaker as knowledgeable and trustworthy. Absent external forces this means perceived knowledge and perceived common interests
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch05-delegation]] (pp. 84–85): here collapsed into a binary speaker type, persuasive or not [as `conditions for persuasion (Lupia–McCubbins)`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch06-theory]] (p. 98): the theory whose theorems 3.1–3.3, 4.1, 4.2 are converted into predictions [as `conditions for persuasion (Lupia–McCubbins)`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch08-laboratory]] (p. 155): named in Table 8.8 but not defined in this unit; here treatment and control are defined by the speaker's knowledge, interests and external forces
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch09-survey]] (p. 185): absent external forces, perceived common interests and perceived speaker knowledge are each necessary for persuasion; external forces can substitute for common interests (and for each other)
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch10-institutions]] (pp. 205–206, 208): on these pages, a cue or speaker is effective when voters perceive the speaker as knowledgeable and trustworthy; institutions can supply or support these conditions
