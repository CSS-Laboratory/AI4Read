# free-rider

## Canon spine entry

- **free rider** (political-science): someone who enjoys a public good without contributing to it (HS)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch08-democratic-p1]] (p. 279): someone who enjoys the good without paying; here the one who stays home from a protest
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch08-democratic-p3]] (p. 305): individual who gains from democracy without protesting; explains rarity of mass protest [as `free rider`]
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 147): each individual evades his share of the cost of indivisible benefits if others' behaviour is given. Downs does not use this word [as `free rider`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch01-theory-p1]] (p. 21): the idea (not the term here) of a member who enjoys the benefit "whether he had borne part of the cost or not" [as `free rider`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch01-theory-p2]] (p. 35): a member who receives the good free from others and has no incentive to contribute (the idea is stated, not the term) [as `free rider`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch03-labor]] (p. 76): a worker who gets union gains without supporting the union; unions are known to worry about him [as `free rider`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch05-orthodox]] (p. 129): the member who is as likely to get the group's benefits whether or not he worked for them (p. 129) (canon link; not stated in this unit as a term) [weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch06-by]] (p. 134): a member of a latent group who enjoys the collective good without bearing a share of its cost [as `free rider`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch01-reflections]] (p. 6): one who enjoys benefits others provide without contributing. The shared core of the three models
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch02-institutional]] (p. 32): one who avoids contributing to provision, tempted by the high cost of exclusion
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch03-analyzing-p2]] (p. 100): someone who sends weak workers to a labour day, leaving others as "suckers"
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch04-analyzing]] (p. 119): (our label; the text does not use the term) benefiting without contributing: Pasadena stopped independent actions that benefited non-contributors (p. 111); Hawthorne held out and pumped above its allocation (p. 120); the text calls a cooperator who cuts while others do not a "sucker" [as `free rider`; weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch05-analyzing]] (pp. 157, 178): someone who benefits from restrictions on others without accepting them; here seen in Mawelle, Nepal
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch06-framework]] (p. 213): an appropriator who lets others pay for new institutions. The temptation is hard to overcome when someone else agrees to pay the costs, as when officials take responsibility
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch08-cooperation]] (p. 236): the farmer who lets the other drain the marsh and gets payoff 2 at no cost (p. 236) (loose use; Shepsle does not use the term here) [as `free rider`; weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch09-collective-p2]] (p. 280): member who enjoys group benefits without contributing; easier to be one in large, anonymous groups
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch10-public-p1]] (pp. 309, 313, 315): enjoys a good, or a cartel's price, without paying or complying; here shown by cheating on quotas and by not lobbying
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch10-public-p2]] (p. 342): in collective action, defection takes the form of free-riding on others [as `free rider`]
