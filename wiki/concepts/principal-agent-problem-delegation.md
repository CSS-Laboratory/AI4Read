# principal-agent-problem-delegation

## Canon spine entry

- **principal–agent problem / delegation** (political-science): a principal (voters, legislature) cannot fully monitor an agent (politician, bureaucrat) whose interests differ (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch12-parliamentary-p3]] (p. 513): difficulties when a principal delegates to an agent with possibly different goals who cannot be perfectly monitored [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 136): governors' motives differ from social ends. Downs does not use the term, but the problem is the one he poses [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/app-ch03]] (pp. 240–241): the principal is uncertain about the speaker's knowledge and interests, though the unit does not use these terms [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/app-ch05]] (p. 257–258): a principal who cannot see the agent's proposal decides whether to accept it
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch05-delegation]] (p. 79): a principal gives authority to an agent whose interests may differ and who may know more [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch06-theory]] (p. 98): the delegation predictions (5.1 with 3.1–3.3) concern agents' proposals being favorable to the principal [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch08-laboratory]] (p. 149): a principal delegates to an agent with possibly conflicting interests; here the agent chooses whether to propose, and the principal accepts or rejects
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch10-institutions]] (pp. 205, 215): voters, legislators and jurors delegate to agents who may not share their interests. Bad delegation becomes "abdication"
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch14-leadership]] (p. 448): followers (principals) delegate to a leader whose skills can be used against them [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch16-cabinet]] (p. 506): parliament → cabinet → ministers, each delegating with before- and after-the-fact controls [as `principal–agent problem / delegation`]
