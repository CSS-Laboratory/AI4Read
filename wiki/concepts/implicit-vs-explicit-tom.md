# implicit-vs-explicit-tom

## Canon spine entry

- **implicit vs explicit ToM** (theory-of-mind): spontaneous, non-verbal belief tracking vs deliberate, verbally reportable belief reasoning (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p1]] (pp. 2, 15): roughly the infant/animal versus adult contrast the paper builds on
- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p2]] (p. 38): here, implicit looking responses and explicit judgements may reflect different systems
- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p3]] (p. nn. 3, 9; pp. 68, 69): here: eye-movement/looking evidence versus explicit judgements, possibly produced by two systems [as `implicit vs explicit ToM`]
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch03-evidence]] (p. 36): looking and acting patterns that anticipate false belief without correct explicit judgement
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch05-evidence]] (p. 98): here, altercentric interference is described as an indirect or implicit test of perspective-taking
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch06-cognitive]] (pp. 134–135): lower-level processes may be implicit and unavailable for report; higher-level ones operate at personal-level awareness
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch07-elaborating]] (pp. 152–154): implicit measures (gaze, interference) may reveal low-level processing. Explicit judgements are assumed to recruit high-level processing
- [[heyes-2014-cultural-evolution/main]] (pp. 1, 3): implicit: automatic, accurate behavioural expectations, no executive demands. Explicit: deliberation and talk about mental states, executive-demanding [as `implicit vs explicit ToM`]
- [[onishi-2005-infant-false-belief/main]]: nonverbal understanding shown by looking, versus verbal answers; the authors suggest infants have a theory of mind "at least in a rudimentary and implicit form" (para. 11)
- [[premack-1978-chimpanzee/main]] (p. 516): not used by name; the paper uses non-verbal choice tests (canon link; not stated in this unit)
- [[strachan-2024-llm-humans/main]] (§ "Main"): early, efficient but inflexible implicit processes vs later, flexible, demanding explicit abilities [as `implicit vs explicit ToM`]
