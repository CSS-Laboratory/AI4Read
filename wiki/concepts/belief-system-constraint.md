# belief-system-constraint

## Canon spine entry

- **belief-system constraint** (political-science): the degree to which a person's attitudes on different issues predict one another (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch01-democratic]] (pp. 11–12): Converse's finding: citizens' belief systems are thin, disorganized and ideologically incoherent
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 32): correlations among related policy views. Converse found them modest outside elites [as `belief-system constraint`]
- [[converse-1964-beliefsystems/ch01-introduction]] (p. 3): predictability of one attitude from another within a system; static or dynamic; a matter of degree
- [[converse-1964-beliefsystems/ch02-sections]] (p. 30): predictability of one attitude from others. Converse treats constraint and organization as very nearly identical and measures it by the size of correlations among specific issue attitudes
- [[converse-1964-beliefsystems/ch03-sections]] (pp. 38, 44): how far beliefs go together (defined in earlier sections); here shown to fall with information, and not to hide idiosyncratic coherence
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch06-mainstream]] (pp. 113–114): cross-issue consistency; in this account, learned from elites, rising with awareness (D27, D28)
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch09-two]] (pp. 209–210): Converse's "what goes with what". The author adds that, in dynamic settings, lags can make older liberals' constraint point toward supporting the war
