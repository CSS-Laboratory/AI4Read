# principal-agent-problem

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/app-retrospective]] (p. 332): voter as principal using a threshold rule on an incumbent agent whose effort he cannot observe
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch03-tumbling]] (pp. 88–89): officials' relation to voters is not modeled as this in the chapter; it contrasts the instructed-delegate and trustee conceptions of representation
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch04-rational]] (p. 100): the sanctioning model treats voters as principals and the incumbent as an agent with moral hazard
- [[achen-2016-realists/ch05-blind]] (p. 136): used here for the argument that voters monitor responses to disasters to select and motivate leaders
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch07-laboratory-p2]] (n. 25, p. 139): related idea; the only mention here is the authors' note that the experiments apply to learning from either an agent or a speaker
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch01-reflections]] (p. 17): a regulatory agency must ensure its own hired monitors do their job
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch02-institutional]] (pp. 29, 40): (the term itself does not appear in these pages) in the theory of the firm, participants become agents of an entrepreneur who monitors them; this chapter's "principals" are self-organizing users with no outside boss
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch11-institutions]] (pp. 367–368, n. 8): delegator–delegatee relationship with a control problem; delegation to subunits is one instance
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch12-legislatures]] (pp. 384, 389): parent chamber (principal) delegates provisional authority to committees (agents) whose median differs
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch13-bureaucracy]] (pp. 423–425): principal hires a specialist agent whose interests and effort are imperfectly controlled; here legislature/coalition and bureau
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch15-courts]] (pp. 493, 500): raised in Problem 2 about lifetime tenure; the model also treats the agency as an agent

### economics-finance

- [[stiglitz-2002-paradigm/main-p1]] (p. 481–482, fn 33): designing incentives for a manager or worker whose actions are unobserved [as `principal-agent problem`; new]
- [[stiglitz-2002-paradigm/main-p3]] (p. 523): giving incentives to those entrusted to act for others; applies to politics as well as firms [new]

### llm

- [[prince-2023-udl/ch21-deep]] (p. 422): conflicts can arise when a human delegates to an artificial agent: misspecified objectives or informational asymmetry [new]
