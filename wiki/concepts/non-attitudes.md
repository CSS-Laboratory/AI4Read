# non-attitudes

## Canon spine entry

- **non-attitudes** (political-science): survey answers that look like random responses because no real opinion exists (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[chong-2007-framing/main]] (pp. 103–104): survey answers that look random because no real opinion exists, as in the opening literature review
- [[converse-1964-beliefsystems/ch03-sections]] (p. 49): meaningless, randomly varying opinions of people lacking information (p. 49). Converse does not use this term in these pages.
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch01-introduction]] (p. 1): related to idea 3 (no fixed attitude on every issue). The unit does not use this term
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch02-information]] (p. 31): Converse's claim that instability stems mainly from people lacking meaningful beliefs. The author calls it extreme (meant for certain highly abstract issues) and says "crystallization" remains more a metaphor than a testable theory
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch05-making]] (p. 94): Converse's thesis, which Zaller rejects as a general account
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch06-mainstream]] (p. 101): less aware people show higher no-opinion rates (p. 101); Zaller does not use the term here.
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch09-two]] (pp. 194, 205): here "No opinion" is modelled as having no accessible consideration. The probability falls with message intensity
- [[zaller-1992-massopinion/ch11-evaluating-p2]] (p. 308): Zaller says people hold poorly integrated considerations, not "true attitudes" on most issues
