# moral-hazard

## Canon spine entry

- **moral hazard** (economics-finance): hidden actions after a contract change behavior, as with insured parties taking more risk (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### economics-finance

- [[akerlof-1970-lemons/main]] (p. 493, n. 1): mentioned as the different problem stressed by Arrow, in contrast to adverse selection
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch11-marginal]] (§ IV): here, the lender's risk of voluntary default (§ IV); narrower than the canon sense [as `moral hazard`]
- [[stiglitz-2002-paradigm/main-p1]] (pp. 481–482): insurance weakens incentives to avoid the insured contingency; here the related incentive problems are also called "adverse incentives" [as `moral hazard`]
- [[stiglitz-2002-paradigm/main-p2]] (p. 505): care cannot be observed. Each person ignores his effect on the average-risk premium, giving a negative externality [as `moral hazard`]
