# stereotype-lippmann

## Canon spine entry

- **Stereotype (Lippmann)** (communication-theory): a culturally supplied simplifying template that shapes perception before experience (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch03-reconceiving]] (p. 59): what news spreads where no good machinery of record exists
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch01-introduction]] (§7): here only previewed: limited messages from outside, formed into a pattern, identified with a person's interests
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch06-stereotypes]] (§2, ¶1; §3, ¶3): culturally supplied standard picture of a type, which fills in perception and intercepts information before it reaches consciousness
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch07-stereotypes]]: an ordered, more or less consistent picture of a possible world (para. 2); in its perfect form it precedes reason and imposes a character on sense data (para. 7)
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch08-blind]] (§1, para. 1): a fixed picture of the world we expect, which gives familiarity when events match; not necessarily an ideal
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch09-codes]] (§3, para. 1): a loaded picture of kinds of people and events that guides what we notice and judge
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch10-detection]] (§2, §8): a fixed picture that selects evidence and blocks inconvenient facts. In this chapter it is shown at work on space, time, numbers and causes
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch11-enlisting]] (§1.2, §3.2): here the "human stereotype": imputing human nature to inanimate or collective things; also the stock images fixing what seems true or desirable
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch13-transfer]] (§2, para. 4): a shared stock image through which names such as "Lincoln" carry transferred piety
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch14-yes]] (§3, paras 1, 3): stereotyped vision: leaders have it too, and it often makes them "absurd routineers"; hierarchies pass great stereotypes down
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch15-leaders]] (§ 3, para. 2): great symbols take over the loyalties of an "ancient and stereotyped society" (§ 1, para. 2), and the mass hears reports "already stereotyped to a certain pattern of behavior" (§ 3, para. 2). The chapter uses the idea but does not define it.
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch17-self]] (§3, para. 3; §4, para. 1; §5, para. 1): simplifying inherited picture, here the township-based "democratic stereotype" later applied to a complex world
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch18-role]] (§3.1): the set of images, ideas and phrases in which Americans describe politics to each other, e.g. Jefferson's democratic reading of the Constitution
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch19-old]] (§4): access, stereotype and self-interest limit opinion about matters out of sight (§4). Reuse the canon id "Stereotype (Lippmann)".
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch22-constant]] (§1, para. 5): readers do not boggle at untestable news that fits their stereotypes
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch23-nature]] (§5 para. 1): a familiar ready-made pattern that gives readers a foothold and cues their feelings
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch24-news]]: where there is no objective test, the journalist's opinion is in some vital measure built from his own stereotypes, code and interest (para. 3)
- [[lippmann-1922-publicopinion/ch25-entering]] (§1): simplifying template; here one component of how interesting opinion is built, which expert facts break through
