# convention

## Canon spine entry

- **convention** (analytic-philosophy): a self-sustaining regularity that solves a recurring coordination problem, maintained by mutual expectation (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch04-beautiful]] (pp. 136, 138): a commonly understood rule for choosing among equilibria, such as who calls back
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch09-cooperation]] (pp. 259–260): a shared practice; several can be equilibria, and a better one may not win
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch14-case]] (pp. 393–394): hidden-price advertising as a bad equilibrium like QWERTY
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch02-nash-p1]] (pp. 20, 27): the unit calls equilibria stable "social norms"
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch03-nash-p2]] (p. 77): equilibrium read as a steady-state regularity: "challenger always concedes" or "owner always concedes"
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appa-nuclear]] (p. 301): a tradition or precedent whose authority lies in mutual expectations, here the nonuse of atomic weapons
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appc-re-p1]] (p. 343): a priority rule without legal or moral force that still coordinates, e.g. a right-of-way rule
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch03-bargaining]] (p. 83): a "conventional priority", weak for heads before tails and much stronger for the order A, B, C
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch04-toward]] (p. 113): Schelling's "tradition": an expected set of rules that wins by default because everyone expects everyone to be conscious of it

### analytic-philosophy

- [[lycan-2019-language/ch07-psychological]] (n. 9, p. 102): mentioned only: Schiffer's alternative uses Lewis's theory of conventions
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch02-problem]] (p. 32): a decision about rules. Popper's sense is a chosen rule of the "game of science", which differs from a rule of pure logic
