## Canon spine entry

- **cycling / intransitivity** (political-science): majority rule can prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, so no stable collective preference exists (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 26): the problem behind "chaos theorems": any policy can be beaten by another in a majority vote [as `cycling / intransitivity`]
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch01-i]] (p. 3): majority prefers A to B, B to C, C to A
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch05-v]] (p. 46): majority decision does not satisfy Condition 1 with more than two alternatives (p. 48); since its $R$ is always connected (p. 46), the failure is one of transitivity (added step). [as `cycling / intransitivity`]
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch07-vii]] (p. 80): the example of the "paradox of voting" from Chapter I (not described in this unit; usually a cyclical-majority case); it is not single-peakable (p. 80). Note this differs from the canon's "paradox of voting" about decisiveness.
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p1]] (pp. 417–418): Condorcet's paradox: rational individuals can form a group whose pairwise majorities cycle, so the group acts as if intransitive
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p2]] (p. 442): group preferences may be intransitive; transitivity is needed for stability without agenda manipulation
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch03-getting]] (p. 47): group majority preference that loops although each individual's is transitive
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch04-group]] (p. 54): majority prefers $a$ to $b$, $b$ to $c$, $c$ to $a$, violating transitivity
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p1]] (p. 96, p. 109): majority preference may cycle under universal domain; the author's concluding paragraph says "always" [as `cycling / intransitivity`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p2]] (p. 110): in McKelvey's multidimensional result, every point is in one great cycle
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch06-strategic]] (p. 187): the B–C–D majority cycle used in the Plott–Levine design [as `cycling / intransitivity`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch12-legislatures]] (p. 374): in multidimensional issues no majority-rule equilibrium exists, which motivates procedures [alias `cycling`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch14-leadership]] (p. 456): multidimensional bills need only some preference diversity to cycle, so procedural control can be decisive for the outcome [as `cycling / intransitivity`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch16-cabinet]] (p. 524): in lattice examples with no equilibrium, majority preferences cycle over all lattice points [as `cycling / intransitivity`]
