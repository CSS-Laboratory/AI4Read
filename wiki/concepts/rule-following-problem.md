# rule-following-problem

## Canon spine entry

- **rule-following problem** (analytic-philosophy): no finite set of past uses fixes what counts as "going on in the same way" (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch02-family]] (§85, p. 44e): a rule, like a signpost, sometimes leaves room for doubt and sometimes not, and that is an empirical rather than a philosophical point [as `rule-following problem`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch03-understanding]] (§§198, 201–202, pp. 86e–88e): the paradox that no course of action could be determined by a rule, because every course can be brought into accord with it. W. treats it as resting on a misunderstanding, and points to a way of grasping a rule that shows itself in following it and going against it [as `rule-following problem`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch04-private]] (§258, p. 99): one would still have to apply a rule in the particular case without guidance (§292, p. 106); in the diary case nothing besides my impression is available to check against [as `rule-following problem`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch05-thinking]] (§380, p. 124e): rules for a private transition from the seen to the word would hang in the air, since the institution of their application is lacking [as `rule-following problem`]
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch06-intentionality]] (§§433, 506, pp. 136e, 147e): related, not named here: adding signs to an order and the question of interpretation [as `rule-following problem`; weak: not really used in this unit]
