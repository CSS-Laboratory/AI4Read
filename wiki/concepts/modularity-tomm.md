# modularity-tomm

## Canon spine entry

- **modularity (ToMM)** (theory-of-mind): an innate, domain-specific mechanism dedicated to computing mental states (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p1]] (p. 4): belief ascription by modules, fast, structures fixed in infancy (Leslie's view as reported)
- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p2]] (pp. 39–41): Leslie's innate ToMM with subsystems and a selection processor (SP); the authors reject it as an explanation of efficiency
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch01-introduction]] (p. 7): in this unit, only as a view held by others: an innate, fast, automatic, domain-specific module
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch02-evidence]] (p. 19): Leslie's view that specialized innate architecture handles mindreading's representational problems
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch03-evidence]] (p. 39): Leslie's theory-of-mind module only generates candidate beliefs; selecting needs executive capacity
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch04-evidence]] (pp. 61–63): the view that a specialised mindreading module exists; imaging evidence is argued not to support its strong form
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch06-cognitive]] (pp. 120–121): here defined mainly by informational encapsulation (following Coltheart); innateness, speed, domain specificity are not essential
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch07-elaborating]] (pp. 146, 174–176): here modules are informationally encapsulated, low-level and limited. Not a general account of mindreading, and not tied to nativism
