# transmission-view

## Canon spine entry

- **Transmission view** (communication-theory): communication as sending signals across space to control or inform receivers (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch00-introduction]] (p. 4): Carey's "transmission or transportation solution": binding a continental republic by roads, canals and print (p. 5). The unit uses this phrase, not "transmission view". The technological side is "space-binding"
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch01-cultural]] (pp. 12–13): communication as sending messages through space to control distance and people; metaphor from transportation
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch02-mass]] (p. 33): communication as sending messages across space for control; the American problematic
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch03-reconceiving]] (p. 59): in Carey's account of Lippmann, communication as "transmission of a secured and grounded truth"; vision is its metaphor
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch05-mythos]] (pp. 104–105): the authors speak of communication as covering space and signaling time (pp. 104–105); this is only a loose link, since the unit does not use the term (canon link; not stated in this unit)
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch06-space]] (p. 119): communication as sending messages across space. Carey's "power" technology, aimed at reach and low cost (p. 119). The term itself is not used in this unit.
- [[carey-1989-cultural/ch08-technology]] (p. 157): Carey's "transmission model": the model for thinking about communication that, he says, the telegraph provided
- [[craig-1999-field/main-p1]] (pp. 125, 127): communication as sending and receiving messages or transferring information between minds; Craig holds it can be one usable first-order model, consistent with the constitutive metamodel
- [[craig-1999-field/main-p2]] (p. 141): cybernetics "epitomizes" it; problems are breakdowns in information flow
