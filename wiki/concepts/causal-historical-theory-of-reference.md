# causal-historical-theory-of-reference

## Canon spine entry

- **causal-historical theory of reference** (analytic-philosophy): a name refers through a chain of uses going back to an initial "baptism", not through a description (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[kripke-1980-naming/addenda]] (pp. 157, 162–163): a name refers through a chain of transmission from an earlier use. Kripke notes complications: unidentifiable baptisms, shifts
- [[kripke-1980-naming/ch02-lecture]] (pp. 91, 96): Kripke's "picture", not a full theory: reference comes via a chain of communication back to a baptism [as `causal-historical theory of reference`]
- [[kripke-1980-naming/ch03-lecture-p1]] (p. 106): in such cases reference seems to be determined by the speaker's place in a community of users, the name passed on from link to link, not by the speaker's descriptions [as `causal-historical theory of reference`]
- [[kripke-1980-naming/ch03-lecture-p2]] (p. 139): a species name's reference passes along a chain; users need not have seen the sample [as `causal-historical theory of reference`]
- [[lycan-2019-language/ch04-proper]] (pp. 56–58): a use of a name refers via a chain of borrowings back to the naming; Kripke requires the receiver to intend to use the name with the source's reference
- [[lycan-2019-language/ch06-use]] (p. 86): according to Lycan, beside the point for Brandom [as `causal-historical theory of reference`]
- [[putnam-1975-meaning/main]] (pp. 165–166): Kripke's causal theory, said to belong to sociolinguistics [as `causal-historical theory of reference`]
