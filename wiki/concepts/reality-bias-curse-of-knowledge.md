## Canon spine entry

- **reality bias / curse of knowledge** (theory-of-mind): the pull of one's own true knowledge when judging others' beliefs (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2009-two-systems/main-p2]] (p. 21): egocentric bias in adults' belief reasoning
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch02-evidence]] (p. 24): responding from one's own knowledge rather than the other's belief; one "expression" account of EF links
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch04-evidence]] (p. 76): candidate link only: the chapter describes WBA's egocentric responding (judging from his own point of view) and does not use this label
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch05-evidence]] (p. 89): own true knowledge biases judgements of what another will think or do (Mitchell et al. call it reality bias; Birch & Bloom call it curse of knowledge)
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch06-cognitive]] (p. 131): egocentric errors arise from similarity assumptions and from competition with one's own privileged knowledge
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch07-elaborating]] (p. 154): egocentric bias, suggested to afflict high-level model construction but not the module
- [[wimmer-1983-beliefs-about-beliefs/main]] (p. 107): the egocentric reading of "wrong" or "true" against one's own knowledge, instead of the protagonist's belief [alias `reality-bias`]
