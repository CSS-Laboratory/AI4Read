# single-peaked-preferences

## Canon spine entry

- **single-peaked preferences** (political-science): each voter has a favourite point on a line, and options get worse the further they are from it. This restriction removes majority cycles (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[achen-2016-realists/ch02-elusive]] (p. 25, fn. 5): everything left of a voter's ideal point ranks above anything further left, and likewise on the right [as `single-peaked preferences`]
- [[arrow-1951-socialchoice/ch07-vii]] (p. 77): all individual orderings are single-peaked on one strong ordering of alternatives; first choices may differ
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch11-problems-p1]] (p. 428): utility peaks at an ideal point and never rises as options move away from it
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch12-parliamentary-p3]] (p. 515): used here for principal and agent on a 0–10 line [as `single-peaked preferences`]
- [[downs-1957-democracy/main]] (p. 142): assumption 2 of the spatial analogy [as `single-peaked preferences`]
- [[lupia-1998-dilemma/ch05-delegation]] (p. 83): each player's utility falls with distance from an ideal point on $[0,1]$ [as `single-peaked preferences`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch04-group]] (p. 83): here: some option in each triple is never worst for any member; sufficient for transitive majority preferences
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p1]] (p. 92): alternatives on a line; each utility function peaks at an ideal point and falls on both sides [as `single-peaked preferences`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p2]] (p. 114): preference declines with distance from an ideal point
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch05-spatial-p3]] (p. 150): the term is only named on p. 142 and not defined on these pages; the Verba problem describes the idea: an ideal point, with policies further away increasingly less desirable [weak: not really used in this unit]

- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch15-courts]] (p. 491): preferences highest at the ideal point, declining on both sides
