# falsifiability

## Canon spine entry

- **falsifiability** (analytic-philosophy): a theory is scientific if some possible observation would refute it (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch04-popper]] (p. 58): a hypothesis is scientific iff some possible observation could refute it
- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch05-kuhn]] (p. 78): contrast case: Popper's permanent openness to criticism, even of fundamentals (p. 78) (differs from canon: the chapter discusses Popper's critical attitude, not falsifiability as a criterion of what counts as science)
- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch07-lakatos]] (p. 117–118): only implicit here: Popper's conjecture-and-refutation cycle as the one process
- [[godfreysmith-2003-theory/ch10-naturalistic]] (p. 158): "simple falsificationism": nature can say NO to a conjecture; the author calls it inadequate but sufficient for his point
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch08-response]] (p. 77): treated as a "methodological stereotype" that history does not match: no direct falsification by comparison with nature
- [[kuhn-1962-structure/ch12-resolution]] (pp. 146–147): Popper's falsification, as Kuhn reads it. Kuhn doubts falsifying experiences exist, since all theories fit the data imperfectly
- [[popper-1959-logic/appi-untitled-2]] (p. 315): a statement or system conveys information about the empirical world only if it can be refuted by tests; one-sided decidability
- [[popper-1959-logic/appii-following]] (p. 284): here the potential falsifier is a falsifying d+1-tuple
- [[popper-1959-logic/appix-untitled]] (p. 411, p. 416): content measured by improbability; testability and confirmability rise with it
- [[popper-1959-logic/appv-untitled]] (p. 297–298): the objection is framed as an attempt to refute Popper's assertion by deriving two contradictory predictions (p. 297–298); the word itself is not used on these pages.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvi-untitled]] (p. 304): loosely: the afterglow is offered as a crucial experiment between two theories
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvii-untitled-2]] (p. 374, 386): testability (the text does not use the word falsifiability): a logically stronger, more informative theory is better testable and can be better corroborated
- [[popper-1959-logic/appviii-untitled]] (p. 392): content equals testability, measured by how few statements can refute a theory
- [[popper-1959-logic/appx-untitled]] (p. 446): Popper's testability criterion; here the only general mark separating laws from metaphysics
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch01-survey]] (p. 18): a system is empirical only if it can be refuted by experience
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch02-problem]] (p. 27): a statement's openness to refutation by possible observations. Here it is the aim the methodological rules secure
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch03-theories]] (p. 48): laws are non-existence statements, so one accepted singular counter-instance refutes them
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch04-falsifiability]] (p. 66): a theory is falsifiable if the class of its potential falsifiers (prohibited basic statements) is not empty
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch05-problem]] (p. 74): theories are testable by deriving basic statements that could contradict them; here reduced to the falsifiability of basic statements
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch06-degrees]] (p. 95): here a matter of degree, measured by the class of potential falsifiers
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch07-simplicity]] (p. 126): the possibility of refuting a theory by observation (defined in earlier chapters, not on these pages). Here it comes in degrees
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p1]] (p. 133): probability statements are said to be impervious to strict falsification
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p2]] (p. 181): Popper's criterion; probability statements fail it logically
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p3]] (pp. 196–197): probability statements are not falsifiable in themselves; they are used as falsifiable ones through a rule
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch09-observations]] (pp. 228, 245): here applied to path hypotheses and to indeterminist conclusions, which must be falsifiable if empirical
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch10-corroboration]] (pp. 263, 267): theories can be contradicted by accepted basic statements; the asymmetry with verification underlies corroboration

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch02-science]] (p. 22): a statement is scientific if some imaginable observation could refute it; it need not have been tested [new]
