# folk-psychology

## Canon spine entry

- **folk psychology** (theory-of-mind): the everyday belief–desire framework people use to make sense of each other (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch01-introduction]] (p. 2): listed as one of several common terms for mindreading, with "mentalizing" and "social cognition"
- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/ch05-evidence]] (p. 99): touched on only briefly here: a false belief predicts action only together with a desire
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch01-setting]] (p. 7): the everyday perspective using belief, desire, fear, intention etc. to make sense of people [as `folk psychology`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch03-three]] (p. 46): the everyday belief–desire craft, learned like grammar, not as an explicit theory
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch04-making]] (pp. 88–91): the everyday practice of belief/desire attribution, including "forgot", "noticed", "overlooked"; Dennett says it contains confabulation [as `folk psychology`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch05-beyond-p1]] (p. 116): ordinary belief-talk psychology; Dennett says the coming chapters give a more realistic sense of what academic psychology might make of it
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch06-styles]] (p. 232): everyday belief–desire framework; here possibly only an idealized competence model
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch07-intentional]] (p. 279): the everyday attribution of beliefs and desires, used in explaining behaviour, e.g. why John declined an invitation [as `folk psychology`]
