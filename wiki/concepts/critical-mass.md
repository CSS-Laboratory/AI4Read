# critical-mass

## Canon spine entry

- **Critical mass** (communication-theory): the adoption level beyond which diffusion becomes self-sustaining (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### communication-theory

- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch01-elements-p1]] (pdf 41, 50): adoption level after which diffusion becomes self-sustaining [as `Critical mass`]
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch02-history-p2]] (pdf 112): Rogers calls the tipping-point process critical mass (pdf 108); in the drug study, the point when a critical mass adopted and the curve took off
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch06-attributes-p1]] (pdf 289): adoption level after which diffusion continues with little promotion; Rogers puts opinion-leader adoption at roughly 3–16 percent
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch06-attributes-p2]] (pdf 334): mentioned in the cell-phone case as the 1994 point after which households adopted
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch07-innovativeness]] (pdf 343, 349, 358): the number of adopters after which, in the Saucío example, the cumulative adoption rate speeded up, and after which, perhaps, early resistance was overcome in the Iowa corn data; early adopters help trigger it
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p1]] (pdf 377): the chapter's central idea: interpersonal communication creates it
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p2]] (pdf 424): point after which adoption is self-sustaining
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch08-diffusion-p3]] (pdf 432, 449): adoption level after which further adoption is self-sustaining; system-level
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch09-change-p1]] (pdf 462): after it, adoption becomes self-generating
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch09-change-p2]] (pdf 479): adoption level where the S-curve takes off by itself
- [[rogers-2003-diffusion/ch10-innovation]] (pdf 511–512, 514): adoption proceeds slowly at first for interactive innovations like e-mail 'until a critical mass is reached'; Volvo's adoption passed it
