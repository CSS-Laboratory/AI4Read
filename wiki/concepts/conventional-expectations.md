# conventional-expectations

## Canon spine entry

- **conventional expectations** (economics-finance): Keynes's idea that people assume the current state persists unless there is reason to think otherwise (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### economics-finance

- [[keynes-1936-general/ch05-expectation]] (§ II, para. 2): related idea, but Keynes does not use the term here: producers base expectations on the assumption that recent results continue unless there are definite reasons to expect change [as `conventional expectations`]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch12-state]] (§ III): assume the present state continues unless specific reasons point to change; not a belief that it will [as `conventional expectations`]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch13-general]] (§ II, para. 10): Keynes says expectations about the future rate of interest are fixed by mass psychology, as the marginal efficiency of capital is fixed by market valuation rather than the "best" opinion (canon link; not stated in this unit) [as `conventional expectations`]
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch15-psychological]] (§ II.iii): Keynes suggests the rate may be better called "highly conventional": a level accepted with sufficient conviction as durable will be durable
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch19-changes]] (§ II, "contingency"): loose fit only; Keynes does not use the term here, but treats expectations about future wages as shifting investment: an expected wage sag is roughly equivalent to a rise in interest
- [[keynes-1936-general/ch22-notes]] (§ IV, para. 2): Keynes mentions a long-term rate that seldom falls below a "conventional level" [as `conventional expectations`]
