# picture-theory-of-meaning

## Canon spine entry

- **picture theory of meaning** (analytic-philosophy): the early Wittgenstein's view that a proposition is a logical picture of a possible fact (grad)

## Definitions in the sources

### analytic-philosophy

- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch00-introduction]] (pp. 9, 18): a picture is a fact whose elements correspond to the objects in the reality; a logical proposition is such a picture and shares logical form with the fact it pictures [as `picture theory of meaning`]
- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch01-propositions]] (pp. 28–29): here only its first part: a picture is a fact whose elements stand for objects
- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch03-proposition]] (p. 2.2, p. 30): the thought is the logical picture of facts (3, p. 30), and a picture has logical form in common with what it pictures [as `picture theory of meaning`]
- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch04-proposition]] (§§4.01, 4.03, pp. 39, 41): a proposition is a picture or model of reality as we think it is, tied to the state of affairs it communicates by being its logical picture (§§4.01, 4.03, pp. 39, 41) (canon link; not stated in this unit) [as `picture theory of meaning`]
- [[wittgenstein-1922-tractatus/ch06-propositions]] (p. 6.341, p. 84): the net laid over a picture of spots illustrates a form of description (6.341, p. 84); the picture theory itself is not stated in these pages [as `picture theory of meaning`]
