# public-good

## Canon spine entry

- **public good** (political-science): non-excludable and non-rival: you cannot keep non-payers out, and one person's use does not reduce another's (UG)

## Definitions in the sources

### political-science

- [[clark-2017-principles/ch08-democratic-p1]] (p. 278): nonexcludable and nonrivalrous; here democracy is treated as one
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch08-democratic-p3]] (p. 305): democracy treated as a public good, hence the collective action problem [as `public good`]
- [[clark-2017-principles/ch09-democracy]] (p. 344): democratic leaders are argued to provide more of these than dictators

- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/app-untitled]] (pp. 170–171): the text mostly says "collective good" (and "public goods" once, p. 172); the benefits of such a good may reach only a locality or nearly the whole earth [as `public good`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch01-theory-p1]] (p. 14; fn. 21): Olson's "collective good": cannot feasibly be withheld from others in the group once one consumes it; jointness not required [as `public good`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch01-theory-p2]] (p. 28; fn. 44 on Samuelson's pure public good): here "collective good": others cannot be excluded once one member provides it [as `public good`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch02-group]] (pp. 53, 55): here: a meeting's decisions or a partnership's earnings, shared whatever each member contributes
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch03-labor]] (pp. 76, 94): here "collective good": a benefit that cannot be withheld from any one in the group (union gains, taxes-funded services) [as `public good`]
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch04-orthodox]] (pp. 98–100): a good whose benefits go to everyone ("indivisible"), so state services need compulsory payment
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch05-orthodox]] (pp. 126, 129): the group's "public or collective" political objective, whose benefits a member is as likely to get whether or not he worked for them
- [[olson-1965-collectiveaction/ch06-by]] (pp. 133–134): in this chapter "collective good", such as lobbying gains that go to all group members; contrasted with noncollective goods [as `public good`]
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch01-reflections]] (p. 15): Ostrom notes a competitive market is itself a public good, since one can enter without paying for it
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch02-institutional]] (pp. 32–33): here: nonsubtractive goods. CPR provision resembles it, CPR appropriation resembles private goods
- [[ostrom-1990-commons/ch04-analyzing]] (p. 138): information about the basin, funded by voluntary association dues [as `public good`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch09-collective-p1]] (p. 269): once the group goal is produced, all enjoy it whether or not they contributed. Shepsle's polar case ignores exclusion
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch10-public-p1]] (p. 307): nonexcludable and jointly supplied (nonrivalrous) good
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch10-public-p2]] (p. 325): here, an externality is treated as a special case: jointly supplied and unavoidable [as `public good`]
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch12-legislatures]] (p. 377): policy-relevant information and oversight are public goods needing incentives
- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch17-final]] (p. 531): in equilibrium, public goods are underproduced
