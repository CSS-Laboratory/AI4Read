# theory-of-mind-mentalizing-mindreading

## Canon spine entry

- **theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading** (theory-of-mind): attributing unobservable mental states (beliefs, desires, intentions) to explain and predict behaviour (HS)

## Definitions in the sources

### theory-of-mind

- [[apperly-2011-mindreaders/coda]] (p. 183): here "mindreading": ascribing mental states, studied by Apperly mainly for epistemic states [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
- [[baron-cohen-1985-autism/main]] (p. 39): the ability to impute mental states to oneself and others, hence to predict behaviour (Premack & Woodruff, as adopted here) [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
- [[dennett-1987-intentional-stance/ch07-intentional]] (pp. 250, 269): attributing mental states; appears in Premack and Woodruff’s question whether chimps have a theory of mind [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
- [[kosinski-2024-llm-tom/main]] (§ "Significance"): tracking others' unobservable mental states; here the test is false-belief tasks [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
- [[saxe-2003-tpj/main]] (p. 1, footnote 1): here: any reasoning about another person's representational mental states, also called belief-desire psychology; broader than the narrow "theory-theory" sense [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
- [[shapira-2024-clever-hans/main]] (p. 2257): the ability to understand that others have thoughts, beliefs and emotions that differ from one's own; here asked of LLMs as N-ToM [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
- [[strachan-2024-llm-humans/main]] (§ "Main"): ability to track others' mental states; here treated as a set of interconnected notions, not one construct [as `theory of mind / mentalizing / mindreading`]
