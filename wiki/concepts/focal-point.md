# focal-point

## Canon spine entry

- **focal point** (decision-game-theory): a salient solution people coordinate on without communicating (HS)

## Definitions in the sources

### decision-game-theory

- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch01-ten]]: not used in this unit [weak: not really used in this unit]
- [[dixit-2008-art-strategy/ch04-beautiful]] (p. 112): an equilibrium that is obvious ad infinitum, so players' expectations converge on it
- [[osborne-2004-introduction/ch02-nash-p1]] (p. 30): an equilibrium that seems more likely than others to attract players' attention (for example because of the nature of the actions or other information, or, in Figure 29.1, because it is preferable). The term is Schelling's
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appa-nuclear]] (p. 303): a limit with uniqueness and qualitative character on which both sides' expectations converge [as `focal point`]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appb-abandonment]] (p. 327): any clue that leads both players to the same choice; symmetry is one such clue
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appc-re-p1]] (p. 337): a clue that makes one equilibrium stand out for tacit coordination; here "clue", "signal" or "power of suggestion"
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appc-re-p2]] (p. 345): an outcome made salient by uniqueness, simplicity or convention, so that players converge on it; Schelling speaks of "focusing quality" (p. 362) and of "signaling qualities"
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/appc-re-p3]] (p. 386): a "coordination clue" (n. 160, p. 389); in the matrix game of n. 154, only a discriminatory clue can point to a concerted choice [as `focal point`]
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch03-bargaining]] (pp. 75, 91): an outcome that stands out as prominent or unique, so expectations converge on it
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch04-toward]] (pp. 111, 133–134): a clue or qualitatively distinct point that makes players' expectations converge on one outcome; here also dynamic (small concessions are often less likely than large ones)
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch05-enforcement-p1]] (p. 159): the only possible point for tacit collaboration after a coordination agreement
- [[schelling-1960-conflict/ch05-enforcement-p2]] (p. 170): a salient point of agreement that a mediator can create by a dramatic suggestion

### political-science

- [[shepsle-2010-analyzing/ch09-collective-p1]] (p. 272): a salient outcome ("everyone contributes") on which expectations converge (Schelling) [new]
