# Anderson, P. W. (1972). *More Is Different*. Science, 177(4047), 393–396. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.177.4047.393

## In one line
Reductionism (everything obeys the same fundamental laws) does not imply constructionism (rebuilding the universe from those laws), because scale and complexity bring entirely new properties at each level, which, Anderson thinks, need research as fundamental as any other; Anderson suggests that broken symmetry helps to explain this.

## The gist
Anderson opposes the apparent corollary that, if all matter obeys the same fundamental laws, only the people studying those laws do fundamental science. The reductionist hypothesis does not imply a constructionist one: scale and complexity bring entirely new properties. His main tool is "broken symmetry": a large system can settle in a state with less symmetry than the laws that govern it. Ammonia, sugar, crystals and nuclei illustrate this. Mathematically sharp transitions belong to the limit of infinitely many particles. He speculates that life and higher levels continue this hierarchy. So no science is "just applied" the one below it. He suggests, hedged, that the whole–parts relation is perhaps a one-way street: analysis may be fruitful, synthesis all but impossible.

**Prerequisites:** basic quantum mechanics ideas (stationary state; superposition, a state that is a weighted sum of other states; tunnelling), the idea of symmetry, and what a crystal is. The toy example also uses Dirac (bra-ket) notation and complex phases.
