# Grimmer, J., Roberts, M. E., & Stewart, B. M. (2022). *Text as Data: A New Framework for Machine Learning and the Social Sciences*. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press.

## In one paragraph
The book argues that computational text analysis in social science should be organised around research tasks, not around algorithms. The tasks are selecting and representing text, discovering concepts, measuring them, and making inferences (predictive or causal). No representation, model or categorisation is the "true" one. A method is trusted because it passes validation tests tied to the task, not because of its fit statistics or its origin. Research should be iterative. Discover on one part of the data, then measure and test on fresh data, with humans in the loop. The book also treats text as outcome, treatment and confounder in causal studies. Its main point is that borrowing computer-science defaults without checking them against the social-science question leads to weak inferences.

## The path through the work
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch01-introduction]] — Text-as-data methods borrowed from computer science should be chosen, validated and interpreted according to the social-science task (discovery, measurement, prediction, causal inference), not by computer-science defaults.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch02-social]] — Research with text as data should be iterative and cumulative (discovery, measurement, inference, with fresh data for testing), guided by six principles, among them that the best method depends on the task and that validation is essential.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch03-principles]] — The corpus and the numerical representation of text should both be chosen for the specific research question; there is no single "right" representation, and the representation should be checked by external validation.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch04-selecting]] — When a corpus stands in for a population of interest, how well it can answer a question depends on how well it represents that population, and four types of bias (resource, incentive, medium, retrieval) recur frequently as threats to that representativeness.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch05-bag]] — A bag-of-words representation turns each text unit into a row of word counts, built by a sequence of judgement-laden preprocessing choices that are only a default recipe.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch06-multinomial]] — A document can be modelled as a bag of words drawn from a multinomial with author-specific word rates, and a Dirichlet prior works much like simple "pseudo-count" smoothing and keeps estimates away from exactly zero.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch07-vector]] — Treat each document as a vector of word counts, and choose the similarity or distance measure and the word weights to match what "close" should mean for your research question.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch08-distributed]] — Word embeddings replace one-hot word vectors with dense low-dimensional vectors learned from large unlabeled text, so that words used in similar contexts get similar representations.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch09-representations]] — Methods that treat text as an ordered sequence (text reuse, POS tagging, NER, dependency parsing) let social scientists measure copying, phrases, actors and events by using word order, which the word-focused methods of Chapters 5–8 did not.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch10-principles]] — Discovering a new way to organize data is judged by how useful the resulting concept is, not by how it was found, but ideally it should be tested on fresh data, and discovery still needs deep substantive knowledge.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch11-discriminating]] — Words that characterize a known group of documents can be found with several measures (mutual information, Fightin' Words, prediction-based scores), but all must trade off distinctiveness against prevalence, so there is no single best method.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch12-clustering]] — Clustering sorts documents into groups to help a researcher discover new concepts, and because no single grouping is "correct", the choice among representations, methods and K must be judged by usefulness, human reading and later validation on new data.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch13-topic]] — Topic models replace the "one document, one cluster" assumption with mixed membership: each document is a weighted mixture of topics. This gives more focused, interpretable categories for discovery, and metadata can be built into the model.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch14-low]] — PCA and classical MDS place each document in a space of usually one to three dimensions that approximates the high-dimensional text data, and with Euclidean distance the two methods give the same embedding.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch15-principles]] — A text measure is a deliberate compression of documents into a latent representation tied to a theoretical concept, and it should aim to meet five principles, which may not all be fully met in one project: clear goal, an identified (ideally public) source, explainable and reproducible coding, validation, and documented limitations.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch16-word]] — Humans can supervise text measurement at the word level (keyword lists, dictionaries, reference-text word scores); this is simple and cheap to apply but has accuracy drawbacks, so the authors argue each measure needs explicit validation in its own context.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch17-overview]] — Supervised classification turns measurement into prediction: humans label a sample of documents, a model learns to reproduce those labels, and the model's performance is checked on held-out labeled data before the measure is used.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch18-coding]] — A training set of labelled documents can come from trained human coders, a crowd, or found data; hand coding rests on a final codebook and reliability checks, crowd labels must be reconciled, and found data demands careful validation.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch19-classifying]] — Supervised classifiers, from Naive Bayes to deep networks, can essentially all be seen as fitting labelled examples by minimizing a loss plus a complexity penalty, and the fitted model then labels (or measures) a much larger unlabelled corpus.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch20-checking]] — A text measure earns trust by surviving, again and again, tests it would probably fail if it were wrong, using held-out gold-standard labels where they exist and varied external checks where they do not.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch21-repurposing]] — The authors advise using unsupervised discovery models for measurement by splitting the data, choosing a model for its fit to the concept, and validating in training and test data, because the model's output alone does not show that anything was measured.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch22-principles]] — Prediction and causal inference are distinct inferential tasks with different assumptions, goals, validation and use of features, and researchers must say which one they are doing.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch23-prediction]] — Text can be used to predict outcomes outside the text, but good prediction depends on how the model is evaluated, because the link between predictors and outcome can drift and prediction can change behaviour.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch24-causal]] — If the function that turns text into a measure (g) is discovered and used for causal estimation on the same data, SUTVA breaks and results may overfit, so discover g on one part of the data (or in an earlier experiment) and estimate effects on another.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch25-text]] — When text is the outcome of a causal study, learn a mapping $g$ from text to a low-dimensional outcome on training documents, then estimate the average treatment effect on held-out test documents.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch26-text]] — When the treatment is a text, we must learn which latent features of the text matter, and randomising texts identifies their effects only under extra assumptions about the features we did not measure.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch27-text]] — When the text of a document may be related to both treatment and outcome and we do not know which parts matter, we must estimate a low-dimensional summary of the text and adjust for it by regression or matching.
- [[grimmer-2022-textasdata/ch28-conclusion]] — Text-as-data methods help social science most when they are organised around social-science tasks, used iteratively and sequentially, judged by task-specific validation rather than by faith in any one model, and sold with modesty.

## Big ideas across units
- **Organise by task, not by algorithm.** Methods are chosen for discovery, measurement, prediction or causal inference, not by computer-science defaults. This view is set out in Ch. 1–2, returns in the part openers (Ch. 10, 15, 22), and closes the book in Ch. 28.
- **Representation is a question-driven choice.** There is no single right corpus or representation. The corpus is a sample of a population and can carry bias (Ch. 3–4). Counts, similarity measures and weights (Ch. 5–7), embeddings (Ch. 8) and sequence-based features (Ch. 9) each keep and discard different information.
- **No true organisation of text exists; usefulness decides.** Concepts are judged by their utility, not by the method that found them (Ch. 10). This runs through discriminating words, clustering, topic models and low-dimensional embeddings (Ch. 11–14). It also underlies the "agnostic" stance (Ch. 1–2) and the treatment of different model specifications as different measures (Ch. 21).
- **Validation is the central standard.** Ch. 2 states it as a principle, and Ch. 3 asks for external validation of representations. Ch. 15 builds it into the principles of measurement. Ch. 16–21 apply it to dictionaries, classifiers, gold-standard checks and repurposed unsupervised models. Ch. 28 closes with validation tied to a model's use.
- **Separate discovery from testing with fresh data.** Sample splitting and iteration are the safeguard against overfitting and forking paths (Ch. 2, 10, 15, 21). The same logic reappears in the causal chapters, where discovering the text-to-measure function on the estimation data breaks SUTVA (Ch. 24–27).
- **Measurement is compression, and its error should be carried forward.** Ch. 15 frames measurement as compression. Word counting, hand coding and supervised classifiers (Ch. 16–19) are different ways to define the mapping. Measurement error and classifier uncertainty should enter later analysis (Ch. 17, 19, 20).
- **Prediction and causal inference are different tasks.** They differ in identification, goals, validation and treatment of features (Ch. 22). Prediction faces drift and feedback (Ch. 23). Causal work needs identification assumptions and sample splitting (Ch. 24).
- **Text can be the outcome, the treatment or the confounder in causal designs.** Each role needs a learned low-dimensional mapping, assessed on held-out data (Ch. 24–27). Using text does not remove the usual causal problems (Ch. 24, 27, 28).

  - Ch. 8 builds on bag-of-words and cosine similarity (Ch. 5, 7).

  - Ch. 12 needs Ch. 6 and Ch. 7. Ch. 13 builds on the clustering model of Ch. 12.
  - Ch. 17 sets up Ch. 18–19, and Ch. 20 builds on supervised classification.
  - Ch. 21 uses topic models (Ch. 13) and the validation ideas of Ch. 20.
  - Ch. 23 builds on Ch. 19–20.
  - Ch. 24 builds on the inference principles of Ch. 22. Ch. 25–27 use Ch. 24, and Ch. 25–26 also use topic models (Ch. 13).

If you skip Ch. 6, expect Ch. 7 and Ch. 8 to be harder, since they assume its material. Ch. 28 is a short summary and is easy to read at the end.
