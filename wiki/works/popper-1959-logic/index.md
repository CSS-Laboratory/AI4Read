# Popper, K. R. (1959). *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*. London: Hutchinson. (Trans. of *Logik der Forschung*, Springer, 1934 [imprint 1935].) Routledge Classics ed., 2002, 544 pp.

## In one paragraph
Popper argues that science cannot justify its theories by induction. No number of confirming observations verifies a universal law, but one accepted counter-instance can refute it. What marks a theory as empirical is therefore falsifiability: it forbids some possible observations. The rules of method are conventions that protect this property, and basic statements are accepted by decision, not proved by experience. Theories that forbid more are more testable, say more, and are "simpler". A theory is never made probable by evidence. It is corroborated if it survives severe tests, so science is bold conjecture under rigorous test, never certainty. The book also builds a frequency theory of probability, a formal probability calculus, and a critique of the standard reading of quantum theory. The later appendices extend and revise these arguments.

## The path through the work
- [[popper-1959-logic/front-preface-2]] — Popper argues that philosophy's genuine problems are about things, not words, and that epistemology's central problem — the growth of knowledge — is best studied through the growth of *scientific* knowledge, not through analysing ordinary or artificial languages.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch01-survey]] — Popper argues that science cannot justify its theories by induction, so he proposes to analyse it as deductive testing, and to mark it off from metaphysics by falsifiability.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch02-problem]] — Popper holds that science is defined by the rules (conventions) we adopt for treating theories, with the supreme rule that no statement may be protected from falsification, and not merely by the logical form of its statements.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch03-theories]] — Popper treats scientific laws as strictly universal statements that forbid certain events, so they can be refuted but not verified, and a refuted prediction falsifies the whole system that deduced it.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch04-falsifiability]] — A theory is empirical if it forbids at least one possible basic statement (a singular statement of fact), and it stays empirical only if scientists decide not to rescue it with conventionalist stratagems (ad hoc moves that save a theory from refutation).
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch05-problem]] — Basic statements, the singular statements on which tests of theories stop, are accepted by rule-governed decision, not justified by perceptual experience, so science has no absolute bedrock.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch06-degrees]] — Theories can be ranked, partially, by how severely testable they are: a theory that forbids more (a larger class of potential falsifiers) says more about the world, is logically less probable, and is preferred.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch07-simplicity]] — Popper proposes that the epistemologically useful sense of "simplicity" is degree of falsifiability (testability), so simpler theories are prized because they tell us more, have greater empirical content and are better testable.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p1]] — Popper wants a consistent, objective frequency theory of probability whose relation to experience he also wants to clarify, and he begins it with finite classes, selection, n-freedom and the first binomial formula.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p2]] — Popper replaces von Mises's "no gambling system" axiom with the weaker "absolute freedom" (n-freedom for every n), derives the law of great numbers from it without a convergence axiom, and then faces the fact that probability statements are logically unfalsifiable.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch08-probability-p3]] — Probability statements are linked to basic statements only through their existential content, and they are used as falsifiable physical laws through a methodological rule that forbids explaining reproducible effects as accumulations of accidents.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch09-observations]] — Popper argues that Heisenberg's formulae are statistical scatter relations that do not forbid exact single measurements; reading them as limits on precision is an extra assumption, and he traces the rise of indeterminism to a misunderstanding of formally singular probability statements.
- [[popper-1959-logic/ch10-corroboration]] — Theories cannot be made "probable" by evidence; they can only be corroborated, meaning they have survived severe tests, and the more testable (logically improbable) a theory is, the more corroborable it is.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appii-following]] — Popper gives a provisional definition of a theory's dimension, then derives the multiplication, addition, division and Bayes rules for frequencies in finite classes, and the first binomial formula, mostly by counting from definitions, with induction for the binomial formula.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appiv-untitled]] — Popper shows how to build, by a mathematical rule, 0–1 sequences that are free from after-effect in ever-longer stretches. These serve as models showing that "absolutely free" sequences exist, with a frequency limit of 1/2, with a middle frequency but no limit, or with two distinct middle frequencies.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appv-untitled]] — Popper answers an imaginary two-slit objection by re-interpreting it statistically, replacing the single atom with a cluster of atoms; in the third case this gives no interference, so the supposed contradiction does not arise.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvi-untitled]] — Popper's older argument that selecting particles by momentum lets one calculate their earlier positions is withdrawn in a footnote to the appendix title, although the remaining text argues that the procedure leaves the statistical scatter relations intact.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvii-untitled]] — Popper argues that, in his imaginary experiment, each factor of the product (ΔP)y·(Δb₂)y can be made as small as we like, independently of the other. He adds that showing one factor can be made small without the other growing beyond all bounds would already suffice to refute Heisenberg's assertion about the limits of attainable precision.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appi-untitled-2]] — Popper argues that falsifiability, not meaningfulness, separates empirical science from metaphysics, that a logic of induction is superfluous, and that hypotheses should not be given probabilities.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appii-untitled]] — Popper argues that probability theory should be built as an uninterpreted formal axiom system, which classical, frequency and logical readings can all satisfy, with absolute probability defined from relative probability or the reverse.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appiii-untitled]] — Popper uses the classical "favourable over possible" definition, extended to weighted cases, to motivate the general multiplication theorem $p(ab,c)=p(a,bc)\,p(b,c)$, and says that a formal treatment must start from an axiom system, not a definition, and that the theorem may be adopted as an axiom where $p(a,b)$ is to be meaningful even if $p(b)=0$.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appiv-untitled-2]] — Popper builds a six-axiom system for relative probability p(a, b) that does not fix what a and b are, lets p(a, b) be defined even when b has probability zero, and from which the Boolean laws can be derived once Boolean equivalence is defined by formula (*).
- [[popper-1959-logic/appv-untitled-2]] — From a small set of axioms for a two-place probability $p(a,b)$, Popper derives the bounds, the Boolean laws and the usual formula $p(a,b)=p(ab)/p(b)$ (when $p(b)\neq 0$), and so argues that, in its logical interpretation, his calculus is a genuine generalization of the logic of derivation.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvi-untitled-2]] — Popper holds that randomness can be described objectively as a type of order, by constructing "ideally random" infinite sequences whose every beginning segment is as random as its length allows.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appvii-untitled-2]] — Popper argues that, in an infinite universe, every non-tautological universal law (one that says something, not true by logic alone) has probability zero, so probability cannot measure how well a theory is supported or how much it says, and he proposes a finer ordering of zero-probability theories by content.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appviii-untitled]] — Popper argues that a theory with fewer parameters (a simpler one) is never more probable than one with more, so a "simplicity postulate" that links simplicity to high probability contradicts the probability calculus.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appix-untitled]] — Popper argues that the degree to which evidence supports or corroborates a theory cannot be its probability, and he proposes measures (E, C) that reward informative content and severe tests instead.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appx-untitled]] — Popper accepts that laws of nature form a category logically stronger than merely true universal statements, and defines natural necessity as deducibility from a statement function satisfied in all worlds that differ from ours, if at all, only in initial conditions. In his view this changes the metaphysics of laws, not his falsificationist method.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appxi-untitled]] — Imaginary experiments are legitimate as critical or heuristic tools, but in argument the defender may make only idealizations that favour or are acceptable to the opponent; Popper argues that several replies in the quantum-theory debates broke this rule.
- [[popper-1959-logic/appxii-untitled]] — In a 1935 letter, Einstein rejects Popper's imaginary "super-pure case" experiment, argues that the quantum ψ-function is not a complete description of a single system, and says statistical conclusions can come from a deterministic theory plus ignorance.

## Big ideas across units
- **Falsifiability as the demarcation criterion.** Popper proposes it as a convention to separate empirical science from metaphysics, without calling metaphysics meaningless. It is refined, defended against conventionalism, and applied to hard cases. Units: ch01-survey, ch02-problem, ch04-falsifiability, ch06-degrees, ch08-probability-p3, appi-untitled-2, ch10-corroboration.
- **Induction rejected; verification and falsification are asymmetric.** Any principle of induction leads to infinite regress or apriorism, while falsification needs only deduction (modus tollens). Later units extend the point to probability and natural necessity. Units: ch01-survey, ch03-theories, ch10-corroboration, appi-untitled-2, appvii-untitled-2, appx-untitled.
- **Method and the empirical basis rest on decisions, not foundations.** Methodological rules are conventions justified by their consequences. Basic statements are accepted by rule-governed decision (the swamp-and-piles image). A rule against explaining reproducible effects as accidents makes probability statements falsifiable. Units: front-preface-2, ch02-problem, ch04-falsifiability, ch05-problem, ch08-probability-p3, appxi-untitled.
- **Testability comes in degrees and underlies content and simplicity.** The more a theory forbids, the greater its empirical content, the lower its logical probability and the fewer its parameters. Popper equates simplicity with degree of falsifiability. Units: ch06-degrees, ch07-simplicity, appii-following, appvii-untitled-2, appviii-untitled.
- **Corroboration is not probability.** A report on how severely a theory has been tested is not a probability, and more testable theories are less probable. This is backed by zero-probability arguments, measures E and C, and a formal calculus where p(a, b) is defined even when p(b) = 0. Units: ch10-corroboration, appii-untitled, appiv-untitled-2, appvii-untitled-2, appviii-untitled, appix-untitled.
- **A frequency theory of probability that connects to experience.** Popper builds the frequency calculus for finite classes, replaces von Mises's randomness axiom with n-freedom and drops the convergence axiom. He builds model sequences and treats randomness as a type of order. Units: ch08-probability-p1, ch08-probability-p2, ch08-probability-p3, appii-following, appiv-untitled, appvi-untitled-2.
- **A formal, interpretation-free probability calculus.** An axiom system with relative probability as primitive generalizes the logic of derivation. Units: appii-untitled, appiii-untitled, appiv-untitled-2, appv-untitled-2.
- **Quantum theory, indeterminism and imaginary experiments.** Heisenberg's formulae are read as statistical scatter relations. Indeterminism is called metaphysical. The section 77 experiment is attacked, defended, and finally retracted. Rules for imaginary experiments follow, and Einstein's letter disposes of the experiment. Units: ch09-observations, appv-untitled, appvi-untitled, appvii-untitled, appxi-untitled, appxii-untitled.

## How to read it
- **Essential:** The catalog's focus chapters are ch01-survey, ch02-problem, ch04-falsifiability, ch05-problem, ch06-degrees and ch10-corroboration. Read them in that order. The preface (front-preface-2) is a short statement of Popper's aims.
- **Dependencies:**
  - ch02 relies on falsifiability and demarcation from ch01.
  - ch04 develops that criterion, and ch06 uses its "potential falsifiers".
  - ch07 relies on ch06 (dimension of a theory).
  - ch10 draws on ch04, ch06 and logical probability.
  - ch03 gives the logical background (strictly universal statements, modus tollens). It is useful but not in the catalog focus.
- **Probability block (ch08):** read p1, then p2, then p3 in order. Appendices i–iii (appii-following) and iv (appiv-untitled) support p1 and p2. The block can be skipped on a first pass, but ch08-probability-p3 shows how probability statements stay falsifiable.
- **Quantum block:** ch09 comes after ch08 and uses its notion of formally singular probability. Appendices v–vii (appv-, appvi-, appvii-untitled) concern sections 76–77. Section 77 and its supporting appendices are retracted in later notes. Read appxi and appxii for the criticism and Einstein's letter. All of these can be skipped on a first pass.
- **Later appendices:**
  - appi-untitled-2 is a short early statement of demarcation and the case against induction.
  - The formal-probability group depends on this order: appii-untitled, then appiv-untitled-2, then appv-untitled-2. appiii-untitled is a heuristic bridge.
  - appvii-untitled-2, appviii-untitled and appix-untitled extend ch06, ch07 and ch10. appix-untitled needs the relative-probability calculus.
  - appx-untitled on natural necessity is optional.
