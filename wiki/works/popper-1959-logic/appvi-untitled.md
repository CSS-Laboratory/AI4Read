# Appendix vi — A non-predictive procedure of measuring

## L0 — One line
Popper's older argument that selecting particles by momentum lets one calculate their earlier positions is withdrawn in a footnote to the appendix title, although the remaining text argues that the procedure leaves the statistical scatter relations intact.

## L1 — The gist (≤ 120 words)
Popper asks whether a filter that selects particles by momentum disturbs their position. If it does not, the earlier path can be calculated. If it does, the particle must have jumped, which accepted quantum theory forbids for free particles. He argues that the procedure leaves Heisenberg's statistically read formulae untouched, and that reversing the order (shutter first, then filter) gives no "super-pure case". He also proposes a crucial experiment (afterglow behind a filter). A footnote then says the argument fails for a filter acting on a photon and for the electric field, so it is invalid. Einstein's remarks (appendix *xii) are cited for the filter case and the afterglow.

**Prerequisites:** momentum and velocity; wave-packets (sums of waves); probability as long-run frequency; Heisenberg's uncertainty idea. "Scatter relations" and "pure case" come from Popper's section 77 and are not defined here (the text only implies that a pure case is the most homogeneous group the scatter relations allow). Corroborated = supported by tests; superluminal = faster than light; after-glow = light still arriving after the source is switched off; "theory of indeterminacy" = Popper's name for a modified quantum theory, described on p. 303.

## L2 — Key ideas
- Heisenberg's imaginary experiment says momentum uncertainty comes from disturbance, while position uncertainty comes from avoiding strong disturbance (p. 301).
- Popper's old argument: a weak, momentum-preserving interaction also leaves position unchanged, so the electron's past before the first measurement can be calculated (p. 301).
- Two assumptions are compared. If selection leaves position alone, the path can be extended back. If selection disturbs position, exact paths hold only between the two measurements (p. 302).
- A position disturbance with unchanged velocity amounts to a superluminal jump, which accepted quantum theory does not allow for free particles (pp. 302–303).
- Even a modified "theory of indeterminacy" could be corroborated only statistically. The method leaves Heisenberg's statistical formulae unaffected (p. 303).
- Shutter-then-filter cannot beat a pure case. Sharper selection makes finding any particle less probable, and the particles appear unpredictably (p. 304).
- Proposed crucial experiment: filter "after-glow" after the source goes out (p. 304).
- The footnote to the title concedes that what holds for Heisenberg's "observations" does not hold for a filter acting on a photon or for the electric field, which invalidates this appendix, the next one and section 77 (p. 302).

## L3 — Walkthrough

**Setting (p. 301–302).** Take a non-monochromatic beam (many momenta) moving in the x-direction. A filter selects momenta; for electrons an electric field perpendicular to the beam plays the filter's role. Following Heisenberg, assume the selection leaves the x-momenta, and so the x-velocities, unchanged (p. 302). Behind the filter a Geiger counter (or moving film) records arrival times. Since the velocities are known, the x-coordinates at any earlier instant can be calculated (p. 302).

**Two assumptions (p. 302).**
- (a) Selection by momentum does not interfere with the x-coordinates. Then position and momentum measurement "can be validly extended" to the time before the filter selected the momentum (p. 302).
- (b) Selection does interfere with them. Then paths can be calculated exactly only between the two measurements (p. 302).

*Intuition note.* If the velocity is known and unchanged, the position at one time fixes the position at other times. The question is whether the filter secretly changes the position.

**Why (b) is costly (pp. 302–303).** An incalculable change of position, with velocity unchanged, is equivalent to the particle jumping discontinuously, with super-luminal velocity, to another point of its path (p. 302). Popper says this is incompatible with quantum theory as at present accepted. That theory permits jumps only for particles in an atom (discontinuous eigenvalues), not for free particles (continuous eigenvalues) (p. 303).

**The escape: a "theory of indeterminacy" (p. 303).** Popper says it is presumably possible to design a theory that alters quantum theory so that (b) is compatible with it. It could derive only statistical consequences from the principle of indeterminacy, so it could be corroborated only statistically. The principle would then be only a formally singular probability statement, although its content would go beyond the "statistical scatter relations" (p. 303).

**Main claim (p. 303).** The scatter relations are compatible with the assumption that selecting momentum does not disturb position. So that assumption does not let us infer a "super-pure case", which the scatter relations forbid. The method therefore does not affect Heisenberg's statistically interpreted formulae. Popper says the method may thus be said to occupy, "as it were", the same "logical place" within his statistical interpretation as Heisenberg's statement denying the "physical reality" of exact measurements has within Heisenberg's; he adds that one might regard his own statement as a translation of Heisenberg's into statistical language (p. 303).

*Intuition note.* Popper says that, read statistically, the position/momentum limits are not broken even if the filter leaves position undisturbed. The procedure therefore does not give a sharper-than-allowed beam.

**Support: reversing the order (pp. 303–304).** Try selecting position first with a fast shutter, then momentum with a filter, hoping the filter picks a small momentum range without disturbing position (p. 303). Popper calls this mistaken. Schrödinger wave-packets, built by superimposing waves of various frequencies, give only probabilities, read statistically, of finding particles of the given momentum in the group the shutter lets through (pp. 303–304).
- For any finite momentum range $\Delta p_x$, this probability tends towards 0 as the wavetrain is made infinitely short, i.e. as position is measured with arbitrary precision (p. 304). The text layer reads "tends towards to"; see Reading notes.
- "In the same way" it tends towards 0 for any finite shutter-open period (any $\Delta x$) provided $\Delta p_x \to 0$ (p. 304).
- So the more exactly position and momentum are selected, the less probable it is that any particle is found behind the filter. Only among very many experiments will some show particles, and which ones cannot be predicted (p. 304).
- Hence particles appear only at intervals scattered at random, and no aggregate more homogeneous than a pure case can be produced this way (p. 304).

**Crucial experiment (p. 304).** Popper says a comparatively simple crucial experiment seems possible between the theory of indeterminacy and quantum theory. According to the former, photons would reach a screen behind a highly selective filter even after the source is extinguished, for a time. This "after-glow" would last longer the more selective the filter (p. 304). Footnote *2 says this is what will happen according to Einstein's remarks in appendix *xii, and refers to von Weizsäcker's criticism (1934) (p. 304).

**Footnote *1 (pp. 301–302).** This note reports Heisenberg's imaginary experiment.
- To observe position one needs high-frequency light, which strongly disturbs momentum. To observe momentum one needs low-frequency light, which leaves momentum practically unchanged but cannot fix position (p. 301).
- Popper's "old argument": since momentum measurement interacts weakly, it also leaves position unchanged though it does not disclose it. A second measurement may disclose it. So the past before the first measurement can be calculated (p. 301).
- Popper does not see how Heisenberg can avoid this conclusion without essentially modifying his argument, and still believes that his argument and the experiment of section 77 can point out an inconsistency in Heisenberg's discussion of observing an electron (pp. 301–302).
- **Retraction.** He now believes he was wrong to assume that what holds for Heisenberg's "observations" also holds for his "selections". Einstein shows (appendix *xii) that it fails for a filter acting on a photon. It also fails for the electric field, because the beam must be wide for parallel motion. Then the position before entry cannot be calculated precisely after deflection. This invalidates the argument of this appendix, the next, and section 77 (p. 302).

## Toy example (ours, not from the text)
Assume the filter passes only particles with $v = 2$ m/s and does not disturb position (assumption (a)). The counter at $x = 10$ m records arrival at $t = 5$ s.
- Position at $t = 1$ s: $x = 10 - 2\times(5-1) = 10 - 8 = 2$ m.
- Position at $t = 3$ s: $x = 10 - 2\times(5-3) = 10 - 4 = 6$ m.

Under assumption (b), the filter may have shifted the particle by an unknown amount $d$ while keeping $v$. Then the formula still gives $x = 10 - 8 = 2$ m at $t = 1$ s if the filter acted before that instant, but not for instants before the filter acted. At instants before the filter acted, the true position differs from the formula's value by the unknown shift $d$, so we cannot calculate it. So the path is known only between the filter and the counter.

## Concepts
- [[selection-vs-measurement]] (new) — Popper's "selecting" particles by a filter versus Heisenberg's "observing"; the footnote says Popper was wrong to assume that what holds for Heisenberg's observations also holds for selections (p. 302).
- [[non-predictive-measurement]] (new) — a measurement that reconstructs the past (calculates earlier positions) instead of predicting the future (title, p. 301).
- [[statistical-scatter-relations]] (new) — Popper's statistical reading of Heisenberg's formulae; not defined here (p. 303).
- [[super-pure-case]] (new) — an aggregate more homogeneous than a pure case, which the scatter relations forbid (pp. 303–304).
- [[corroboration]] — here: a theory of indeterminacy could be corroborated only statistically (p. 303).
- [[falsifiability]] — loosely: the afterglow is offered as a crucial experiment between two theories (p. 304).

## Canonical instances
- [[filter-and-counter-experiment]] (new) — filter selecting momentum, then counter timing arrivals (p. 302).
- [[shutter-then-filter]] (new) — attempt to get a super-pure case by reversing the order of selections (p. 303).
- [[afterglow-experiment]] (new) — photons arriving behind a selective filter after the source is off (p. 304).
