# Entman, R. M. (1993). Framing: Toward Clarification of a Fractured Paradigm. *Journal of Communication*, 43(4), 51–58. DOI: 10.1111/j.1460-2466.1993.tb01304.x

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[entman-1993-framing/main]].

## In one line
To frame is to select some aspects of perceived reality and make them more salient in a text so as to promote a particular problem definition, causal interpretation, moral evaluation and/or remedy.

## The gist
Entman asks what "framing" means, since many fields use it but none gives a general statement of how frames sit in texts and shape thinking. His answer: framing is selection plus salience, and frames typically define problems, diagnose causes, make moral judgments and suggest remedies. Frames sit in at least four places: the communicator, the text, the receiver and the culture. What a frame leaves out may matter as much as what it includes. A clear concept, he argues, would help debates on audience autonomy, journalistic objectivity, content analysis and democratic theory. He also proposes that communication could become a "master discipline" that synthesizes scattered concepts like this one.

**Prerequisites:** The idea of a mental schema (stored cluster of ideas that guides processing); reading percentages in a survey; basic idea of majority voting.
