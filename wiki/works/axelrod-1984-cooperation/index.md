# Axelrod, R. (1984). *The Evolution of Cooperation*. Basic Books. (Revised ed., Basic Books, 2006, 241 pp.)

## In one paragraph
Axelrod asks how self-interested players with no central authority can come to cooperate. He models the problem as the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma. Computer tournaments show that the simple rule TIT FOR TAT does best. It is nice, provocable, forgiving and clear. Theory then shows how reciprocity can start in small clusters, resist invasion, and spread, provided the "shadow of the future" is long enough. Case studies from trench warfare and biology support the argument. The later chapters give advice to players, advice to reformers, and an account of how social structure shapes cooperation. The main message is that durable interaction, not trust or friendship, supports cooperation. Success comes from drawing out cooperation, not from beating the other player.

## The path through the work
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch01-problemof]] — Self-interested players with no central authority can come to cooperate only if they are likely to meet again, and the Prisoner's Dilemma is the simple model Axelrod uses to study this.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch02-success]] — In two computer tournaments of the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma, the simple rule TIT FOR TAT won both, and Axelrod traces its robust success to being nice, retaliatory, forgiving and clear.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch03-chronology]] — Cooperation based on reciprocity can start in a world of unconditional defectors if cooperators arrive in small clusters, and once established it can be stable if the future matters enough and the rule is "nice" (never the first to defect) but provocable (it retaliates).
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch04-live]] — In the quiet sectors of the Western Front, small enemy units facing each other for long periods formed an iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, and reciprocity let restraint start, last, and then collapse when headquarters imposed raids it could monitor.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch05-evolution]] — Under the conditions the chapter gives, reciprocity (as in TIT FOR TAT) can get started, thrive and defend itself in a biological world, as analysed with the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, and foresight is not necessary.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch06-choose]] — In a long-running Prisoner's Dilemma, do well by being nice, provocable, clear and not envious, with a level of forgiveness that suits the environment, because your own success depends on drawing cooperation out of the other player.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch07-promote]] — Cooperation among egoists can be promoted by changing the game itself: make the future matter more, change the payoffs, teach caring and reciprocity, and improve players' ability to recognize each other and detect defection.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch08-social]] — Who interacts with whom (labels, reputations, governments, neighbourhoods) changes which strategies can survive and spread, and in a territorial system any collectively stable strategy is also territorially stable.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/ch09-robustness]] — Cooperation based on reciprocity can start in small clusters, thrive among many kinds of strategies, and protect itself once established, provided the future casts a large enough shadow.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/appa-tournament]] — Five "representative" rules explain 96% of the variance in round-two scores, and TIT FOR TAT still wins five of six hypothetical tournaments in which one kind of opponent is made five times more common (second in the sixth), so its victory is robust.
- [[axelrod-1984-cooperation/appb-proofs]] — In the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma, whether a strategy resists invasion depends on how much the future matters and on whether newcomers arrive alone or in groups.

## Big ideas across units
- **The shadow of the future.** Cooperation is possible only if players may meet again and later moves count enough (discount parameter w). This runs from the problem statement through stability, biology, and reform to the closing chapter. Units: ch01-problemof, ch03-chronology, ch05-evolution, ch07-promote, ch09-robustness, appb-proofs.
- **What makes TIT FOR TAT succeed.** It is nice, provocable (retaliatory), forgiving and clear. It won the tournaments, and its win holds up under changed mixes of opponents. Units: ch02-success, ch06-choose, ch09-robustness, appa-tournament.
- **Cooperation can start small.** Reciprocators can invade a world of defectors in a small cluster, though a lone cooperator cannot. Once established, a nice strategy that resists single invaders also resists clusters (a "ratchet"). Units: ch03-chronology, ch05-evolution, ch07-promote, ch08-social, ch09-robustness, appb-proofs.
- **Stability depends on conditions.** TIT FOR TAT is collectively stable if and only if w is large enough, and ALL D is always stable against single invaders. Appendix B proves these claims. Units: ch03-chronology, ch05-evolution, appb-proofs.
- **No trust, foresight or central authority needed.** The same logic covers soldiers, bacteria and nations. Units: ch01-problemof, ch04-live, ch05-evolution, ch09-robustness.
- **Not envy, not winning, but eliciting cooperation.** The game is non-zero-sum, so the other player's success is not your loss. Units: ch01-problemof, ch02-success, ch06-choose, ch09-robustness.
- **Changing the game promotes cooperation.** Reformers can lengthen the future's shadow, change payoffs, and improve recognition and memory. The trench case shows monitorable raids destroying cooperation. Units: ch04-live, ch07-promote, ch08-social, ch09-robustness.
- **Social structure shapes what spreads.** Labels, reputation, regulation and territoriality change who meets whom, and so which strategies survive. Units: ch03-chronology, ch07-promote, ch08-social, appb-proofs.

## How to read it
- **Essential:** ch01-problemof, ch02-success and ch03-chronology form the core argument. ch09-robustness gives the summary and lessons.
- **Depends on the core:** ch06-choose (advice) and ch07-promote (reform) build on the tournament results and the stability theory of ch02 and ch03. ch05-evolution relies on TIT FOR TAT and ALL D from chapters 1–3. ch08-social relies on collective stability and the "nice" strategies of ch03.
- **Case study:** ch04-live is an application and can be read after ch03 or skipped on a first pass.
- **Support material:** appa-tournament supports ch02. appb-proofs supports ch03 and ch08 (Propositions 2–8). Both can be skipped at first. Appendix B needs geometric series and inequalities.
