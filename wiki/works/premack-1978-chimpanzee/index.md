# Premack, D., & Woodruff, G. (1978). Does the chimpanzee have a theory of mind? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 1(4), 515–526. doi:10.1017/S0140525X00076512

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[premack-1978-chimpanzee/main]].

## In one line
The authors suggest that a chimpanzee's choices of "solution" photographs for videotaped human problems can be understood as imputing purpose (and perhaps knowledge) to the actor, and they lay out experiments to test this.

## The gist
The paper asks whether a chimpanzee imputes (attributes) unobservable mental states, such as purpose and knowledge, to others. The authors showed the chimpanzee Sarah videotaped human problems and let her pick a solution photograph. She did well, in some cases even when the solution did not look like the scene or the problem was not about food. The authors favour a theory-of-mind reading. They rule out physical matching and treat associationism (learning familiar sequences) and empathy (predicting what she herself would do) as rivals to be tested. They admit the first experiment cannot decide this alone, so most of the paper is a programme of further tests: knowledge, guessing, pretending, lying and self-knowledge.

**Prerequisites:** binomial "chance" reasoning (a two-choice test gives 50% by luck); the idea of controls in animal experiments; basic notions of association versus rule-based learning.
