# Murphy, K. P. (2023). *Probabilistic Machine Learning: Advanced Topics*. MIT Press.

## In one paragraph
The book argues for a model-based view of machine learning: learn models of the data-generating process, and use Bayesian inference as one way to make this precise. It starts with probability, statistics, graphical models, information theory and optimization. It then treats inference as computing posterior expectations, which are exact only in special cases. For the rest it offers approximations: Gaussian filtering, message passing, variational inference, Monte Carlo, MCMC and sequential Monte Carlo. The same tools are applied to predictive models, generalized linear models, Bayesian neural networks, Gaussian processes, distribution shift, deep generative models (VAEs, flows, energy-based models, diffusion, GANs), latent factor and state-space models, decision making, reinforcement learning and causal inference. Its value is a single notation that links these topics. The catalog suggests using it as a lookup reference, not reading it cover to cover.

## The path through the work
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch02-probability-p1]] — The book adopts model-based learning of the data-generating process, with Bayesian inference as one way to formalise it, and Chapter 2 starts from the probability axioms and Bayes' rule and then catalogues the distributions used as building blocks.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch02-probability-p2]] — Gaussians are closed under marginalizing, conditioning and Bayesian updating, and they belong to the exponential family, whose log partition function generates the cumulants (first the mean and covariance) of the sufficient statistics.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch02-probability-p3]] — Densities change under transformations by a Jacobian rule, Markov chains settle into a stationary distribution under stated conditions, and distributions can be compared through density ratios (f-divergences) or through differences of expectations (IPMs such as MMD).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch03-statistics-p1]] — Statistics infers parameters from data; the Bayesian way conditions on the observed data and integrates over parameter uncertainty, while the frequentist way studies how estimators vary across hypothetical repeated datasets, which produces counterintuitive quantities such as confidence intervals and p-values.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch03-statistics-p2]] — When the prior is conjugate to the likelihood, Bayesian updating is just adding data statistics to prior "virtual" counts; where that fails, one uses default, robust or hierarchical priors, and hierarchical models make groups share strength through shrinkage.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch03-statistics-p3]] — In this part, the marginal likelihood (evidence) is used to set hyperparameters and compare models, predictive checks to find misfit, Bayes factors or posteriors over effect sizes to test hypotheses, and an explicit model of the missingness mechanism to handle incomplete data.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch04-graphical-p1]] — A directed acyclic graph defines a joint distribution as a product of small local conditional distributions, and the graph's missing edges encode conditional independences that can be read off by d-separation and used for sampling, inference and learning.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch04-graphical-p2]] — When edge directions are unnatural, define a joint distribution as a normalised product of clique potentials (an MRF or energy-based model); this makes conditional independence easy to read, but the partition function makes learning and inference costly.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch04-graphical-p3]] — Directed and undirected graphical models represent different sets of conditional independences, can be converted into each other at a cost, and can be extended (factor graphs, relational and open-universe models, programs) and given a causal reading as structural causal models, which support intervention and counterfactual questions.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch05-information-p1]] — The size of an update from beliefs q to beliefs p is measured, uniquely up to a constant, by the KL divergence, and entropy is a shifted KL divergence from the uniform distribution.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch05-information-p2]] — Mutual information is the KL divergence between a joint distribution and the product of its marginals; it measures dependence and appears in variational bounds, rate–distortion and bits-back compression, and bottleneck objectives; the part ends with Kolmogorov complexity, an alternative notion of information for individual sequences.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch06-optimization-p1]] — Gradients of complicated, possibly random objectives can be computed automatically (forward/reverse-mode AD, score-function and reparameterization estimators), and can be rescaled by the Fisher information matrix (natural gradient) so that steps are measured in distribution space rather than parameter space.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch06-optimization-p2]] — When the true objective is hard to optimize directly, optimize something easier instead: a lower bound that is refitted each step (MM, EM), a probabilistic surrogate of an expensive function (Bayesian optimization), or a search over candidates or distributions that needs only function values (derivative-free methods).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch06-optimization-p3]] — Optimal transport compares distributions by the cheapest way to move mass from one to the other, and submodular (diminishing-returns) set functions can be minimized exactly in polynomial time, while monotone ones (polymatroid functions) can be maximized approximately by greedy search under a cardinality constraint.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch07-inference]] — Bayesian inference means computing posterior expectations (integrals), which is exact only in special cases; otherwise we choose among approximations (the MAP point estimate, grid, Laplace, variational inference (VI), Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)) that trade off speed, accuracy and generality.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch08-gaussian-p1]] — In state-space models, filtering and smoothing are recursions of predict, update and backward-correct steps. They are exact in closed form when everything is linear-Gaussian (Kalman filter and RTS smoother), and approximate after linearizing a nonlinear model (extended Kalman filter).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch08-gaussian-p2]] — When the dynamics or observation model is nonlinear or non-Gaussian, one can still filter with Gaussians by approximating the needed joint distribution (or the posterior) with a Gaussian chosen by moment matching.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch09-message]] — On a sparse graph, posterior inference can be organised as messages passed between neighbouring nodes (dynamic programming): exact on chains and trees, an approximation on loopy graphs, while variable elimination is exact on any discrete graph at a cost exponential in the size of its largest intermediate factor.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch10-variational-p1]] — Variational inference turns intractable posterior inference into optimization: maximize the ELBO, a lower bound on log evidence, over a family of simple distributions q, using reparameterized gradients, minibatches and inference networks.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch10-variational-p2]] — Beyond plain gradient ascent on the ELBO, one can optimise a mean-field posterior factor by factor (CAVI, with update $q_j\propto\exp\mathbb E[\log\tilde p]$), improve the family or the bound, replace the ELBO by wake-sleep objectives, or use expectation propagation (EP), which works with $D_{\mathbb{KL}}(p\|q)$ locally.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch11-monte]] — Replace a hard integral (an expectation) by an average over random samples, whose error shrinks like $1/\sqrt{N_s}$ in principle whatever the dimension, and then work on how to generate good samples and how to cut the variance.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch12-markov-p1]] — MCMC samples from a hard, high-dimensional target by running a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is that target (via accept/reject moves, conditional resampling, auxiliary variables, or simulated Hamiltonian dynamics).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch12-markov-p2]] — MCMC samples are only trustworthy after the chain has mixed, so we need theory and diagnostics for mixing, and extra tools (reparameterization, minibatch gradients, trans-dimensional moves, annealing) when geometry, data size, model size or multimodality break simple samplers.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch13-sequential]] — Sequential Monte Carlo grows weighted samples ("particles") through a sequence of related distributions, using resampling to stop the weights collapsing, and the same machinery serves both state-space filtering and sampling from static targets.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch14-predictive]] — Predictive models (fitted by ERM, MAP or Bayesian-style objectives) can be judged by proper scoring rules, calibration and joint predictions, and conformal prediction can wrap any of them in sets with a distribution-free coverage guarantee.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch15-generalized]] — A GLM models a response with an exponential-family distribution whose mean is a function of a linear predictor $w^\top x$, and the chapter shows how to do Bayesian inference, choose priors and share information across groups for the main GLMs.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch16-deep]] — A deep neural network is any differentiable function written as a computation graph of simple blocks (linear, convolutional, attention, recurrent and others), and it can be used either inside probabilistic models or to approximate their posteriors.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch17-bayesian]] — Because many different parameter settings of a deep network fit the training data well yet generalize differently, averaging predictions over the posterior (approximately, e.g. with ensembles of distinct modes) can improve accuracy and uncertainty representation. How well this works depends on the prior, the architecture and the approximation used.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch18-gaussian-p1]] — A Gaussian process puts a prior directly on functions, defined by a mean function and a kernel. With Gaussian noise the posterior is another GP in closed form, and its mean equals kernel ridge regression (when the penalty λ equals the noise variance).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch18-gaussian-p2]] — Exact GP inference costs $O(N^3)$, but inducing points, variational bounds, matrix-vector methods and state-space forms scale it up, while the kernel itself can be learned from data (by marginal likelihood, search, spectral mixtures, deep features) and relates to infinitely wide neural networks.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch19-beyond]] — When test data differ from training data, the right remedy depends on which factor of the joint distribution changed, and worst-case (adversarial) errors exist whenever errors exist.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch20-generative]] — A generative model is a joint distribution p(x) (optionally p(x|c)); models differ in density, sampling, training, latents and architecture, serve many tasks, and are hard to evaluate: no single metric covers everything, and likelihood and sample quality can disagree.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch21-variational]] — A VAE trains a decoder (generative model) and an encoder (amortized approximate posterior) together by maximizing the ELBO, and the chapter then presents variants that reweight the ELBO terms, change the latent structure or change the encoder or decoder, and several methods address the recurring problem of posterior collapse.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch22-autoregressive]] — The chain rule writes any joint distribution as a product of "next item given the past" conditionals; autoregressive models give each conditional a restricted neural form (masked networks, causal convolutions, transformers), which gives exact likelihoods but slow sequential sampling.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch23-normalizing]] — A normalizing flow turns a simple base distribution into a complex one through an invertible map whose Jacobian determinant is cheap to compute, giving easy sampling and an exact likelihood (p. 829).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch24-energy]] — An energy-based model defines a density up to an intractable constant $Z_\theta$, so training must avoid computing $Z_\theta$: either by sampling the model (MCMC, contrastive divergence) or by using objectives in which $Z_\theta$ cancels (score matching, noise contrastive estimation, and others).
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch25-diffusion]] — Diffusion models generate data by learning to reverse a fixed process that gradually turns data into Gaussian noise. Training reduces to predicting the added noise (equivalently, the score), and the same model can be run as a stochastic or a deterministic sampler.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch26-generative]] — Implicit generative models have no usable likelihood, so GANs train a critic from samples to approximate a divergence or distance between data and model, and train the generator to shrink that approximation.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch28-latent-p1]] — A latent factor model explains observed data x through a few hidden factors z via a simple decoder, and by changing the prior on z and the likelihood of x you obtain mixtures, factor analysis, PPCA, CCA and many other classical models.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch28-latent-p2]] — Replacing the Gaussian latent prior of factor analysis by gamma, Dirichlet, logistic-normal, Laplace or other non-Gaussian priors gives non-negative parts, mixed-membership topics, independent sources that can be recovered up to permutation and sign, and sparse codes.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch29-state-p1]] — A hidden Markov model is a state-space model with a discrete hidden state, and its parameters can be learned by EM (expectation-maximisation, called Baum-Welch for HMMs), by SGD (stochastic gradient descent) on the forward-algorithm likelihood, by spectral methods, or by Bayesian posterior sampling.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch29-state-p2]] — Starting from the HMM, one can add durations, hierarchy, several chains or run lengths; making everything linear-Gaussian gives the LDS, with exact filtering, EM learning and tracking uses, while switching or nonlinear versions need approximate inference.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch29-state-p3]] — A forecasting model can be built by adding simple linear-Gaussian latent components (trend, season, regressors); the same state-space idea then extends to count data, to counterfactual causal-impact estimation, and to neural-network dynamics trained with an ELBO.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch30-graph]] — Probability models can be used for graphs in two ways: explain a known graph with latent variables, or infer an unknown graph from data (structure learning), which is useful for understanding, prediction and causal inference.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch32-representation]] — Representation learning trains a network on a cleverly designed task (supervised, generative or self-supervised) so that its features help with later, unknown tasks. Such features are judged by downstream performance or by similarity measures, and the theory of when they are recoverable is still thin.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch33-interpretability]] — Interpretability is not a single mathematical property but a means to an end: an explanation is judged by whether, in a given context, it helps a human do their end-task better.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch34-decision]] — Once you have a posterior, the best action (or policy) is the one that minimises posterior expected loss; the chapter works this out for one-shot choices, A/B tests, bandits, MDPs and active learning.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch35-reinforcement]] — An agent that does not know its environment can learn a reward-maximizing policy by bootstrapped value updates, policy gradients, or a learned model. Learning from another policy's data needs correction for distribution mismatch, and control can also be recast as probabilistic inference.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch36-causality-p1]] — The average treatment effect is defined through interventions in a structural causal model, and if all common causes are observed and overlap holds, it equals a covariate-adjusted difference of means that can be estimated by outcome regression, propensity weighting, or a doubly robust "double ML" estimator.
- [[murphy-2023-pml2/ch36-causality-p2]] — When we cannot adjust for all confounders, we can sometimes still identify causal effects from observational data by adding a design assumption (instrument, parallel trends, or a causal graph via the do-calculus), and we should then stress-test the assumptions that data cannot check.

## Big ideas across units
- **Inference is integration, and approximations trade accuracy for scale.** Posterior quantities are expectations whose normalising constant is the bottleneck. Responses are the grid and Laplace approximations, VI, MCMC and SMC. Units: ch07-inference, ch10-variational-p1, ch10-variational-p2, ch11-monte, ch12-markov-p1, ch12-markov-p2, ch13-sequential, ch09-message.
- **Gaussians and conjugacy give closed-form updates that later chapters reuse.** The same update shows up in conjugate priors, Kalman filtering, GPs and linear-Gaussian state-space models. Moment matching then carries it to non-Gaussian settings. Units: ch02-probability-p2, ch03-statistics-p2, ch08-gaussian-p1, ch08-gaussian-p2, ch18-gaussian-p1, ch29-state-p2, ch29-state-p3.
- **KL divergence and information-theoretic quantities underlie many objectives.** KL is the unique measure of belief updates. Forward and reverse KL explain moment matching (EP, ADF) and mode-seeking VI. Mutual information drives bottleneck and InfoNCE objectives. Units: ch02-probability-p3, ch05-information-p1, ch05-information-p2, ch10-variational-p1, ch10-variational-p2, ch08-gaussian-p2, ch21-variational, ch32-representation.
- **The ELBO and amortized inference connect classical and deep latent-variable models.** The path runs from EM and MM through VI to VAEs, SMC bounds and deep state-space models. Units: ch06-optimization-p2, ch10-variational-p1, ch21-variational, ch13-sequential, ch29-state-p3, ch28-latent-p1.
- **Graph structure supplies independence, factorisation and efficient inference.** The sequence is DAGs and MRFs, then message passing and variable elimination, then HMM and state-space inference, then graphical language for causality. Units: ch04-graphical-p1, ch04-graphical-p2, ch04-graphical-p3, ch09-message, ch29-state-p1, ch29-state-p2, ch30-graph.
- **Hierarchical models share strength, and their funnel geometry needs care.** Shrinkage and non-centering recur in statistics, sampling diagnostics, GLMs and BNNs. Units: ch03-statistics-p2, ch03-statistics-p3, ch12-markov-p2, ch15-generalized, ch17-bayesian.
- **Representing and evaluating uncertainty matters as much as point accuracy.** Topics are aleatoric versus epistemic uncertainty, posterior predictives, calibration, conformal sets, BNN posteriors and GP kernels. Units: ch03-statistics-p1, ch14-predictive, ch17-bayesian, ch18-gaussian-p1, ch18-gaussian-p2, ch19-beyond.
- **Causal and decision questions go beyond prediction.** Structural causal models and the do operator lead to identification and estimation (adjustment, IV, DiD, do-calculus). Bayesian decision theory covers bandits, MDPs and RL. Units: ch04-graphical-p3, ch34-decision, ch35-reinforcement, ch36-causality-p1, ch36-causality-p2, ch29-state-p3.

## How to read it
- **Use it as a reference.** The catalog recommends lookup, not cover-to-cover reading. Its focus chapters are Ch. 7 (ch07-inference), Ch. 10 (ch10-variational-p1, p2), Ch. 12 (ch12-markov-p1, p2) and Ch. 36 (ch36-causality-p1, p2).
- **Essential for the focus chapters:**
  - ch07-inference assumes Bayes' rule, KL divergence and basic exponential families (ch02-probability-p1–p2, ch03-statistics-p1).
  - ch10 needs KL and entropy (ch05-information-p1) and the reparameterization and score-function estimators (ch06-optimization-p1). Part 2 builds on part 1.
  - ch12-markov-p1 needs stationary distributions, detailed balance and ergodicity (ch02-probability-p3). ch11-monte supplies the sampling basics, and ch12-markov-p2 depends on ch12-markov-p1. The funnel and non-centered parameterization link back to ch03-statistics-p2.
  - ch36-causality-p1 builds on structural causal models and do-notation (ch04-graphical-p3) and d-separation (ch04-graphical-p1). ch36-causality-p2 depends on ch36-causality-p1.
- **Dependencies elsewhere:**
  - ch08 builds on the Gaussian results in ch02-probability-p2. ch08-gaussian-p2 needs ch08-gaussian-p1.
  - ch13 needs ch11 and ch08.
  - ch29 uses ch09 (forwards-backwards) and ch08 (Kalman). Its parts follow in order.
  - The deep generative models (ch21, ch23, ch24, ch25, ch26) draw on ch10, ch05 and ch20.
  - ch17 and ch18 draw on ch07, ch10 and ch12.
- **Skippable on a first pass:**
  - ch06-optimization-p3 (optimal transport, submodularity) and most of ch06-optimization-p2 apart from EM.
  - ch30-graph and ch32-representation, which are mainly maps of the topic. ch30 defers its methods to online supplementary material, and ch32 covers Ch. 31 only as a one-page introduction.
  - ch33-interpretability.
  - The extensions in ch22-autoregressive, ch28-latent-p2 and ch29-state-p2–p3, unless you need them.
- **Not covered:** there are no digest units for Ch. 27 as its own chapter (its pages are folded into ch28-latent-p1) or for Ch. 31 beyond the introduction.
