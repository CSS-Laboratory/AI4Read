# Cover, T. M., & Thomas, J. A. (1991). *Elements of Information Theory* (1st ed.). New York: Wiley.

*Edition used in AI4Read: the library copy above. The catalog lists 2nd ed., Wiley-Interscience, 2006; page numbers follow the library copy.*

## In one paragraph
The book builds information theory from a few quantities: entropy, relative entropy and mutual information. Entropy sets the limit for compressing data, and channel capacity sets the limit for sending data reliably. Typical sets (the AEP) are the main proof tool. The same quantities then answer questions in other fields. In gambling and portfolio theory they give the growth rate of wealth. In statistics they give the exponents of hypothesis tests and large deviations. Maximum entropy gives a principle for choosing distributions, and Kolmogorov complexity describes single objects. Later chapters extend the theory to continuous variables, Gaussian channels, rate distortion and networks. They end with a web of inequalities. It matters because it supplies the precise definitions and bounds that you cite in papers.

## The path through the work
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch01-introduction]] — Information theory answers two questions, "how far can data be compressed?" (entropy $H$) and "how fast can data be sent reliably?" (capacity $C$), and its quantities $H, I, C, D, K, W$ recur across physics, computer science, statistics, probability and economics.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch02-entropy]] — Entropy measures the uncertainty of a random variable; mutual information extends this notion and is a special case of relative entropy, and Jensen's inequality underlies many of their basic properties (such as non-negativity); together with the chain rules it gives the data processing inequality.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch03-asymptotic]] — For i.i.d. data, the per-symbol surprisal of a long observed sequence converges in probability to the entropy H, so almost all probability sits on about 2^{nH} sequences of probability about 2^{-nH}.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch04-entropy]] — For a stationary process, the entropy per symbol, $\frac1n H(X_1,\dots,X_n)$, and the conditional entropy of the next symbol given the whole past have the same limit, called the entropy rate; it is easy to compute for Markov chains and can be bracketed for functions of Markov chains.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p1]] — The expected length of any prefix (or uniquely decodable) code is at least the entropy $H_D(X)$, the best code is less than 1 symbol above it, and Huffman's algorithm finds that best code.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p2]] — Codeword lengths near $\log 1/p(x)$ cost within a small constant of the entropy $H(X)$, whether we compress a source (Shannon–Fano–Elias, arithmetic coding) or run the problem backwards and generate a random variable from fair coin flips.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch06-gambling]] — In a horse race with independent outcomes, if all wealth is bet each race and there is no cash option, betting in proportion to the win probabilities gives the best exponential growth of wealth, and that growth rate is tied to entropy and mutual information, and (through arithmetic coding) to data compression.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch07-kolmogorov]] — The complexity of a single string is the length of its shortest program on a universal computer. It is machine-independent up to a constant, it matches entropy on average, and it is within an additive constant of −log of the universal probability, but it cannot be computed.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p1]] — The capacity $C=\max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$ of a discrete memoryless channel is the highest rate at which messages can be sent with error probability going to zero, and the proof of achievability uses random codes with joint-typicality decoding.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p2]] — Capacity $C=\max_{p(x)}I(X;Y)$ is a sharp rate threshold: above it the error probability stays bounded away from 0 (even with feedback), and a source can be sent reliably if its entropy rate is below $C$ and not if it is above $C$.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch09-differential]] — Differential entropy $h(X)=-\int f\log f$ extends entropy to continuous variables: it is the log of the equivalent side length of the smallest set holding most of the probability, so it can be negative and changes under scaling, while relative entropy and mutual information keep their discrete properties.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch10-gaussian]] — With additive Gaussian noise of variance $N$ and input power limit $P$, the capacity is $\tfrac12\log(1+P/N)$ bits per use, and the same method gives band-limited, parallel (water-filling), coloured-noise and feedback results.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch11-maximum]] — Under moment constraints the entropy-maximizing density, whenever the multipliers can be chosen to meet the constraints, has the exponential form $e^{\lambda_0+\sum\lambda_i r_i(x)}$, and under autocorrelation constraints up to lag $p$ the maximum-entropy-rate process is the $p$th-order Gauss–Markov process (Burg).
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p1]] — Grouping sequences by their empirical distribution ("type") gives sharp exponential estimates of class sizes and class probabilities (and an exact formula for a single sequence) in terms of entropy $H(P)$ and relative entropy $D(P\|Q)$, which yield the law of large numbers, universal codes and Sanov's large-deviation theorem.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p2]] — Conditioned on a rare event, i.i.d. samples look like draws from the distribution P* in the event set that is closest to the source in relative entropy, and the best error exponents in hypothesis testing are relative entropies to that kind of closest point.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p3]] — Lempel–Ziv parsing compresses any stationary ergodic source, asymptotically and with probability 1, to a length per symbol of at most its entropy rate, without knowing the source, and Fisher information gives the lower bound $1/J(\theta)$ on the variance of any unbiased estimator.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch13-rate]] — The minimum rate for describing an i.i.d. source within expected distortion $D$ equals $R(D)=\min I(X;\hat X)$ over conditional distributions $p(\hat x|x)$ meeting the distortion constraint, and it is approached by coding long blocks jointly.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p1]] — When many senders and receivers share a noisy channel, interference, cooperation and feedback arise; Gaussian examples give concrete capacity answers, and an extended joint AEP (a result on which long sequences of several variables are typical together) is the tool for the later proofs.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p2]] — Two independent senders sharing one receiver can reach every rate pair inside a region bounded by conditional mutual informations, and two separately encoded correlated sources need a total rate of only H(X,Y), the same as joint encoding.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p3]] — The network problems treated in this part are handled by combining random coding, binning and typicality: Slepian–Wolf mirrors the multiple access channel, the degraded broadcast channel needs superposition coding with an auxiliary "cloud" variable $U$, and side-information source coding needs a description $U$ of $Y$ with $R_1\ge H(X|U)$, $R_2\ge I(Y;U)$.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p4]] — When the decoder also sees correlated side information Y, describing X within distortion D costs $R_Y(D)=\min\,[I(X;W)-I(Y;W)]$ bits (Wyner–Ziv), and in any multiterminal network the information flow across every cut is bounded by a conditional mutual information, though this bound is not always achievable.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch15-information]] — Maximizing the expected log of daily wealth (the doubling rate) gives a portfolio that is optimal for long-run growth, one-day expected ratio and competitive play, and its mathematics mirrors entropy, the AEP and mutual information.
- [[cover-2006-elements/ch16-inequalities]] — Convexity and non-negative relative entropy generate a web of inequalities for entropy, Fisher information, volumes and determinants. Because Gaussian entropy is a log-determinant, information inequalities become matrix inequalities.

## Big ideas across units
- **Entropy as the price of description.** Entropy is the average number of bits needed to describe a variable. It bounds prefix codes, is approached by Shannon, Huffman, arithmetic and Lempel–Ziv codes, and is the cost of generating a variable from fair coins. The same idea, entropy per symbol, extends to stationary processes as the entropy rate. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch01-introduction]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch03-asymptotic]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch04-entropy]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch06-gambling]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p3]].
- **Typicality as the proof engine.** A long sequence lies in a set of about $2^{nH}$ equally likely sequences. This idea is refined into joint typicality, the typical sets of densities, distortion-typical sets, types and the extended joint AEP. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch03-asymptotic]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch09-differential]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch13-rate]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch15-information]].
- **Relative entropy as the cost of being wrong, and as an exponent.** $D(p\|q)$ is the extra code length for a wrong distribution, the loss in doubling rate for wrong beliefs, the exponent of a type class's probability, and the exponent of hypothesis-testing errors (Stein, Chernoff). Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch02-entropy]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch06-gambling]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch15-information]].
- **Capacity as a sharp threshold, from one channel to networks.** The capacity $\max I(X;Y)$ is achieved by random codes. Above it, error is bounded away from zero. The same method gives the Gaussian channel and water-filling. For networks the answer becomes a region of rates, source–channel separation can fail, and feedback can enlarge regions. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch01-introduction]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch10-gaussian]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p3]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p4]].
- **Fano and data processing as the converse toolkit.** Converses are proved by chaining mutual information with the data processing inequality and Fano's inequality. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch02-entropy]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch10-gaussian]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch13-rate]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p3]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p4]].
- **Maximum entropy, the Gaussian, and the geometry of distributions.** The normal maximizes differential entropy for a given covariance. Maximum entropy under moment constraints gives exponential-form densities, and Burg's theorem gives the AR($p$) process. The closest-distribution (tilting) form appears in Sanov's theorem, the Pythagorean inequality and the conditional limit theorem. Gaussian entropy is a log-determinant, which links to matrix inequalities. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch09-differential]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch10-gaussian]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch11-maximum]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch16-inequalities]].
- **Betting and portfolios as a dual of entropy.** The doubling rate plays the role of entropy in gambling and investment. Side information adds at most $I(X;Y)$. Gambling schemes act as compressors, and the stock-market theory has its own chain rule and AEP. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch01-introduction]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch06-gambling]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch07-kolmogorov]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch15-information]].
- **Describing single objects and universal methods.** Kolmogorov complexity drops the probability distribution, and its expected value matches entropy. Universal coding (types-based codes, Lempel–Ziv) compresses without knowing the source. Units: [[cover-2006-elements/ch01-introduction]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p2]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch07-kolmogorov]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p3]].

## How to read it
The unit digests do not mark any unit as optional. The advice below comes only from the stated prerequisites and the dependencies between units.
- **Essential core.** Read [[cover-2006-elements/ch01-introduction]] (a map of the book), then [[cover-2006-elements/ch02-entropy]] and [[cover-2006-elements/ch03-asymptotic]]. Almost every later unit relies on entropy, mutual information, the data processing and Fano inequalities, and typical sets. After that comes compression, [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p1]] and [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p2]], and channel capacity, [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p1]] then [[cover-2006-elements/ch08-channel-p2]]. [[cover-2006-elements/ch04-entropy]] (entropy rate) is a prerequisite for the general-process results in [[cover-2006-elements/ch05-data-p1]].
- **Continuous and Gaussian branch.** [[cover-2006-elements/ch09-differential]] is needed for [[cover-2006-elements/ch10-gaussian]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch11-maximum]] and [[cover-2006-elements/ch13-rate]], and for the Gaussian parts of [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p1]] and [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p2]]. [[cover-2006-elements/ch10-gaussian]] in turn is assumed by the Gaussian network examples.
- **Self-contained side branches.** [[cover-2006-elements/ch07-kolmogorov]] needs only entropy, typical sets and the Kraft inequality, so it can be read whenever you want it, and nothing later depends on it. [[cover-2006-elements/ch06-gambling]] and [[cover-2006-elements/ch15-information]] form the investment strand. The first needs only entropy and mutual information, and the second builds on the same ideas. [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p1]], [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p2]] and [[cover-2006-elements/ch12-information-p3]] form the statistics strand. The second part requires the first (types and Sanov), and the Lempel–Ziv and Fisher information part is largely independent of those two.
- **Specialist and later material.** Network information theory ([[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p1]] to [[cover-2006-elements/ch14-network-p4]]) is cumulative. Part 1 supplies the extended joint AEP, part 2 the multiple access channel and Slepian–Wolf, and parts 3 and 4 depend on part 2 (part 4 also on the rate-distortion ideas of [[cover-2006-elements/ch13-rate]]). The author says there is no complete theory, so this is the most skippable block on a first pass. [[cover-2006-elements/ch16-inequalities]] collects and extends inequalities used earlier and is best read last. The long proofs inside [[cover-2006-elements/ch15-information]] (such as the Shannon–McMillan–Breiman sandwich argument) can be skipped at first.
- **Numbering caution.** The catalog's focus list uses different chapter numbers from the unit titles (for example, it lists channel capacity as Ch. 7). Follow the unit titles and ids on this page.
