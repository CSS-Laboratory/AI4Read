# Wittgenstein, L. (1953/2009). *Philosophical Investigations* (trans. G. E. M. Anscombe, P. M. S. Hacker & J. Schulte; rev. 4th ed., ed. Hacker & Schulte). Chichester: Wiley-Blackwell. (German–English facing text.)

## In one paragraph
Wittgenstein rejects the picture that every word is a name of an object. He asks us to look at how words are used in "language-games", activities in which language and action are woven together. Concepts such as "game" are held together by overlapping similarities (family resemblance), not by a hidden essence. Following a rule is a trained, shared practice. No inner interpretation or private mental process fixes it, and justifications end in "this is simply what I do". He applies the same method to mental words such as sensation, thinking, meaning, expecting, believing and seeing-as. Instead of asking what inner object they name, he asks how they are used, in what circumstances and with what criteria. Philosophy, on this view, describes use and does not explain or reform it. The work is a foundation for usage-based views of meaning.

## The path through the work
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch01-augustine]] — Wittgenstein challenges the picture on which every word is a name of an object, as a picture of all language, and asks us to look at how words are actually used in simple "language-games" (a language-game is a language together with the activities it is woven into).
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch02-family]] — Wittgenstein says that "game", "number" and "language" have no single common essence, only overlapping similarities. He concludes that philosophy should describe everyday use, not hunt for hidden essences.
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch03-understanding]] — Wittgenstein suggests that understanding a rule is not a mental process behind correct use and is not fixed by interpretation; it shows itself in a trained, customary practice of applying the rule, where justifications end in "this is simply what I do".
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch04-private]] — Wittgenstein tries to show that sensation-words do not work by naming private objects that only the speaker can know: the diary case leaves no criterion of correct use, and such a private object drops out of how the word is used.
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch05-thinking]] — Wittgenstein argues that we do not find out what words such as "think", "understand", "imagine", "I" and "conscious" mean by inspecting an inner process or object, but by looking at how the words are used (their "grammar").
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch06-intentionality]] — Thought, wish, expectation, order and understanding can seem to reach what is not there through a special mental process, and in these remarks Wittgenstein keeps turning to how the words and signs are used.
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch07-negation]] — For negating, expecting, intending, willing and "meaning someone", Wittgenstein says we should not look for an inner process that gives words life, but at the surrounding circumstances, the criteria, and the language-game.
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch08-ppf]] — In these remarks Wittgenstein asks whether psychological words such as hope, meaning something, believing and being afraid get their sense from their use in human practices and contexts, rather than from inner objects, inner pictures or inner observation.
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch09-ppf]] — Seeing an aspect (seeing a figure that has not changed "as" something) seems to lie between seeing and thinking, and Wittgenstein investigates it as a question about the concept, looking at its use and at the behaviour and abilities that go with it, rather than at an inner picture or a physiological cause.
- [[wittgenstein-1953-investigations/ch10-ppf]] — Wittgenstein asks how meaning, intending, inner speech, knowing one's own mind and certainty should be understood, and looks to use, behaviour, consequences and language-games rather than to hidden inner processes accompanying words and actions.

## Big ideas across units
- **Meaning as use in language-games, against the naming picture.** The work begins by testing the idea that words name objects against simple language-games. It then carries "look at the use" through every later topic. Units: ch01, ch02, ch06, ch07, ch08, ch10.
- **No hidden essences.** Family resemblance, blurred but usable concepts, goal-relative exactness and the doubt about "simples" all turn down the demand for one common essence. Reading, too, has no single common feature. Units: ch01, ch02, ch03.
- **Rule-following as a public practice.** Rules can leave room for doubt, and interpretation by itself fixes nothing. Understanding shows in training and custom, justifications give out, and the diary case shows that "correct" has no application without a criterion. Units: ch02, ch03, ch04, ch06.
- **Rejection of the inner process or inner object.** Understanding, sensation, thinking, imagining, meaning, intending, expecting and experiencing a meaning are not found by inspecting something inside. The private object "drops out", like the beetle in the box. Units: ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08, ch10.
- **Philosophy as description of grammar.** Philosophy gives a surveyable view of use and "leaves everything as it is". Many "facts" about the mind turn out to be grammatical remarks, and nonsense is a boundary drawn in grammar. Units: ch02, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07.
- **Form of life and agreement.** Speaking belongs to a form of life, and communication needs agreement in judgements, not in opinions. Forms of life are "the given". Hope belongs to a complicated form of life. Units: ch01, ch03, ch08, ch10.
- **Criteria, circumstances and context for mental words.** What we go by in applying a word, together with the setting, replaces the search for an inner feeling. Units: ch04, ch05, ch07, ch08, ch09.
- **Pictures mislead, and conceptual questions differ from causal ones.** Pictures that force themselves on us (the inner picture, the visual room, the "undertone" of words) mislead. A picture before the mind does not fix a use. The problem of seeing aspects, and the confusion in psychology, are conceptual, not causal or experimental. Units: ch02, ch05, ch07, ch09, ch10.

## How to read it
- **Essential first.** ch01 to ch04 match the catalog focus (Augustine's picture, language-games, family resemblance, rule-following and private language). ch01 sets the language-game idea that every later unit's prerequisites assume.
- **Dependencies stated in the material.**
  - ch02 and ch03 assume the language-game idea from ch01.
  - ch03 also assumes the cause/reason contrast.
  - ch07 and ch10 assume language-game and meaning as use.
  - ch08 assumes the language-game idea.
  - ch09 assumes the difference between causal and conceptual questions and the habit of looking at how a word is used.
  - ch04's private-language material connects to the rule-following results in ch03 (both units link [[rule-following problem]]).
- **Can wait on a first pass.** ch05 to ch10 apply the method to mental concepts. The material states no strict order among them, but ch08 to ch10 (the Part II "Philosophy of Psychology – A Fragment") are the most specialised. ch09 and ch10 continue each other on aspect-seeing and aspect-blindness. ch06 and ch07 pair as remarks on intentionality, expecting, meaning and willing.
- **Note.** The page ranges of ch04, ch05 and ch06 are partial (the digests say the supplied pages end or begin mid-section), so some sections are not covered.
