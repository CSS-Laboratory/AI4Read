# Quine, W. V. O. (1951). Main Trends in Recent Philosophy: Two Dogmas of Empiricism. *The Philosophical Review*, 60(1), 20–43. doi:10.2307/2181906. Reprinted in Quine, *From a Logical Point of View* (Harvard UP, 1953).

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[quine-1951-dogmas/main]].

## In one line
Quine argues that the analytic/synthetic distinction and the reductionist idea that single statements have their own experiential content are ill-founded dogmas, and proposes that only science as a whole faces experience.

## The gist
Quine examines two assumptions of modern empiricism. The first is a sharp line between analytic truths (true by meaning) and synthetic truths (true by fact). The second is reductionism: each meaningful statement is equivalent to one about immediate experience. He argues that each attempt he examines to explain analyticity (through meaning, definition, synonymy, interchangeability or semantical rules) leans on notions no clearer than analyticity itself. Radical reductionism has long left Carnap's philosophy, but a subtler form survives in the belief that single statements can be confirmed on their own. His alternative: statements face experience together, as one system, and any statement can be revised. The supposed boundary between metaphysics and natural science blurs, and pragmatism is carried further.

**Prerequisites:** the idea of a logically true sentence; truth-functional connectives ("if and only if"); the difference between a name and what it names; basic idea of a scientific hypothesis and of testing it against observation.
