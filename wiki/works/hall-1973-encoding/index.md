# Hall, S. (1973). *Encoding and Decoding in the Television Discourse*. CCCS Stencilled Occasional Paper No. 7. Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies, University of Birmingham. (Revised as "Encoding/decoding" in Hall et al. (Eds.), *Culture, Media, Language*, Hutchinson, 1980, pp. 128–137.)

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[hall-1973-encoding/main]].

## In one line
Television messages are encoded into a meaningful discourse and then decoded; the two meanings need not match, and Hall argues that in societies like ours communication between broadcasting elites and audiences is necessarily "systematically distorted", so that beyond the denotative level "improving communications" is extremely difficult to call a neutral goal, and making the dominant elites' hegemonic codes more effective is a political, not a technical, choice.

## The gist
Hall asks how formal study of "televisual language" can be joined to societal structure and cultural policy. He treats the message as a coded discourse sitting between two moments, encoding and decoding. These are linked to production and reception structures, but the codes of the two sides may not be symmetrical. Using the TV Western, he argues that meaning, including the meaning of violence, depends on codes and context, not on counting content. Connotative meanings are structured in dominance, not closed. Hall offers four decoding positions: dominant, professional, negotiated, oppositional. Beyond the literal level, cultural policies for "better communication" are hard to call neutral; Hall says misreading the problem as technical is unconscious collusion with dominant interests.

**Prerequisites:** sign, signifier and signified; denotation (literal meaning) vs connotation (associated, ideological meaning); polysemy (one sign, more than one possible meaning); the idea of ideology; the effects-research tradition (violence, "selective perception"). Below, "message-form" means the coded, symbolic shape a message takes, and a "determinate moment" is a stage that cannot be skipped or ignored.
