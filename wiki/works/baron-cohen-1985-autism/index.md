# Baron-Cohen, S., Leslie, A. M., & Frith, U. (1985). Does the autistic child have a "theory of mind"? *Cognition*, 21(1), 37–46. doi:10.1016/0010-0277(85)90022-8

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[baron-cohen-1985-autism/main]].

## In one line
In a Sally–Anne false-belief test, most of 20 relatively able autistic children failed (they pointed to where the marble really was), while almost all Down's syndrome and normal preschool children passed; the authors take this as a deficit in representing mental states, specific to autism.

## The gist
The authors ask why autistic children have such severe social difficulties, which mental retardation alone does not explain. Using Leslie's model of metarepresentation (the ability to represent representations), they predict that autistic children lack a "theory of mind": they cannot attribute beliefs to others. They test this with the Sally–Anne puppet task in three groups: autistic (higher mental age), Down's syndrome and normal preschoolers. Sixteen of 20 autistic children failed the Belief Question, while about 85% of each control group passed. All groups passed the control questions about reality and memory. The authors conclude that the deficit is independent of mental retardation and specific to autism.

**Prerequisites:** the idea of a false belief (believing something that is not true); mental age vs chronological age; basic percentages and the idea of a control group.
