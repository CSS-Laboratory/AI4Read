# Strang, G. (2023). *Introduction to Linear Algebra* (6th ed.). Wellesley-Cambridge Press. ISBN 978-1-7331466-7-8.

## In one paragraph
This textbook treats linear algebra as the art of factoring a matrix into simpler pieces. It starts with a matrix as a combination of its columns and the factorization A = CR. It then moves through elimination (A = LU), the four fundamental subspaces, and orthogonality with least squares (A = QR). Next come eigenvalues (A = XΛX⁻¹ and S = QΛQᵀ) and the SVD (A = UΣVᵀ). Later chapters show that a matrix is a linear transformation written in chosen bases. They also apply the same tools to optimization (gradient descent, Lagrange multipliers, duality) and to learning from data (ReLU networks, covariance). The result is a path from basic vector ideas to the mathematics behind data science and machine learning.

## The path through the work
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch01-vectors]] — A matrix times a vector is a combination of the matrix's columns; the independent columns give the rank and the factorization A = CR.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch02-solving-p1]] — Elimination turns Ax = b into a triangular system Ux = c that back substitution solves, and recording the steps gives A = LU, with the multipliers sitting in L.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch02-solving-p2]] — Row exchanges (permutations) rescue elimination as PA = LU, the transpose is the matrix that moves between inner products, and second-difference matrices K, T, B show how boundary conditions decide invertibility.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p1]] — A subspace is a set closed under linear combinations; elimination to the reduced echelon form R turns Ax = 0 into an easy problem whose n − r special solutions form a basis for the nullspace; the same elimination also gives A = CR.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p2]] — Every solution of Ax = b is one particular solution plus any nullspace vector, and the size of a subspace is the number of vectors in any of its bases.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p3]] — For an m by n matrix of rank r, the row and column spaces have dimension r and the two nullspaces have dimensions n − r and m − r; elimination from A to R₀ preserves all four dimensions.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch04-orthogonality-p1]] — When Ax = b has no solution, the best substitute is the projection p = Ax̂ of b onto the column space, and x̂ solves the normal equation AᵀAx̂ = Aᵀb because the error b − Ax̂ is orthogonal to that column space.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch04-orthogonality-p2]] — Orthonormal columns make projection and least squares trivial (x̂ = Qᵀb), Gram-Schmidt builds such columns and gives A = QR, and the pseudoinverse A⁺ extends "inverse" to every matrix.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch05-determinants]] — The determinant is one number per square matrix that can be computed from pivots, cofactors, or n! signed terms; it signals invertibility and equals the volume scaling of the map (as |det|).
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p1]] — An eigenvector is a special direction that a square matrix only stretches, shrinks or flips; its eigenvalues are the roots of det(A − λI) = 0, and they make powers of A easy.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p2]] — Eigenvectors turn a matrix into a diagonal one (A = XΛX⁻¹), which makes powers and recurrences easy, and real symmetric matrices always allow this with orthonormal eigenvectors (S = QΛQᵀ); positive definite symmetric matrices have five equivalent tests.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p3]] — Allowing complex numbers makes Hermitian, unitary and circulant matrices fit eigenvalue theory (the Fourier matrix diagonalizes all circulants), and eigenvalues then solve du/dt = Au through e^{λt}x, with the real parts of λ deciding stability.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch07-singular]] — Every matrix factors as (orthogonal) × (diagonal) × (orthogonal), A = UΣVᵀ, and cutting that sum of rank-one pieces after k terms gives the closest rank-k matrix, which is used in image compression and in principal component analysis (PCA).
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch08-linear]] — A linear transformation is fully determined by what it does to a basis, so each choice of input and output bases turns it into a matrix, and a good choice of bases (eigenvectors, singular vectors, and so on) makes that matrix simple.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch09-linear]] — At a smooth minimum the gradient is zero. This gives a linear system for quadratics, gradient-type iterations for other functions, and Lagrange multipliers or duality once constraints appear.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/ch10-learning]] — A learning function built by composing layers ReLU(Av+b) is continuous piecewise linear, its flat pieces can be counted exactly for one hidden layer in general position (and estimated for more), and covariance matrices (positive semidefinite, transforming as V_Z = A V_X Aᵀ) tie probability to linear algebra.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/app1-ranks]] — Multiplying matrices cannot raise rank, and matrices can be factored in many ways (CR, LU, QR, SVD, Jordan and others, each with its own requirements) that help explain rank, parameter counts, conditioning and eigenvalue behaviour.
- [[strang-2023-introduction/app10-computer]] — Adding a fourth coordinate equal to 1 turns shifts, which are not linear in R³, into 4 by 4 matrix multiplication, so translation, scaling, rotation and projection can all be chained as matrices.

## Big ideas across units
- **Everything is a factorization.** A = CR, then LU and PA = LU, then QR, then XΛX⁻¹ and QΛQᵀ, then UΣVᵀ. The appendices list 16 factorizations with their requirements and count their parameters. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch01-vectors]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch02-solving-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch02-solving-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch04-orthogonality-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch07-singular]], [[strang-2023-introduction/app1-ranks]].
- **Columns, rank and the four fundamental subspaces.** The column space and independent columns lead to rank, nullspace bases, dimension counts and orthogonal complements. The SVD later gives orthonormal bases for these spaces. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch01-vectors]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p3]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch04-orthogonality-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch07-singular]], [[strang-2023-introduction/app1-ranks]].
- **Elimination as the practical engine.** It solves Ax = b, finds R₀ and null-space bases, gives determinants as a product of pivots, gives S = LDLᵀ, and drives block elimination for KKT systems. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch01-vectors]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch02-solving-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch03-four-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch05-determinants]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch09-linear]], [[strang-2023-introduction/app1-ranks]].
- **Orthogonality, projection and least squares.** The error is perpendicular to the column space. This yields normal equations, Q-based least squares, the pseudoinverse (minimum-norm solutions), PCA as perpendicular least squares, and the minimum-norm link to double descent. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch04-orthogonality-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch04-orthogonality-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch07-singular]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch10-learning]].
- **Eigenvectors make a matrix simple.** They give powers, recurrences, differential equations (e^{λt}), and the spectral theorem. The idea reappears as the choice of a good basis for a linear transformation, and as eigenvalues of AᵀA in the SVD. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p1]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p3]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch07-singular]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch08-linear]].
- **Symmetric and positive definite matrices.** The symmetric structures AᵀA and LDLᵀ lead to second-difference matrices, the five positive definite tests, Cholesky factorization, convex minimization, and covariance matrices. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch02-solving-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch09-linear]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch10-learning]].
- **Matrices as transformations that compose.** Matrix multiplication is composition, and changing bases explains diagonalization. Affine maps and graphics chains use the same rule. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch01-vectors]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch08-linear]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch10-learning]], [[strang-2023-introduction/app10-computer]].
- **From linear algebra to optimization and learning.** Gradient descent, backpropagation, Lagrange multipliers and duality lead to ReLU networks, SGD, double descent and probability. SVD/PCA, positive definiteness and the condition number supply the tools. Units: [[strang-2023-introduction/ch06-eigenvalues-p2]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch07-singular]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch09-linear]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch10-learning]].

  - Ch. 6 parts 1 → 2 → 3 run in order, and part 1 needs determinants.
  - Ch. 7 needs eigenvalues of symmetric matrices, orthogonal matrices and the four subspaces.
  - Ch. 9 needs symmetric positive definite matrices (6.3) and block elimination.
  - Ch. 10 uses SGD and backpropagation from Ch. 9, and positive semidefinite matrices.
- **Can be skipped or postponed on a first pass (outside the focus list):**

  - [[strang-2023-introduction/ch08-linear]], [[strang-2023-introduction/ch10-learning]], and the appendices [[strang-2023-introduction/app1-ranks]] and [[strang-2023-introduction/app10-computer]], which are reference or application material.
- **Appendices as reference:** [[strang-2023-introduction/app1-ranks]] collects rank rules, the factorization list and the condition number, and is useful alongside Ch. 3, 6 and 7. [[strang-2023-introduction/app10-computer]] only needs matrix products, orthogonal matrices and projection.
