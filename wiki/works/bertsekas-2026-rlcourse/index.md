# Bertsekas, D. P. (2026). *A Course in Reinforcement Learning* (2nd ed.). Athena Scientific, Belmont, MA. ISBN 978-1-886529-29-8.

## In one paragraph
Exact dynamic programming (DP) finds optimal decisions by working backwards from the end of a problem, but it is usually too expensive. The book's main idea is approximation in value space. You replace the exact cost-to-go with a cheap approximation and then look ahead one or more steps, only at the states you actually visit. Rollout is the central example: the approximation is the cost of a base heuristic or policy, and under stated conditions the result is no worse than that base. The author reads this as a Newton step on Bellman's equation. That reading explains why lookahead adds so much to an off-line trained policy, and it links RL to model predictive control (MPC), adaptive control and AlphaZero-style systems. Later chapters cover constraints, stochastic and minimax problems, multiagent control, and training values and policies from data.

## The path through the work
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch01-exact-p1]] — Exact dynamic programming computes optimal cost-to-go functions backwards, and when that is too expensive, approximation in value space (e.g. rollout) replaces them with cheap approximations used in a forward, one-state-at-a-time lookahead.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch01-exact-p2]] — The DP recursion carries over to random disturbances and infinite horizons, and because exact solutions are rare, one replaces the optimal cost-to-go by an approximation (lookahead, rollout) or replaces the optimal policy by a trained parametric one.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch01-exact-p3]] — In the one-dimensional linear quadratic problem, one-step lookahead with a quadratic terminal cost approximation is one Newton step for the Riccati equation, and further lookahead steps are value iterations that only improve the Newton starting point.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch01-exact-p4]] — There is no unique way to choose state and control variables, and many practical problems (delays, forecasts, partial information, many agents) can be recast into the standard DP form, and the recast form can often be simplified or approximated.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch01-exact-p5]] — When parameters change or constraints bind, re-solve a short lookahead problem on-line (rollout, MPC); this is a Newton step on Bellman's equation, and it can be shown to be stable when the base policy is stable or the terminal cost is suitably chosen.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch01-exact-p6]] — The book's principal subjects are approximation in value space, rollout and policy iteration, which the author relates to Newton's method on the Bellman equation. The notes give sources for these strands, and the exercises let the reader test them.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p1]] — In a deterministic finite-horizon problem, a rollout algorithm replaces the unknown optimal cost-to-go by the cost of a cheap base heuristic, and it is guaranteed to do no worse than that heuristic when the heuristic is sequentially consistent or sequentially improving.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p2]] — Rollout (one-step lookahead whose tail cost comes from a base heuristic) can be adapted to many settings, with a cost-improvement guarantee kept where the sequential improvement condition, or fortification, holds.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p3]] — Rollout (one-step lookahead using a base policy or heuristic to evaluate the leaves) is guaranteed to do at least as well as its base policy under a sequential-improvement condition, and it can be extended cheaply to long lookahead and to trajectory constraints.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p4]] — Rollout (one-step lookahead that scores each control by the cost of a base heuristic or policy) can be extended to constraints, tiny stage costs, continuous time and stochastic problems, keeping the "no worse than the base" guarantee under stated conditions.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p5]] — Rollout can be made cheaper: by selective sampling (MCTS, with a hopefully small risk of worse performance), by optimization solvers for continuous controls (the certainty-equivalent variant loses cost improvement), and by choosing the components of a multi-component control one agent at a time, which keeps the cost-improvement guarantee at cost of order $nm$ instead of $n^m$.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p6]] — Rollout (replace the unknown optimal tail cost by the simulated cost of a base policy) carries over to belief-state problems and to worst-case (minimax) problems once the state, the expectation or the cost operator is redefined accordingly.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch02-approximation-p7]] — Rollout and one-step lookahead extend to minimax (adversarial) control by computing a maximization per Q-factor, and replacing the maximizer by a fixed nominal opponent policy gives a cheaper deterministic one-player problem that keeps the Newton-step picture.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch03-learning-p1]] — Off-line, a cost-to-go or policy is fitted from sampled data by a parametric architecture, often a linear weighting of features, trained by least squares or, for nonlinear architectures, by incremental gradient steps.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch03-learning-p2]] — Cost-to-go functions, Q-factors and cost differences can be approximated by least-squares fits of a (neural network) architecture to Bellman-equation targets, solved stage by stage or policy by policy.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch03-learning-p3]] — A policy can be trained off-line from state-control samples by regression or classification, but on-line lookahead is still needed on top of it, because a trained policy may be unfit for replanning and may lack the extra power of the exact Newton step that lookahead supplies.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch03-learning-p4]] — Instead of fitting a fixed policy, one can parametrize policies by a vector $r$ and minimize the expected cost over $r$ directly, using sampling-based gradient, scaled-gradient, random-direction or random-search methods; the last two need only simulated cost values, and the likelihood-ratio gradient additionally needs $\nabla\log p$.
- [[bertsekas-2026-rlcourse/ch03-learning-p5]] — Aggregation builds a much smaller DP problem on "aggregate states", solves it exactly, and interpolates its optimal costs into a cost approximation for the original problem, with an error that is small when $J^*$ varies little inside each aggregate's footprint.

## Big ideas across units
- **Off-line training plus on-line lookahead (the AlphaZero architecture).** A cost or policy is trained off-line, and decisions are then made by on-line lookahead. The author says this split is conceptually very important. It returns in the argument that a trained policy alone lacks the Newton step and cannot replan. Units: ch01-exact-p1, ch01-exact-p2, ch01-exact-p6, ch03-learning-p3, ch03-learning-p4, ch03-learning-p5.
- **Lookahead as a Newton step on Bellman's equation.** One-step lookahead is a Newton step, longer lookahead improves the starting point, and rollout and policy iteration are repeated Newton steps. The author contrasts this with gradient descent. Units: ch01-exact-p2, ch01-exact-p3, ch01-exact-p5, ch01-exact-p6, ch02-approximation-p3, ch02-approximation-p4, ch02-approximation-p7, ch03-learning-p3.
- **Rollout and its cost-improvement guarantee.** The cost of a base heuristic or policy replaces the optimal tail cost. Sequential consistency or sequential improvement (or fortification) ensures the result is no worse than the base. The guarantee is extended to constraints, stochastic problems, continuous time, multiagent control and minimax. Units: ch01-exact-p1, ch01-exact-p6, ch02-approximation-p1, ch02-approximation-p2, ch02-approximation-p3, ch02-approximation-p4, ch02-approximation-p5, ch02-approximation-p7.
- **Making lookahead affordable.** Truncation, simplified and parallel variants, incremental multistep rollout, Monte Carlo tree search, certainty equivalence, common random numbers, and agent-by-agent multiagent rollout (cost $O(nm)$ rather than $O(n^m)$). Units: ch01-exact-p4, ch02-approximation-p2, ch02-approximation-p3, ch02-approximation-p4, ch02-approximation-p5, ch02-approximation-p6, ch02-approximation-p7.
- **Reformulating problems into DP form.** State augmentation, sufficient statistics and belief states turn delays, partial information, adaptive control and Bayesian optimization into DP problems. Units: ch01-exact-p4, ch01-exact-p5, ch02-approximation-p6, ch03-learning-p2.
- **Model predictive control, adaptive control and stability.** MPC is read as rollout, and stability comes from a stable base policy or a Lyapunov-type terminal cost. The region of stability grows with lookahead length. Units: ch01-exact-p2, ch01-exact-p3, ch01-exact-p5, ch01-exact-p6, ch02-approximation-p4.
- **Adversarial and worst-case control.** Replacing the expectation by a maximization keeps rollout valid, but Newton's method and policy iteration become less reliable. A nominal opponent policy gives a cheaper one-player problem (computer chess, leader-follower games). Units: ch02-approximation-p6, ch02-approximation-p7.
- **Learning values and policies from data.** Parametric architectures, neural networks, fitted value iteration, approximate policy iteration, advantages and cost differences, policy gradients, and aggregation. Units: ch02-approximation-p4, ch03-learning-p1, ch03-learning-p2, ch03-learning-p3, ch03-learning-p4, ch03-learning-p5.

## How to read it
- **Essential:** ch01-exact-p1 introduces the DP algorithm, approximation in value space and rollout, and everything else builds on it. Then read ch01-exact-p3 (the Newton-step view), ch01-exact-p6 (the summary of the book's core), ch02-approximation-p1 (rollout and its guarantee) and ch02-approximation-p3 (cheaper lookahead, constraints).
- **Dependencies in Chapter 1:** ch01-exact-p2 extends p1 to stochastic and infinite-horizon problems, and p3 needs its Bellman equation, value iteration and policy iteration. ch01-exact-p5 depends on p3 (Newton step) and p4 (belief states, state augmentation).
- **Dependencies in Chapter 2:** all of Chapter 2 builds on ch01-exact-p1. ch02-approximation-p2 and p3 need the sequential-improvement condition from ch02-approximation-p1. ch02-approximation-p4 and p5 build on p1 to p3. ch02-approximation-p6 uses multiagent rollout from p5 and belief states from ch01-exact-p4. ch02-approximation-p7 needs ch02-approximation-p6 and the Newton-step view from ch01-exact-p3.
- **Dependencies in Chapter 3:** it builds on the rollout and policy iteration ideas of the first two chapters. Within it, p1 comes first. Then p2 and p3 use rollout, policy iteration and Q-factors. ch03-learning-p4 needs the policy-training ideas in p3. ch03-learning-p5 needs only discounted DP and contraction mappings.
- **Can be skipped on a first pass:** the detailed variants and applications in ch02-approximation-p2, p4, p5 and p6 (Bayesian optimization, Wordle, MCTS, constrained and continuous-time forms). ch02-approximation-p7's chess and leader-follower material and the Notes and Exercises sections can also wait. The terminology comparison in ch01-exact-p5 can be skimmed. Chapter 3 (ch03-learning-p1 to p5) lies outside the catalog's suggested focus and can be left for later reading.
