# Onishi, K. H., & Baillargeon, R. (2005). Do 15-month-old infants understand false beliefs? *Science*, 308(5719), 255–258. doi:10.1126/science.1107621

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[onishi-2005-infant-false-belief/main]].

## In one line
In a nonverbal looking-time task, 15-month-olds looked longer when an actor searched where she did not believe the toy was, whether her belief was true or false; the authors read this as implicit understanding of belief.

## The gist
In the standard verbal false-belief task, most 3-year-olds fail and 4-year-olds typically pass. Some researchers see this as part of the evidence for a shift at about age 4 to a "representational" theory of mind (beliefs as representations that may be wrong). Others blame task demands. The authors ask whether 15-month-olds expect an actor to search according to her belief, true or false. Infants watched a toy hidden, then saw a change that the actor either watched or did not watch. In the test they looked longer when she reached into the box inconsistent with her belief. This held for true and false beliefs alike. Simple rules ("search where the toy is") fail to explain this. The authors prefer a belief-attribution reading and doubt a fundamental change at age 4.

**Prerequisites:** the idea of a false belief; the logic of a controlled experiment; the meaning of an interaction in a factorial design (ANOVA).
