# McCombs, M. E., & Shaw, D. L. (1972). The Agenda-Setting Function of Mass Media. *Public Opinion Quarterly*, 36(2), 176–187. DOI: 10.1086/267990

## One digest
This work has a single digest, which is also its work-level summary: [[mccombs-1972-agendasetting/main]].

## In one line
In a small 1968 Chapel Hill study, the issues voters not yet definitely decided called important matched the issues the news media stressed (r ≈ +.97), which the authors read as supporting, but not proving, a media agenda-setting function.

## The gist
The authors ask whether media set the "agenda" (which issues voters see as important), even if media change attitudes little. They interviewed 100 Chapel Hill voters not yet definitely decided in the 1968 presidential campaign. They content-analysed the nine news media these voters used. Media emphasis on issues correlated very strongly with voters' emphasis (+.967 for major, i.e. prominent, items; +.979 for minor). Voters' judgments seemed to follow all the news, not just news about a favoured candidate. This is better explained by agenda-setting than by selective perception (reading mainly what favours one's own side). The authors say this does not prove the hypothesis. It is a whole-group test; later work must go to the individual level.

**Prerequisites:** correlation coefficient; percentages and tables; the idea of content analysis (counting coded categories in media texts).
